    Curriculum Levels

    A curriculum level is an ordered learning stage for a defined educational context. Its goals must be reachable from the declared entry basis and earlier levels, allowing a suitable learning order within the level itself. Its estimated workload plus explicit reserve must fit its available learning budget under stated learning conditions.

    A school year is a familiar cross-subject example. A semester, a course stage, or a bounded continuing-education period can use the same concept.

    Concept status. This page defines the curriculum-level design and its quality claims. It does not announce an implemented level schema, validator, badge, or 3D interface. The optional formal profile is specified in Graph Definition §12. Existing graph validity, projection roles, learner state, and runtime modes retain their own contracts.

    Vocabulary and purpose

    Use curriculum level in English, curriculares Level in German, and CurriculumLevel when a technical name is needed. This is distinct from:

    • the four Levels of Personalization, which describe how curriculum scope and learner state are resolved;
    • goal difficulty metadata such as level / Anforderungsbereich;
    • curriculum quality maturity levels;
    • a learner's individual competence or a general experience-points score.

    Curriculum levels connect two responsibilities: prerequisite-consistent curriculum organisation and realistic workload planning. They also give learners recognisable milestones between an individual learning goal and a complete educational programme.

    A year label alone establishes neither responsibility. An official year placement is input to the check; the result may expose a missing preparation, an ordering conflict, or an unsupported workload estimate. Such a finding must remain visible rather than being presented as a validated level.

    Contextual stages over stable goals

    The level assignment belongs to a resolved educational context, for example a specified jurisdiction, school form, duration model, subject selection, course profiles, and curriculum release. A canonical goal keeps the same identity even when another context assigns its acquisition to a different level.

    Use the existing program and placement layers and reviewed composition views. A curriculum level is a planning contract over those structures, not another kind of assessable goal. Where a composition view is authoritative, derive the level's target membership from that view and validate any additional assignment against it; do not maintain a competing copy of the curriculum structure.

    Within one resolved learning path:

    • every target has one acquisition level;
    • later repetition, application, and assessment may reference the same goal;
    • first acquisition is counted once, even when several subjects need it;
    • the level's participating subjects and required target scope are explicit.

    A Mathematics-and-Physics level must be labelled with that scope. It cannot claim to cover the whole school year across subjects that were never included. Alternative pathways must be resolved explicitly; do not validate the workload of an arbitrarily selected light pathway as if it covered every permitted one.

    Condition 1: prerequisite-consistent and traversable

    For every target, all required competencies must belong to the declared entry basis, an earlier level, or the same level. A required competency assigned to a later level is an ordering conflict. A required competency with no assignment and no declared entry assumption is a planning gap.

    The check includes direct requirements, inherited requirements, longer prerequisite chains, the atomic contents of required clusters, and applicable cross-subject requirements. Resolve these before applying the level's display filter. An outside prerequisite must not disappear just because it is hidden from a learner-facing tree or absent from one subject's target list.

    The entry basis states what the reference plan assumes at its start. It does not certify that a particular learner already possesses those competencies. Declaring entry requirements or simulating a valid learning order never writes learner mastery.

    Requirements within the same level

    Dependencies within a level are allowed. The level must admit an order in which all its targets can actually be reached from the assumed entry state. The formal profile uses the existing global frontier to check this; it catches within-level deadlocks as well as dependencies on later levels.

    The following example is illustrative, not a claim about an official syllabus. Suppose a Physics target in level 7 requires a Mathematics target for rearranging a simple equation:

    Mathematics acquisition level Structural result Remaining planning task
    6 Prerequisite available from an earlier level Diagnose individual gaps when needed.
    7 Permitted if the level has a valid internal learning order Schedule Mathematics before the dependent Physics target.
    8 Ordering conflict Review the prerequisite's acquisition placement or the dependent target's stage.
    No assignment and no entry assumption Planning gap Resolve where the necessary preparation belongs.

    The level check establishes coarse ordering. A dated plan must additionally provide prerequisites before the dependent target's planned use. Two targets in the same school year can still be ordered incorrectly within that year.

    Repairs must preserve the meaning of the competencies and their justified requirements. Moving a preparation earlier or revising the dependent target's placement may resolve a conflict. A change to the official-curriculum mapping must remain traceable to its source and reviewed context. Where earlier preparation supplies the same canonical competence, its acquisition must be assigned there in that plan; a later encounter is repetition or application.

    Cross-subject support goals

    A Mathematics goal may be a normal target in the Mathematics view and prerequisiteOnly in the Physics view. A combined level resolves both roles against the same canonical identity. Its acquisition workload is counted once; additional Physics practice may consume separately planned time.

    A required goal outside the combined target set needs an explicit entry assumption or an authorised provision for learning it. Detecting that need does not automatically promote a projection role, widen the Personal Curriculum, or make a hidden goal selectable. The level review reports the gap for the appropriate curriculum or planning decision.

    Condition 2: workload fits a declared learning budget

    Workload feasibility is conditional on the reference learners, their assumed entry competencies, the learning conditions, and the available time. A calendar year by itself is not a learning budget.

    The curriculum time-axis contract owns calendar capacity, plan-local effort units, activity allocation, and reserves. A curriculum-level assessment uses that contract:

    Estimated learning work plus protected reserve must fit the available capacity, using the same documented unit and scope.

    The work estimate covers the specified acquisition, practice, repetition, assessment, and preparation activities. Their time is accounted for once. First acquisition of a shared goal is not duplicated; genuine additional activities may have additional effort.

    Goal counts and the existing weight field do not establish learning time. Plan-local units from different subjects are not comparable without a common calibration. Cross-subject budgeting therefore uses a shared time unit, preferably minutes, and a documented workload model.

    For a fixed timetable, check both the overall budget and the binding subject or resource budgets. Spare capacity in one subject does not automatically resolve an overloaded subject. Out-of-class work is included only when the plan explicitly identifies its expected time. A shared activity must not spend the same minute independently in several subjects.

    Reserve is explicit. No universal reserve percentage follows from the level definition; a zero reserve remains a disclosed planning choice under the time-axis contract.

    Evidence and uncertainty

    Structural feasibility and workload feasibility are separate findings:

    Finding What it establishes
    Structurally feasible All level targets can be traversed under the resolved prerequisite semantics and declared entry assumptions.
    Workload feasible under stated assumptions A supported estimate, its reserve, and all binding budgets fit under the published reference conditions.
    Workload not yet assessed Necessary estimates, calibration, or reference conditions are missing.

    The workload record identifies its method, version, assumptions, uncertainty, and evidence, such as author estimates, subject-expert review, or relevant empirical calibration. For an interval estimate, state which bound or decision rule supports the budget judgement. Arithmetic fit and strength of evidence remain distinguishable. A structural pass alone must not produce an overall claim of a validated, manageable level.

    A feasible reference budget is not a guarantee for every learner. Already achieved goals reduce an individual's remaining work. Missing entry competencies can add preparation and require a revised personal schedule without changing the reference curriculum's past definition.

    Level completion and individual availability

    Related proposed profile: Assessment-Gated Completion and Prerequisite Discharge derives completion from a fixed level scope, mastery of the required assessments and reviewed static rules for waiving remaining learning work. Mastery is the sole stored goal result; waivers and level completion are calculated. Later-level consumers outside that scope do not block it, and waived work never becomes global mastery. This proposal does not change the currently implemented goal results, level completion, or exam access rules.

    A level declares its completion rule over an explicit required target set and any applicable, resolved selection rules. Completion is derived from the stored goal results using the appropriate goal-type semantics. A date, time spent, or number of consumed planning units does not establish completion. An orientation completion must not be described as evidence of subject-matter competence.

    The level's required scope and the complete Personal Curriculum may differ. Name both scopes. This concept does not introduce a new selection flow or turn existing target goals into optional work. Any optional extension or choice must already be explicitly represented by the applicable scope contract.

    A level-wide completion result is distinct from availability of an individual goal. Within the authorised learning scope, an available Mathematics goal in a later level may be pursued while an earlier level still has open targets in another subject. Do not introduce blanket requires edges between level containers or require all earlier-level targets before every later target.

    The runtime's existing focus, normal/strict evaluation, and continuation rules remain authoritative. A global structural check of the reference plan does not silently change those runtime settings. Level progress is not a cross-subject pace score; the per-subject plan-status contract continues to govern pace and plan balances.

    The knowledge-building metaphor

    The Whitepaper's house metaphor describes learning as building on foundations. Curriculum levels add larger construction stages to that picture:

    Concept Possible representation
    Confirmed subject-matter competence A completed building block
    Topic or subject A room or wing
    Actual prerequisite A supporting connection to the required blocks
    Curriculum level A construction stage, possibly visualised as a floor
    Currently available goal An accessible next building position

    Different wings may grow at different rates. A construction stage can remain partly open while a supported extension grows elsewhere. A visual floor must not imply that every block below it is a prerequisite for every block above it. The larger stage provides a recognisable completion milestone while each confirmed competence remains a visible contribution.

    This is a presentation design, not evidence that a particular visualisation improves motivation or learning. Any implementation derives its state from canonical goal results; moving to another view must not erase or award skills.

    Information needed for a reviewable level plan

    The conceptual contract needs the following information. These are semantic requirements, not a new serialised schema or a second source of target placement.

    Information Purpose
    Plan identity, revision, educational scope, and referenced curriculum/view versions Identify exactly which claim was assessed.
    Ordered stages and resolved target-to-stage assignments Define acquisition stages over stable goal identities.
    Participating subjects and explicit entry basis Make the coverage and starting assumptions visible.
    Resolved prerequisite evidence and structural result Expose later-level conflicts, gaps, and within-level deadlocks.
    Reference learner profile, learning conditions, and workload method Explain the effort estimate and its limits.
    Available overall and binding sub-budgets, activity allocations, and reserve Check capacity without double-counting.
    Completion rule and its required target scope Derive an interpretable milestone from existing goal results.
    Workload result and evidence status Keep unassessed effort distinct from supported feasibility.

    After a relevant change to goals, prerequisites, placements, entry assumptions, or budgets, re-evaluate the affected level claim against the new revision. Preserve the former assessment as a claim about its own inputs; do not silently carry a green result to changed content. A projection change never rewrites historical learner achievements.
