    Appendix B — Implementation Status and Boundaries of Claims

    Status of this edition. Current candidate, 13 November 2026: PR #141 brings together the Gymnasium follow-up work on the updated main baseline 2ad03c810c. The 12 HARD / 50 PARTIAL / 55 OPEN / 28 OPT/DOC rows and four required GVR-012 scopes belong to this candidate, which has not been integrated into main. Local gate, negative-case, documentation, and PDF checks are evidenced in the follow-up record. Final remote CI and main acceptance are not recorded here as complete; the earlier CI for PR #131 does not evidence these changes. This appendix records the repository states against which the specification's claims were audited and documents where the inspected implementation diverges from the normative model (GD-D01). This unified Single-Source edition consolidates the 4 November concept correction, the 8 November atomic normal-form mandate and milestone mapping, the 9 November projection contract clarification, the 10 November closed Personal Curriculum learning scope, the 11 November 2026 decisions on non-compilable learning contexts and on learning-unit validity, and the 12 November 2026 decisions on complete current target-state validation and on the representation of learning units across Web and PDF. Since 11 November 2026, CQR-001 also enforces G17 for the canonical Gymnasium catalog. The CQR/GVR hardening delivered in PR #131 is integrated into main and supported by the dated CI evidence below; its exact domains and two remaining gaps are recorded in the rule registry. Both backend and WebGUI now evaluate the fixed atomic prerequisite closure; the closure compiler PersonalCurriculumLearningScope.java reuses canonical normalization. Local regression tests cover learnability, planning, focus, reference errors, and cross-subject prerequisite delivery. Full native, curriculum, and remote-CI acceptance as well as applicability validation of the current state remain separate steps. Global application recognition (\(\Meff\)) is not yet implemented. Readable documentation evidence or a green document build proves neither runtime conformance nor production deployment.

    Binding Product Owner decisions.

    • 4 November 2026: Implicit mastery through passed assessments of a valid local contract demonstrates independent application and carries global effect (\(\Meff\)); transitive minimality remains mandatory. Assessment and memory goals remain protected: for memory goals, verified completion recall alone establishes goal completion, and subsequent card due state never revokes it (Sections 3, 8, and 13; GD-D03).
    • 7 November 2026: Every reachable prerequisite of a visible goal requires an explicit projection role (target or prerequisiteOnly) in subject composition views; at the Personal Curriculum compile boundary, the 10 November 2026 closure rule supersedes blanket manual per-atom view entries with derived target roles (Section 7.2).
    • 8 November 2026: Atomic normal form \(R\subseteq A\times A\) as the binding canonical target state without cluster exceptions, mandatory ordered migration sequence, and mapping of all 143 rule groups to validation areas and milestones (Sections 2 and 3, Appendix A). The migration-order and preservation obligations from that decision are superseded by the decision of 12 November 2026.
    • 9 November 2026: Projections preserve full prerequisite closure; a canonical cluster never becomes an atomic runtime goal through a composition view (Sections 5 and 6.3).
    • 10 November 2026: The Level 2 Personal Curriculum learning scope is \(L=\Closure(S)\) on the unpruned canonical skill graph, fixed independently of mastery, assigning derived target roles to all necessary prerequisite atoms (Sections 5 and 7).
    • 11 November 2026 (learning context): Hard closure errors belong to validation, curriculum QA, and native acceptance, not to learner reads. When the canonical graph of a learning context is not strictly compilable, the runtime keeps the reviewed seed scope without derived closure, claims no learning basis, offers no goal with broken demand, and logs the failure once per curriculum and error code; seed-selection errors stay hard (Section 7.2).
    • 11 November 2026 (learning units): The recognition rule of 4 November stays: an own ordinary goal is recognized once no open own assessment or memory goal of its unit still requires it (if assessments \(\nd{Q_1}\) and \(\nd{Q_2}\) of the same unit both require \(\nd{A}\), \(\nd{A}\) stays open until both are passed). The alternative recorded on the archived branch of pull request #83 as a “6 November 2026 target decision” – immediate recognition from the qualified coverage list of a single passed assessment, including proof by a parent or foreign unit – was reviewed and is not adopted. Recognition is sound only inside units that satisfy rules R1–R5: R1, R3, and R4 as hard validity conditions from the first authored unit, R2 at M5 with the current per-goal binding at M7, and R5 as a runtime precondition before recognition is enabled in production; a unit's purpose is a secure foundation before planned learning builds on it (Sections 11 and 13.3).
    • 12 November 2026 (G17/GD-C17): Complete deterministic validation of the current declared subject inventory is required before every subject and scope milestone pass M1–M7. Historical closure equality and a fixed migration order are no longer acceptance conditions. Historical reduction proofs remain available for dependent review bindings; they determine neither the current catalog inventory nor the G17 result (Section 3.1).
    • 12 November 2026 (learning-unit representation): Learning units are part of the skill landscape and live in its placement layer; a separate catalog file beside the landscape was rejected as a bolt-on in favor of a stable long-term contract with a new curriculum-package contract version (1.2) where one is needed (Section 11.2). The unit work resumes under the working plan Learning Units for Mathematics and Physics.
    • 12 November 2026 (frozen reduction proof): The prerequisite-reduction proof is frozen at its end state. Its registry (contract V9) records per goal the value the reviews are bound to; an unchanged goal keeps its reviews, a changed goal needs a new review, and every other catalog change leaves the reuse untouched. The history is no longer replayed against the current catalog (Section 3.1).
    • 13 November 2026 (GVR-012 from M7): The stronger semantic route check shall become mandatory for every registered QA scope of each subject obligated to meet M7. Today this covers seven scopes of five M7 subjects; four are secured. Activation of the complete M7 obligation waits for evidence-backed closure of the other three M7 scopes. The full inventory remains twelve; the five M6 scopes become mandatory on later promotion to M7. Existing required scopes and pins remain protected even through later status changes. GVR-012 stays PARTIAL until complete technical enforcement; this target decision neither activates the obligation nor supplies pass evidence or lowers any floor.

    Appendix C — Verification Strategy for Completion Semantics

    The accompanying script check-assessment-model.py is a mathematical reference check written with the Python standard library alone. It verifies the formulas from Section 13 across small finite models, reads no learner data, and mutates no runtime state (GD-D04).

    • Finite model checking. Every topologically ordered DAG of up to four vertices under every assignment of explicit mastery and protected sets where local content goals have complete local assessment coverage: \(5\,222\) valid cases. Graph traversal and static transitive closure yield the identical implicit set \(I_U\).
    • Minimality. Transitive reduction is minimal and preserves closure. Redundant edges of the normalized relation do not alter recognition. The older inheritance counterexample diagnoses transitional data; this bounded model test does not replace complete G17 validation of the current catalog (GD-D05).
    • Global monotonicity. Under fixed valid contracts, additional explicit mastery never reopens effectively mastered work. Assessment and memory goals are never implicitly passed (GD-D06).
    • Recursive units. \(128\) state assignments of the Mechanics example verify completion \(\Complete(U)\), own recognition \(I_U\), and explicit child obligations. A pure aggregate requires no own assessment; a pure parent synthesis requires no new content goals.
    • Unit block grouping. \(948\) grouping cases compare the unit-block DAG against a complete block sequence. One counterexample exhibits an acyclic atomic DAG whose coarse block summary forms a cycle; no technical entry-lock policy is derived from it.
    • Concrete counterexamples. No stopping barrier at mastered intermediate goals; local recognition preserved despite an open later assessment; durable union across contracts, future prerequisites, intersection with planned target sets, and external support outside \(\Own(U)\).
    • Invalid inputs. Cycles, unknown references, and uncovered local content goals fail closed as errors rather than defaulting to automatic mastery.

    Diagram generation in generate-completion-diagrams.py verifies the concrete sets displayed in each figure and compares freshly rendered PNG bytes in --check mode (GD-D07). These model and diagram checks verify neither real task quality, grading reliability, identity verification, nor pedagogical effectiveness. Required independent subject-matter content review remains a separate step; runtime integration and its security, concurrency, and UI tests remain separate engineering work.

    Sources: check-assessment-model.py; generate-completion-diagrams.py.

    Appendix D — Glossary and Symbols

    D.1 Terms

    The first table defines the terms in the order in which this specification introduces them and gives the corresponding German terms. The second table lists the mathematical symbols. None of these definitions introduces an additional stored outcome kind: only the binary outcome \(m\) is persisted per atomic goal.

    Term Meaning
    goal (Lernziel, Ziel) Node of the skill graph with a stable UUID identity.
    atomic goal, atom (atomares Ziel, Atom) Goal without contains children. Only atoms carry a stored learner outcome.
    cluster goal, cluster (Cluster) Goal with contains children; structures and aggregates content rather than being mastered directly.
    containment (Containment) The hierarchy formed by authored contains edges.
    requirement, prerequisite (Anforderung, Voraussetzung) The edge \((g,p)\in R\) is a requirement of \(g\); goal \(p\) is a prerequisite of \(g\).
    authored, derived (verfasst, abgeleitet) Only the stored edges in \(C\) and \(R\) are authored. Everything else is computed from them.
    inherited, effective requirement (geerbte, effektive Anforderung) An inherited requirement is one that a goal acquires from a contains ancestor. Effective requirements combine a goal's own and inherited requirements.
    atomic basis (atomare Basis) For an atom, the goal itself; for a cluster, its atomic descendants.
    atomic demand (atomarer Bedarf) All atoms that a goal directly or transitively requires, independent of learner state.
    atomic normal form (atomare Normalform) The property \(R\subseteq A\times A\): all authored requirements connect atomic goals.
    target state, transitional data (Zielzustand, Übergangsbestand) Target state is the intended state of canonical data: atomic normal form and transitive minimality. Transitional data denotes inventories that still carry cluster requirements.
    transitive minimality (transitive Minimalität) No authored requirement is already reachable through the remaining requirements.
    motivation anchor, terminal autonomy goal (Orientierungsanker, terminales Autonomieziel) Starting and ending endpoints of a didactic route.
    explicit mastery, Mastery (Mastery) The stored explicit outcome of an atomic goal: mastered or not yet mastered.
    implicit mastery (implizite Beherrschung) Mastery derived from passed assessments of a valid local recognition contract; for content goals it demonstrates independent application.
    effective mastery, effectively mastered (effektiv beherrscht) Explicitly mastered or implicitly mastered. Both carry equivalent global effect.
    satisfaction (Erfüllung) A goal or cluster is satisfied when its atomic basis is non-empty and effectively mastered.
    frontier (Frontier) The next available atomic goals: themselves not yet mastered, with all required atoms satisfied.
    filter, focus (Filter, Fokus) A filter selects a subset of the skill graph. Focus is a learner's temporary working scope (Level 3).
    normal (optimistic) mode, strict (pessimistic) mode (Normalmodus, strikter Modus) Normal (optimistic) mode checks prerequisites inside the selected scope; strict mode checks all prerequisites globally.
    level of personalization (Personalisierungsstufe, Level) Four narrowing levels: base curriculum, Personal Curriculum, focus with one active goal, and global mastery.
    Personal Curriculum (persönlicher Lehrplan) Committed personal goal selection including its necessary atomic prerequisites at Level 2; fixed independently of mastery under a given configuration. Existing valid recognition counts as satisfied.
    projection, composition view (Projektion, Kompositionssicht) A projection derives the visible tree for a resolved scope. A composition view explicitly defines that scope's reviewed upper tree structure.
    projection role (Projektionsrolle) target: selectable learning target of the view. prerequisiteOnly: subject-local prerequisite support; necessary atoms still receive an acquisition target in the full Personal Curriculum.
    applicability (applicability, Anwendbarkeit) Compiled dimensional metadata specifying in which scopes a goal is visible.
    program unit, goal placement (programUnits, goalPlacements) Organizational container such as a school year or semester; entry attaching an unchanged goal to such a unit. Kind learningUnit declares a learning unit, and relation own assigns it an atomic goal it owns.
    goal type, node type (Zieltyp) Pedagogical kind of an atomic goal: content goal, orientation goal, memory goal, or assessment goal.
    content goal, ordinary subject goal (Inhaltsziel) An ordinary subject-matter learning goal; labelled “ordinary” in figures.
    verified recall, completion recall (verifizierter Abruf, Abschlussabruf) Controlled unaided recall of a card without prompt hints. Verified completion recall across all required cards completes a memory goal.
    direct assessment access (direkter Prüfungszugang) A deliberately chosen assessment attempt is not blocked by unmastered preparation.
    learning unit, child unit, required child (Lerneinheit, Kindeinheit, Pflichtkind) Authorized educational unit with a stated learning outcome; its subunits; the mandatory subunits among them.
    aggregate, synthesis (Aggregat, Synthese) An aggregate bundles child units without adding its own assessment. A synthesis unit additionally assesses their integration.
    local recognition contract (lokaler Vertrag) Binds a learning unit's own goals, its curriculum revision, and the subject-reviewed application meaning of its assessments; also called an acquisition contract.
    unit validity rules R1–R5 (Gültigkeitsregeln) The five conditions of 11 November 2026 without which implicit recognition is unsound: an assessment path for every own ordinary goal (R1), reviewed coverage evidence (R2), coverage only within the assessment's prerequisites (R3), exactly one directly owning unit (R4), and a versioned contract (R5).
    protected goal (geschütztes Ziel) Assessment or memory goal; never implicitly passed.
    open demand (offene Nachfrage) A learning unit's still-open protected goals together with their full transitive atomic demand.
    consumer (Verbraucher) A goal that requires another goal, viewed from that prerequisite.
    whole-unit prerequisite, entry lock (Einheitsvoraussetzung, Einheitssperre) Explicitly authorized prerequisite between whole learning units; a hard technical access lock derived from it. Whether such locks exist remains undecided.
    planning period, entry basis, completion witness (Planungsstufe, Eintrittsbasis, Abschlusszeuge) Calendar interval of an optional schedule; competencies assumed present at entry; ordering that proves a period's targets can be acquired step by step.
    planned target, actual count, cumulative progress (Plan-Soll, IST, Gesamt-IST) Planned target is the scheduled progress of a named scope; actual count is achieved progress. Cumulative actual progress (Gesamt-IST) counts a plan's effectively mastered goals.
    profile (Profil) Named set of additional constraints that applies only where explicitly selected, such as a route, validator, or planning profile.
    scope, QA scope (Scope, QA-Scope) The named validation scope of a rule or route profile.
    gate (Gate) Automated check whose failure blocks a milestone or build.
    maturity level, milestone (Reifegrad, Meilenstein) Level M0 to M7 of automated curriculum QA; Appendix A refers to them as milestones. M7 is not human effectiveness proof.

    D.2 Symbols

    The following table summarizes all mathematical symbols across the specification (graph core in Sections 1 to 3, learner state and filters in Sections 4 and 5, Personal Curriculum in Section 7, goal types in Section 8, learning units and implicit mastery in Sections 11 and 13, planning in Section 12).

    Note on unified notation. Earlier standalone English Markdown drafts used \(D(g)\) for atomic demand, \(U(T)\) for the prerequisite closure of a subset, \(M_A\) for the explicit mastery set, \(M_k\) and \(T_k\) (or \(M\) and \(T\)) for orientation anchors and terminal autonomy goals of a route profile, and \(K\) for memory goals. The unified Single-Source specification standardizes these symbols across both Web and PDF to \(\ReqAtoms(g)\), \(\Closure(T)\), \(M\), \(O_k\) and \(Z_k\) (or \(O\) and \(Z\)), and \(A_{\mathrm{mem}}\), keeping \(K=G\setminus A\) unambiguous for structural clusters and \(M\) unambiguous for explicit mastery. The unchanged static original illustration 06-available-next-goals.png (Figure 8) still shows the historical label \(M_A\), as noted in its caption.

    Symbol Meaning
    \(G\), \(A\), \(K\) Goals, structural atoms, clusters; \(K=G\setminus A\).
    \(C\), \(R\) Authored edges: containment (contains) and direct requirements (requires).
    \(X^+\), \(X^{-1}\) Non-empty transitive closure and inverse relation of a relation \(X\).
    \(\Anc(g)\), \(\Desc(g)\) Ancestors and descendants along contains.
    \(\Atoms(g)\) Atomic basis; the singleton \(\{g\}\) if \(g\) is atomic, otherwise its atomic descendants.
    \(\Reff\), \(R_{\mathrm{inherited}}\) Effective requirements and their inherited subset; \(\Pre_{\mathrm{eff}}(g)\) denotes the effective prerequisites of \(g\).
    \(\Rat\) Fully atomically expanded effective requirement relation, also used in the completion formulas.
    \(R_A\) Directly authored atomic requirements \(R\cap(A\times A)\).
    \(B(g)\), \(\ReqAtoms(g)\) Atomic basis of the effective prerequisites of \(g\) and full atomic demand (written \(D(g)\) in earlier standalone English drafts).
    \(O\), \(Z\), \(E_{\mathrm{route}}\) (\(O_k\), \(Z_k\)) Orientation (motivation) anchors, terminal autonomy goals, and exempt support goals (or per route profile \(k\); written \(M\) and \(T\) or \(M_k\) and \(T_k\) in earlier standalone drafts).
    \(m\), \(M\) Stored binary state per atomic goal and the set of its explicit successes. \(m(a)=0\) does not rule out implicit mastery. Earlier standalone drafts and static Figures 8 and 9 write \(M_A\).
    \(\Meff\) Global union of explicit mastery and validly derived implicit mastery.
    \(\Sat(g,\Meff)\) Satisfaction of an atomic goal or cluster.
    \(\Frontier\), \(\Frontier_K\) Next available atomic goals; derived cluster availability.
    \(P(S)\), \(w(a)\) Weighted progress across a goal set \(S\); progress weight of an atomic goal \(a\).
    \(F\), \(G_F\), \(A_F\) Filter predicate, selected goals, selected atomic goals.
    \(\Applicability\), \(D\), \(V_d\) Applicability mapping, set of filter dimensions, and value vocabulary of dimension \(d\). In Section 5, \(Q\) denotes the active query selection.
    \(\Closure(T)\) Selected atomic goals \(T\) together with their full atomic prerequisite demand (written \(U(T)\) in earlier standalone drafts).
    \(S\), \(L\) Union of valid atomic seed targets across the selected reviewed subject scopes and the resulting closed Personal Curriculum learning scope \(L=\Closure(S)\) at Level 2.
    \(\Frontier_{\mathrm{opt}}\), \(\Frontier_{\mathrm{strict}}\) Frontier in normal (optimistic) mode and in strict mode.
    \(\Missing\), \(\MissingAtoms\) Unsatisfied effective prerequisite nodes; missing atomic goals of the demand.
    \(\operatorname{Cards}(g)\) Fully resolved valid set of required cards of memory goal \(g\).
    \(\operatorname{RecallPassed}(g)\) Explicitly verified successful completion recall across \(\operatorname{Cards}(g)\).
    \(\Score\), \(\Passed\) Score and pass result of an assessment goal; \(\MaxPoints\) and \(\PassingPoints\) come from the scoring rubric.
    \(U\), \(\Own(U)\), \(T(U)\) Learning unit, directly owned atomic acquisition targets, and total recursive scope including child units; \(\Children(U)\) denotes its required child units.
    \(\Complete(U)\) Recursively derived unit completion; never an additionally persisted unit-mastery state.
    \(Q\), \(A_{\mathrm{mem}}\) In Section 13, the sets of atomic assessment goals and atomic memory goals (written \(K\) for memory goals in earlier standalone drafts).
    \(Q_U\), \(A_{\mathrm{mem},U}\), \(P_U\) Directly owned assessment, memory, and combined protected goal sets; \(A_{\mathrm{mem},U}=\Own(U)\cap A_{\mathrm{mem}}\).
    \(\operatorname{Reach}_{\Rat}^{*}(X)\) The set \(X\) together with all atomic goals its elements directly or transitively require.
    \(N_U\) Open demand: full transitive demand of the unit's still-open own protected goals, without stopping at mastered intermediate goals.
    \(I_U\) Implicit set: directly owned unprotected goals outside \(M\) and \(N_U\). For content goals, it witnesses locally derived independent application. Not an additional stored outcome kind.
    \(\operatorname{Cov}(q)\) Reviewed coverage claim of an assessment \(q\): the goals whose independent application its tasks and normal grading test; under rule R3 never larger than \(\{b\in A\mid(q,b)\in\Rat^+\}\). Grants no recognition by itself.
    \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{valid}}\) Person- and revision-bound, durably applicable authoritative local recognition contracts; not merely currently visible units or their latest revisions.
    \(T_{\mathrm{Plan}}\), \(H_{\mathrm{gesamt}}\) Planned target goal set and count of its effectively mastered goals.
    \(T_\pi\), \(B_\pi\), \(\ell_\pi\) Planned acquisition targets, entry basis, and optional calendar-bound planning periods; \(n_\pi\) is the number of periods and \(S_i\) is the target set of period \(i\).
    \(\Rat^{\pi}\), \(\Frontier_\pi\), \(\ReqAtoms_\pi\) Plan requirement relation excluding outgoing requirements of the entry basis; corresponding frontier and atomic demand.
    \(W^{(r)}\) Hypothetical mastery state of the witness verification algorithm after \(r\) steps.
    \(\Theta_\pi\), \(\widehat t_h(\Theta_\pi)\) Declared estimation assumptions of the plan and the non-negative duration of activity \(h\) estimated under them; \(\mathcal H_i\) denotes the activities of period \(i\).
    \(\Work\), \(\Reserve\), \(\Budget\) Scheduled workload, protected reserve, and available time budget of a planning period.
