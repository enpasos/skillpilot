    Curriculum Time Axis, Buffer Planning, and Target-vs-Actual Learning Pace

    Status: Target concept; local, purely browser-based planning pilot implemented. The backend-authoritative target architecture is still open.

    Binding correction of 21 September 2026: Stored learning-goal results are the sole source of goal achievement in the Cockpit, learning-goal tree, and teacher planning. The former manual instructional progress state (CoverageEvent, CoverageLedgerAttestation, instructional actuals together with their associated curve, forecast, and confirmation workflow) is superseded, including as a future target architecture. The corresponding technical design details in Sections 4–18 and in the historical staged rollout plan remain preserved to contextualize existing datasets; they are not active implementation or acceptance requirements. Legacy data is neither deleted nor reinterpreted as mastery. The current pilot contract is defined in Section 19.1; personal target-vs-actual status is governed by the Unified Learning-Plan Status Concept.

    Outcome boundary of 29 October 2026: Mastery is the sole stored outcome state per learning goal: achieved or still open. This also applies to exam goals. Planning reads this state and may evaluate event timestamps for trajectory metrics; it introduces no separate pass projection, no qualifying secondary proof, and no stored failed attempts. The former requirement for AssessmentResultEvent is withdrawn. Missing timestamp or reporting data limits the evaluable history, not the recognition of existing mastery. Percentage values aggregate across multiple goals.

    Scope: First-party SkillPilot WebGUI and backend; no implicit modification of published provider contracts or historical artifacts.

    1. Objective

    SkillPilot should answer not only what is to be learned and what is already mastered, but also:

    • when a subject section is scheduled to start and end,
    • how much real instructional capacity is available during that period,
    • how much buffer remains deliberately protected,
    • what progress is expected by a given date,
    • which concrete learning or exam goals should be completed by a milestone date,
    • how quickly new learning goals have actually been achieved since the individual learning start,
    • whether the stored individual learning progress matches the plan,
    • how a teacher can present both the plan and the current plan posture transparently on a single page to department heads or school leadership,
    • and what transparent plan adjustment would make sense when deviations occur.

    The primary school scenario is a subject course planned by a teacher. The same model must also work for a personal learning plan without a class. The school leadership view is an access-controlled projection of the very same course plan, aggregated by default, rather than a separate reporting system with diverging numbers.

    2. Core Decision

    From a user perspective, a cluster such as "Linear Functions" sits on the time axis as a bar with a concrete start-to-end date range. Technically, however, the canonical cluster receives no date fields.

    The time axis is a dedicated, versioned planning layer:

    • Goals and clusters define what is learned.
    • programUnits and goalPlacements define where a goal resides within a program.
    • The Composition View defines which unambiguous tree a resolved scope sees.
    • A curriculum pacing plan defines when and with what capacity a class or individual works through this material.
    • Mastery defines what an individual has mastered based on available evidence.

    Absolute dates belong neither on canonical clusters nor on programUnits, goalPlacements, or Composition Views. The same cluster can fall into different time windows across different classes, school years, federal states, and individual plans.

    The pacing plan is therefore an execution schedule orthogonal to the four personalization levels. It is neither a new learning goal nor a replacement for Personal Curriculum, focus, frontier, or mastery.

    flowchart LR
    C[Curriculum and Composition View] --> R[Resolved target atomic goals]
    R --> P[Versioned curriculum pacing plan]
    K[Instructional calendar and capacity] --> P
    P --> S[Planned curve]
    M[Stored learning-goal results] --> A[Individual goal achievement]
    S --> V[Target vs. actual, forecast, and buffer]

    3. Single Outcome State, Explicitly Named Planning Scope

    Teachers and learners see the same stored results per learner:

    1. Planned target: What should have been achieved by today according to the pacing plan?
    2. Goal achievement: Which goals have been achieved according to the stored learning state?

    A plan determines dates and scope, not learning outcomes. There is no additional manual instructional confirmation that reveals or hides a stored completion. A completed orientation goal likewise counts within its goal scope without thereby proving subject-matter mastery.

    The full personal goal scope may be larger than the plan set G recorded at the start of the plan—for example due to previously achieved goals or a narrower planning scope. Wherever scopes match, the exact same counting rules apply everywhere; differing scopes are named explicitly. An unchanged planning baseline is not a current learning state. Querying progress rewrites neither the plan nor stored results.

    Missing or stale results remain recognizable as unavailable rather than producing an apparently reliable zero balance. Former coverage documentation is not used as a fallback source.

    4. Functional Domain Model

    4.1 InstructionalCalendar — Instructional Calendar

    The calendar describes the time available specifically for a given course or personal plan:

    • school year and IANA time zone, for example Europe/Berlin,
    • recurring subject lessons with their duration in minutes,
    • school holidays, public holidays, and weeks without subject instruction,
    • cancellations, class trips, project days, and other exclusions,
    • additional or rescheduled lessons.

    Internally, minutes should serve as the shared capacity unit. The UI can render them as lesson periods, double periods, or clock hours. This keeps 45-, 60-, and 90-minute lesson models comparable.

    Calendar data is plan-local or reusable at the institutional level. It does not belong inside a curriculum package because holidays and timetables depend on the school year, location, school, class, and subject.

    The calendar provides only available subject capacity. How that capacity is allocated to initial instruction, review, assessment, remediation, buffer, or still-unallocated time belongs exclusively in the pacing plan. This ensures the same minute is never deducted in the calendar and then booked a second time in the plan.

    4.2 PacingPlan — Curriculum Pacing Plan

    A pacing plan is a stably identified plan for a class or an individual learner. It contains at least:

    • owner type class or learner,
    • title, subject, and school year,
    • the complete Level 2 fingerprint as its validity context,
    • an explicit planningScope, for example subject plus grade level, program unit, or Composition subtree,
    • references to Curriculum, Composition View, and Instructional Calendar,
    • scheduleStartDate and the final plan end date of the shared time grid,
    • a versioned interpretation of the existing mastery state; mastery-threshold-0.9-v1 denotes solely the legacy numeric compatibility evaluation, not a graded subject-matter mastery scale,
    • status draft, published, or retired,
    • a sequence of immutable revisions.

    A class plan represents the shared target schedule. A PlanAssignment binds a learner to the plan through time-bounded revision bindings and records that learner's learning start and baseline. A personal plan can exist without a class plan.

    4.3 PlanRevision — Immutable Published Revision

    Drafts may be edited. A published revision is never overwritten. It stores:

    • revision number and effectiveFrom,
    • predecessor revision and reason for change,
    • scope and projection digests,
    • immutable calendar revision and calendar digest,
    • materialized daily or weekly capacity including all exclusions and overrides,
    • the goal set resolved at that time,
    • positive reference capacity referenceWeekMinutes and start-of-week day,
    • timeline blocks, milestones, pins, and buffers,
    • a versioned calculation and display policy for measurement windows, minimum observation thresholds, and speedometer scaling,
    • creator and creation timestamp,
    • a version number for optimistic locking.

    When replanning, the past portion of the effective schedule is preserved. Only the future portion is replaced by a new revision. Historical target-vs-actual reports therefore cannot be retroactively "planned into the green." Every revision has a half-open validity interval. Plan-local units of already bound goals remain constant across successor revisions; the new planned curve starts with the unchanged continuity offset of the previous curve. Without a new mastery or coverage event, switching revisions must not create a jump in target, actual, or variance values.

    4.4 TimelineBlock — Timeline Block

    A timeline block is the visible bar on the time axis. It contains:

    • a stable block ID and a human-readable label,
    • an explicit reference to a canonical cluster or a stable Composition View node,
    • startDate and endDateInclusive as local calendar dates,
    • a role,
    • scheduledGoalIds as the materialized set of all goals worked on in this block,
    • countedTargetGoalIds as the unambiguous subset that counts toward planned progress for the first time in this block,
    • separate digests for both goal sets,
    • explicitly allocated minutes or lesson slots and their distribution within the date range,
    • when bound to milestones, stable capacity slices with ID, source slot, minute share, and exactly one workItemGoalId prepared by that slice,
    • plan-local planning units and optional goal overrides,
    • optional pins on start dates, end dates, or milestones,
    • the forecast horizon d_b materialized at publication time, derived from the earliest binding pin of this block or its own end date.

    Recommended roles for the initial contract:

    • primary: initial scheduled instruction; counts toward planned progress,
    • revisit: review; consumes capacity, does not count goals a second time,
    • assessment: exam or diagnostic check; consumes capacity, provides an assessment slot, and can be linked to a milestone, but is itself neither a milestone nor silent proof of mastery,
    • remediation: explicit catch-up work on prerequisites or gaps,
    • buffer: protected reserve without learning-goal points.

    A block may reference a structural node, but its scheduled goal set is deterministically compiled upon publication. Historical evaluations do not re-expand the reference against a subsequently modified live tree.

    A date range alone does not yet reserve capacity. For every block, it must therefore be specified which minutes or concrete subject slots it occupies. A simple initial variant distributes allocatedMinutes proportionally across the slots available within the block's date range; explicit slot assignments override this distribution. Only this makes both the planned curve and overbooking checks deterministic. A capacity slice remains owned by exactly one block; milestones merely reference it and do not create a second time booking. If an instructional phase combines multiple goals, planning splits its minutes conservatively across multiple slices. That is a planning approximation, not a claim about second-by-second classroom impact.

    The sum of work slices must not exceed the content minutes of the block. Block minutes not broken down further remain visible for coarse block planning, but must not inflate the denominator of a milestone speedometer.

    Accordingly, a buffer block materializes BufferCapacitySlice entries with ID, source slot, and minute share, but without a learning goal. Referencing a buffer in a forecast does not release that buffer yet; only a new effective revision may turn it into content capacity.

    Within a revision, each buffer slice carries at most one forecastReservationKey. This reserves it exclusively for exactly one still-open forecast commitment—either a block or a milestone. Multiple displays may reference the same reservation only if they describe the exact same functional workload according to goal-set and occurrence digests; even then, the capacity is counted only once across the course. A functionally different block, milestone, or second forecast must not claim the same buffer slice again as reserve.

    In the first version, programUnits may group rows or headings on the time axis, but they are not a valid block target: a program unit does not possess an unambiguous atomic goal set of its own. A later extension would require a dedicated, scope-bound expansion rule via goalPlacements.

    4.5 PlanMilestone — Concrete Goals by a Target Date

    In addition to longer cluster blocks, a plan can contain a due-date-sorted list of concrete milestones, for example:

    Abitur Mathematics problems by 15 March 2027

    A milestone contains:

    • stable ID, clear title, and dueDate,
    • functional kind kind: learning | assessment | exam,
    • deadline bindingness deadlinePolicy: target | hard,
    • workloadGoalIds: the concrete learning goals that may be scheduled as the functionally admissible preparation workload for this milestone,
    • when referencing a cluster, the materialized goal set including its digest,
    • exactly one typed fulfillment rule: allCurrentlyMastered(goalIds), atLeastNCurrentlyMastered(goalIds, n); for exam milestones, these rules reference the exam learning goals,
    • the functional references of the fulfillment rule kept separate from the preparation workload,
    • concrete capacityAllocationIds pointing to capacity slices with exactly one goal ID and a minute share,
    • optional releasableBufferAllocationIds that fall no later than the local due date and are designated as releasable buffer specifically for this forecast,
    • as well as the designated assessment slot where applicable.

    This separation is essential: in the example "Abitur Mathematics problems by 15 March 2027", the preparatory learning goals can govern gradual progress and required pace, whereas mastery of the concrete exam learning goals governs completion of the milestone.

    The initial contract deliberately restricts admissible combinations:

    • kind: learning uses a mastery-based rule; in the initial contract, its set of admissible goal IDs is identical to workloadGoalIds.
    • kind: assessment | exam uses allCurrentlyMastered across the exam learning goals. Their IDs are functionally linked to the preparation workload via a published assessmentGoalMapping. The preparation workload may be empty, in which case no pace speedometer exists.

    For the first pacing-plan version, assessmentId is the stable canonical ID of the exam/assessment goal. A content digest of its approved examData, including the grading rubric, describes the scheduled task and its authoring revision. It is not an additional individual success condition: existing mastery of this stable goal ID counts immediately. A functionally different learning goal requires an appropriately reviewed goal model, not a hidden second pass status attached to the existing goal ID.

    For atLeastNCurrentlyMastered, each PlanAssignment additionally materializes activeWorkloadGoalIds before rendering a speedometer. First, all alternatives currently mastered with rule effect are taken into account—both baseline goals and goals achieved after learning start, even if they were not previously selected. If their count is already at least n, the rule is fulfilled and there is no remaining open speedometer workload. Otherwise, the assignment adds exactly n - q concrete still-open alternatives, where q is the number of these currently rule-effective completions.

    A relevant mastery event therefore triggers a deterministic, logged rematerialization: existing still-open selections are retained in stable selection order, with only surplus trailing entries dropped or missing slots explicitly filled. Thus, newly mastering a previously unselected alternative goal immediately reduces the open workload accurately. As long as the required open selection has not been determined, the UI displays the alternatives group rather than a single pseudo-precise pointer. Changing the selection freely is an audited assignment adjustment; the broader set of functionally admissible alternatives remains unchanged and visible.

    target denotes an adjustable target date. hard denotes an external or deliberately fixed date that neither tolerance bands nor automatic replanning may shift. Modifying it requires a new revision, the appropriate capability, and both actor and rationale; for a class plan, this is normally the authorized teacher, and for a personal plan, its owner. Externally mandated exam dates may only be superseded by an equally authorized replacement date.

    Milestones can originate from the published class plan or be added personally. A personal entry modifies neither the class plan nor other learners' milestones and must remain resolvable within the learner's own Personal Curriculum.

    The milestone itself consumes no minutes and generates no mastery. The associated primary, revisit, assessment, or remediation blocks carry the capacity. A fixed milestone can pin those blocks' end dates and forms a boundary for forecasting and replanning.

    For pace calculations, only the content capacity explicitly allocated to this preparation workload up to the end of the local dueDate counts. An underlying slot allocation may serve multiple milestones as the same work if it prepares the exact same workItemGoalId there via the same capacity slice; this avoids double-booking any minute in the overall plan. By contrast, two functionally disjoint milestone workloads must not each claim the same capacity share for themselves. Where workloads overlap only partially, solely the slices belonging to the genuinely shared goal IDs may be referenced multiple times. Required prerequisites and assessment slots must likewise fall no later than the due date. For buffer slices, the shared revision-wide exclusivity rule from Section 4.4 applies. Once buffer is actually released, the new revision regenerates subsequent forecasts from the remaining reserve.

    All fulfillment rules read the current mastery state. If mastery is legitimately revoked or corrected on subject-matter grounds, a previously fulfilled milestone can become open again. A later ordinary failed attempt on an exam does not revoke existing mastery. No pass projection exists independently of mastery.

    Fulfillment status is evaluated per PlanAssignment. A class milestone is therefore not summarily "done for the class" merely because a single learner has fulfilled it; course coverage remains a separate track.

    For each fulfillment rule, the projection materializes its chronologically ordered validity episodes—that is, every transition into and out of "rule currently fulfilled." Before the due date, it distinguishes openBeforeDue and currentlyFulfilledBeforeDue; after the due date, completedOnTime, completedLate, overdueOpen, and currentlyReopened. Decisive are the end of the local due date in the plan revision's time zone and calendar, the truth value of the rule at that exact boundary, and the current episode:

    • If the rule is fulfilled today and was fulfilled at the end of the due date, the status is completedOnTime.
    • If it is fulfilled today, but was open at the end of the due date, the status is completedLate; the UI shows the start of the current re-fulfillment episode, not some earlier historical completion.
    • If it is open today and there has never been a valid fulfillment episode, the status is overdueOpen.
    • If it is open today and there was at least one prior valid fulfillment episode, the status is currentlyReopened; the metadata fields fulfilledAtDue and reopenedAt additionally preserve whether the rule held at the end of the due date and when the most recent episode reopened.

    An initial completion before the due date therefore does not retroactively make a later re-fulfillment on time if the rule was open on the due date itself. Conversely, losing mastery only after the due date does not erase the historically accurate statement that the milestone was fulfilled on the due date. Append-only correction chains can transparently re-evaluate the episode projection for a new snapshot; the old snapshot remains stable via its watermark.

    From the plan revision and milestones, the runtime first compiles sorted DatedGoalOccurrenceTemplate entries for the concrete goal list. Each entry has a stable occurrence key, a functional goal or assessment ID, a role, a date, exactly one date source, and an explicit dateSemantics:

    • deadline applies only to a published milestone, an explicitly ordered or dated goal occurrence, or a concrete assessment slot;
    • contextEnd is purely a display fallback to the end of its own primary, revisit, or remediation block.

    A contextEnd merely states "this goal lies within this time segment." It is not an independent target deadline and must never trigger overdue, completedLate, or an atomic target quota. The user interface labels the date semantics explicitly rather than making both kinds of dates look identical. As a result, the same goal may carry multiple visible dates—such as "learn," "review," and "assess"—while still counting only once toward primary planning units. A minimum-count requirement is rendered as an "at least n of m" group with genuine alternatives rather than decomposed into m seemingly mandatory individual goals.

    Per assignment, DatedGoalOccurrenceStatus projects orthogonal fields rather than overlapping single status values:

    • completionState: open | done,
    • scheduleState: noDeadline | notDue | overdue | completedOnTime | completedLate,
    • readinessState: ready | blocked including blockedReasons,
    • data quality of the information available for the report.

    For a compact primary badge, precedence is deterministically done before overdue before blocked before open; the remaining fields stay visible alongside it. A goal that is both open past a genuine deadline and functionally blocked is therefore labeled primarily "overdue" while additionally explaining its blockers. A currently mastered goal remains "done" even if a prerequisite gap is surfaced separately. contextEnd always enforces scheduleState: noDeadline.

    For primary learning goals, completion and readiness follow from current mastery and effective prerequisites. For review or remediation, a dedicated PlanActivityEvent is required; existing mastery and mere course coverage do not prove this personal activity. For assessments, the learning-goal outcome likewise follows exclusively from mastery. Initial exam dates and retake dates remain scheduling dates, not separate outcome states. From mastery alone, neither the attended exam sitting nor any failed attempt can be reconstructed; any corresponding historical statement remains unknown without reliable process data.

    Free-form checklist items without a stable functional reference are possible as organizational notes, but count neither toward the planned curve nor as achieved learning goals. "Task opened" or "covered in class" does not automatically fulfill a functional milestone; its published fulfillment rule remains decisive.

    4.6 PlanAssignment — Assignment and Learning Start

    The assignment linking a plan lineage and a learner maintains a sequence of effective AssignmentRevisionBinding entries and additionally records:

    • assignedAt and the functional learningStartedAt,
    • the goal IDs already mastered at start as the baseline,
    • the baseline digest and baseline planning units,
    • the comparison mode classSchedule or rebaseAtStart,
    • assignment-specific active goal selections for alternative milestones,
    • optional authorized individual calendar adjustments,
    • assignment status and end timestamp.

    Learning start is not automatically account creation, first login, or first mastery update. When a learner joins a class late, personal velocity measurement begins at actual entry.

    classSchedule continues to compare a late entrant against the published class schedule. rebaseAtStart, by contrast, distributes only the personal goal workload remaining after the baseline starting from the individual learning start. The mode is chosen explicitly rather than inferred from data.

    In classSchedule mode, individual absences alter personal remaining capacity and forecasts, not retroactively the shared class planned curve. If the personal planned curve is also meant to diverge, the assignment requires rebaseAtStart or an explicitly published individual plan revision. Accommodations and disadvantage compensation thus become visible as authorized planning rather than being treated as a silent arithmetic trick.

    4.7 MasteryEvent and Achievement Projections

    For learning velocity, the current mastery record alone is insufficient. An append-only MasteryEvent records at least:

    • learner ID and stable goal ID,
    • prior and new binary mastery state,
    • event type, idempotency key, and predecessor event where applicable,
    • occurredAt kept separate from recordedAt,
    • source, for example first-party WebGUI, import, Verified Recall, or an explicitly versioned provider integration,
    • import and trust classification,
    • optionally the state version that caused the change.

    These events log transitions of the existing goal outcome and do not create a second success state. Legacy numeric events are retained as compatibility data. Where reliable history exists, two distinct timestamps can be derived from them:

    • firstEverAchievedAt: first valid transition to mastery across the entire history;
    • firstAchievedAtForAssignment: first valid transition to mastery after learningStartedAt for a goal that did not belong to the baseline.

    If a goal was previously achieved once, had become open again by plan start, and is completed again later, that subsequent completion is classified as recovered rather than new. It may count once toward achieved plan scope for this assignment, but is broken out separately in the UI. A further downgrade followed by another completion does not count a second time. Current mastery state remains separately visible alongside it.

    The legacy numeric interpretation mastery-threshold-0.9-v1 remains traceable for already bound legacy data. It is not an additional pass requirement imposed by a plan. The functional goal model knows only mastery or non-mastery; timestamps and new/recovered classify trajectory history, not the validity of current achievement. Historical values are never rewritten, and no new timestamp is fabricated when history is missing.

    A subject-matter downgrade following new evidence (LOSS_REASSESSMENT) leaves the earlier achievement intact as a historical fact. A data correction (DATA_CORRECTION_VOID), by contrast, declares an erroneous prior entry invalid in append-only fashion and removes it from derived achievement curves. Every writer admitted for planning metrics in the future must use the same central, transactional mutation/event path with normalized source and idempotency once that respective writer is explicitly approved for this new planning feature. A source value coach is provenance only, not implementation approval. Existing writes are not retroactively reinterpreted as temporally precise events. As long as a write path supplies no reliable trajectory data, its existing mastery outcomes remain valid; only the velocity display is restricted for that share of the data. Provider contracts and historical published artifacts retain their own modification and acceptance boundaries.

    Imported prior knowledge without a reliable original timestamp is treated as baseline and produces no artificial velocity spike on the import date. If the import occurs after learning start, the original baseline remains unchanged; an audited BaselineAdjustment increases the current actual state and reduces remaining work without retroactively inflating velocity since learning start.

    4.8 CoverageEvent — Superseded Draft for Instructional Coverage

    This section documents the former model, not the current progress contract. The binding correction at the top of this document and Section 3 are authoritative; existing records remain preserved unchanged.

    The teacher confirms separately when a block or concrete goals were actually covered in the course. The event mandatorily references the plan revision, timelineBlockId, and the stable occurrence key. The role is inherited from this bound block occurrence and verified server-side against the revision rather than trusted as free-form client input. Within this occurrence, the event references either:

    • explicit goal IDs,
    • or the complete, already compiled goal scope of a block.

    It contains at least event ID, idempotency key, coveredAt, the separate recordedAt, source, and supersedes in case of a correction. A goal-oriented projection counts each primary goal from a primary occurrence at most once as currently covered. Coverage for revisit, remediation, or assessment documents the execution of that specific course activity, but never increments the primary D(t) curve. Conversely, an earlier primary confirmation does not satisfy a later revisit occurrence. Retries, block confirmation plus individual confirmation, or subsequent corrections therefore do not produce duplicate instructional coverage. This information represents course coverage, not mastery evidence for individual learners.

    A missing CoverageEvent does not by itself prove that a goal was not taught. For forecasts, there is therefore an additional append-only CoverageLedgerAttestation: course, plan revision, completeThroughSlotId, attestedAt, recordedAt, authorized teacher, idempotency key, and supersedes where applicable. It attests solely that all coverage entries known up to that completely elapsed slot have been recorded or explicitly corrected. Without an attestation matching the snapshot, D_b is merely a confirmed lower bound; any coverage forecast or traffic-light color remains unavailable.

    The writer accepts no freely asserted future boundary: the referenced slot must belong to the bound plan and calendar revision, and its local end must already lie in the past at the server-confirmed attestedAt. The server adopts the coverage-stream watermark from an actually existing stream position visible to the writer at that moment; a client can neither fabricate it nor place it in the future. These checks are hard write invariants, not mere UI hints.

    "Matching the snapshot" means: the attestation covers at least the last relevant subject slot completely elapsed by the report cutoff and binds the coverage-stream watermark up to which confirmed and corrected events were verified. New coverage events or corrections after this watermark require a new attestation before a complete forecast becomes available again; older snapshots remain unchanged.

    4.9 Exam Completion from Existing Mastery

    The earlier design featuring a separate AssessmentResultEvent and an independent passed projection is superseded. Exam goals use the exact same outcome state as other learning goals. A successful exam flow writes mastery; a failed attempt leaves mastery unchanged and stores neither the failed attempt nor its score.

    An exam milestone rule reads the mastery of the referenced exam goals directly. It demands no form, version, sitting, or artifact recognition alongside that outcome. Assessment versions describe authored content and plan revisions, not individual outcome variants.

    Scheduling multiple dates for the same learning goal does not alter its functional identity. Existing mastery fulfills the goal commitment even on a retake date; neither actual attendance nor any historical statement about an earlier attempt follows from this. Temporal plan metrics may use existing, reliable mastery event timestamps. Missing timestamp data limits those metrics and creates no additional open exam status.

    4.10 PlanActivityEvent — Confirmed Revisit or Remediation

    Scheduled review or catch-up remediation cannot be derived either from existing mastery or from a class CoverageEvent. A personal append-only PlanActivityEvent therefore records at least:

    • learner, assignment, and occurrence keys,
    • activity kind revisit or remediation,
    • occurredAt, recordedAt, source, and idempotency key,
    • in case of a correction, the superseded event and a rationale.

    This event confirms solely the respective plan activity. It modifies no mastery and does not count primary planning units a second time.

    5. Resolving Clusters into Schedulable Work

    5.1 Binding to the Resolved Scope

    A plan is not created against the unfiltered skill landscape, but against a fully resolved scope comprising:

    • Root Curriculum,
    • jurisdiction or canonical view,
    • school form, stage, and G8/G9 duration model,
    • subject and course profile,
    • Composition View,
    • curriculum release or projection digest.

    This complete Level 2 scope forms the validity context. Within it, planningScope narrows down the concrete planning horizon—for instance to Mathematics in Grade 8. Other subjects, grade levels, or stages of the Personal Curriculum are not removed as a result; they lie explicitly outside this plan.

    For primary blocks, only visible target atomic goals from a referenced cluster or structural node are adopted as countedTargetGoalIds. Alongside these, a remediation block may schedule explicit prerequisiteOnly goals in scheduledGoalIds. Those consume capacity and can continue to block the frontier, but do not count as additional target goal scope of the plan.

    Each revision also declares its planTargetGoalIds: the portion of the Level 2 goal space for which the concrete planning horizon accounts. For every visible Level 2 goal, it is recorded explicitly whether the goal lies within this horizon or outside it together with rationale and follow-up planning. A missing entry is not a valid "later" status.

    5.2 Unambiguous Primary Assignment

    Because canonical goals in a polyhierarchy can sit underneath multiple clusters, each published plan revision enforces:

    • every planTargetGoalId within the horizon has exactly one primary block;
    • overlapping block references are detected at compile time;
    • revisit, assessment, and remediation may reference goals again, but generate no additional progress points;
    • every goal outside the planning horizon receives an explicit disposition such as deferred with a follow-up plan or outsideHorizon with a subject-matter rationale.

    5.3 Prerequisite Edges Remain Authoritative

    The pacing plan prioritizes within functionally valid options. It must never:

    • promote a dated yet locked goal into the frontier,
    • bypass or rewrite requires,
    • infer mastery from reaching a calendar date,
    • alter the Level 3 focus or the active goal outside the rules governing them.

    Class-wide plan validation statically checks whether each prerequisite and its dependent goal are arranged in a chronologically plausible order. It has no knowledge of individual mastery and therefore never claims that a prerequisite is already mastered. Overlapping timeline blocks can be permissible following an explicit check if their internal sequence covers the prerequisite in time.

    Only the PlanAssignment compares this sequence against current individual mastery. Open prerequisites are surfaced there as personal blockers or potential remediation; they do not retroactively invalidate the published class revision.

    6. Planning Units Instead of Repurposing weight

    Existing goal weights express a share of progress and subsequent grading. They are neither time estimates nor effort estimates and must not be reinterpreted as such.

    For the first version, the following transparent rules apply:

    • every eligible target atomic goal receives one plan-local planning unit;
    • clusters never count directly, only via their unambiguous atomic goals;
    • a teacher can assign custom plan-local units to noticeably smaller or larger goals;
    • later calibration must not alter historical plan revisions.

    By default, regular subject-matter atomic goals count. Orientation nodes do not count as subject-matter mastery. Memorization, practice, and exam nodes are included only via an explicit plan role so that their specialized completion semantics do not produce artificial spikes in learning progress.

    Under the one-point approximation, the UI must state clearly that "goals per week" is a coarse metric that depends on goal granularity.

    In the local direct-ID pilot, the planning space is constructed against an immutable snapshot of all projected target atomic goals of the complete personalized subject scope (Level 2); clusters never count. The mutable Cockpit focus (Level 3) does not restrict this selection space. For a concrete learning section, however, only the atomic goals underneath the actually selected goal or cluster count. Its course-plan to-do set consists exclusively of the subset still open at the snapshot with mastery below 0.9. A selected lower-secondary (Sek I) section with 259 atomic goals and 206 mastered goals therefore comprises 53 schedulable goals—even if the personalized subject scope additionally contains upper-secondary (Sek II) goals. Only a dedicated upper-secondary section brings those goals into the concrete plan. Subsequent learning progress does not retroactively alter this planning basis. Rendered target goal counts are rounded to whole goals; rates such as the weekly quota are displayed with at most one decimal place.

    6.1 Comparable Effort for Learning Units and Plan Sections

    Learning units bundle learning goals and subunits into a functionally coherent whole with derived completion. A calendar year or semester can schedule multiple such units; a time period and a functional unit are not automatically identical. The budget conditions below define an additional planning profile. They neither claim an implemented cross-subject budget check or automatic approval nor define the concept or functional completion of a learning unit via available time.

    Without shared calibration, plan-local units from different subject courses are neither directly comparable nor additive into a cross-subject workload. Checking such a budget requires a shared unit of effort—preferably minutes—and a traceable estimate:

    • reference learner profile and assumed entry level,
    • unambiguous goal scope and included learning activities,
    • estimation method, version, and evidence status,
    • expected effort or range together with an evaluation rule.

    Initial acquisition of a canonical goal counts once along the learning path, even when multiple subjects or placements reference it. Review, exams, and additional preparation can consume further time without generating new goal achievement. The one-point approximation from Section 6 remains a progress metric and does not by itself prove manageability. Nor does a cross-subject budget check introduce a cross-subject learning-pace speedometer or offset backlogs across subjects; existing subject-specific status rules remain authoritative.

    7. Capacity, Instructional Weeks, and Buffer

    7.1 No Raw Calendar-Week Arithmetic

    A holiday week has zero capacity for a subject. A week with a canceled double period has less capacity than a regular week. Therefore, the planned curve is based on available subject capacity rather than on the number of elapsed Monday weeks.

    For week w:

    GrossCapacity(w)
  = regular and additional subject minutes

AvailableCapacity(w)
  = GrossCapacity(w)
  - Exclusions(w)

AvailableCapacity(w)
  = primary(w)
  + revisit(w)
  + assessment(w)
  + remediation(w)
  + buffer(w)
  + unallocated(w)

    This equation is a plan invariant: calendar exclusions are deducted exactly once, after which every remaining minute is assigned to at most one plan role. buffer is protected reserve; unallocated is time not yet scheduled, yet not promised as buffer either.

    The UI displays two distinct weekly quotas:

    • Capacity quota: subject periods or minutes in this week,
    • Planned progress: scheduled atomic goals or planning units in this effective instructional week.

    An effective instructional week can be normalized as a capacity fraction. Because referenceWeekMinutes may change across revisions, the normative interval function is piecewise by revision. For the half-open effective revision intervals I_k, the metric-specific formula is:

    W_X(a, b)
  = sum over all k:
      measurableMinutes_X((a, b) intersected with I_k)
      / referenceWeekMinutes_k

    Half an instructional week thus counts as 0.5; a week without subject lessons counts as 0 and does not depress reported learning velocity.

    referenceWeekMinutes is positive and immutable per plan revision. This keeps the display reproducible across timetable or semester changes. Internally, SkillPilot computes entirely in minutes; "effective weeks" is purely a human-friendly presentation unit. Time zone and start-of-week day come from the plan revision, never from the incidental browser locale.

    X denotes the functional denominator of the respective metric, for example all primary content, a milestone workload, or a block. Only when the entire capacity under consideration falls within a single revision may the simplified division by its single referenceWeekMinutes be used. Historical and sliding windows spanning revision boundaries always use W_X; a change in reference capacity alone must never cause a rate jump.

    "Measurable" in each case means the capacity during which plan progress is expected for the metric under consideration. Still-protected buffer, exam time, and organizational time do not belong in the velocity denominator. Once buffer is explicitly released for content, that capacity counts starting from the effective plan revision.

    7.2 Visible Buffer

    Buffer is reserved capacity, not invisible stretching of topic blocks. Sensible combinations include:

    • distributed micro-buffer for cancellations and deeper exploration,
    • end-of-term buffer ahead of exams, report cards, or the end of the school year,
    • dedicated reserve for diagnostics and review.

    The plan breaks down:

    • total available subject capacity,
    • capacity committed to content,
    • protected reserve,
    • buffer already explicitly consumed,
    • remaining buffer in minutes and effective weeks.

    A UI may suggest an initial default, but must not hardcode a buffer percentage as a universally valid pedagogical norm. Zero buffer is permissible, but must be clearly visible as a deliberate, fragile schedule.

    7.3 Total and Subject Budgets of a Plan Section

    The reference workload of learning units scheduled within a cross-subject section is checked against both the overall budget and the binding subject budgets. An adequate overall total does not automatically compensate for an overbooked subject budget. Out-of-class study time counts only if it is explicitly scheduled and declared in the reference profile. Every such budget must satisfy:

    planned learning workload + protected reserve <= available capacity

    Learning workload comprises declared initial instruction, review, exams, and preparation. The same minute must neither be credited in full to multiple subjects nor booked simultaneously as content and reserve. Interdisciplinary activities receive traceable time shares; they double neither the goal scope nor available capacity. A shared reserve replaces a subject buffer only where reallocation is genuinely feasible in practice.

    The budget result and the reliability of its underlying basis are reported separately. Missing effort data yields "not yet evaluable." The estimation basis may, for instance, be provisionally estimated, subject-expert reviewed, or calibrated against matching learner data; each statement remains bound to its reference profile, method, and version. Even an arithmetically fitting reference workload is no guarantee for every individual person. Reserve and deliberately chosen zero reserve remain visible in accordance with Section 7.2.

    Assigning goals to learning units does not replace a concrete chronological schedule: prerequisites within the same unit must be scheduled prior to their use. Detailed temporal validation follows Section 5.3.

    7.4 Reference Workload, Personal Entry Gaps, and Completion

    The reference workload applies under the published entry profile. Personal planning, by contrast, accounts for the stored learning state: already achieved goals reduce open work; missing entry prerequisites surface as additional preparation or potential remediation. Such gaps may exceed the personal budget without retroactively invalidating the reference assignment. Scheduling them follows existing selection rules and expands neither the Personal Curriculum nor the focus automatically. Without scheduled catch-up work, the additional need remains explicitly reported; it must not be treated as already mastered or covered in time.

    A unit's own scope and its explicit completion rule must be distinguished from the complete target set of the Personal Curriculum. Elective rules apply solely to the declared unit scope. An official optional classification removes no goals from the currently binding Personal Curriculum; existing interim selection rules and its progress and completion scope remain intact.

    Completion of a learning unit follows its published goal-type-appropriate rule: mandatory subunits and its own goal obligations are fulfilled. Effective competency encompasses direct results and valid implicit recognition. Dates, instructional duration, and consumed planning units never generate completion. Missing outcome data remains non-evaluable. A completed part stays completed even while a higher-level exam is still open. Mere time scheduling activates no technical access lock. Whether explicit dependencies between whole units— including direct exam attempts—are binding is documented as a separate, still-open product decision in the progression concept.

    8. Planned Curve and Weekly Quota

    For a primary block b, let:

    • P_b be the sum of its planning units,
    • C_b(t) be the usable content capacity of the block up to time t,
    • C_b be the total usable content capacity of the block.

    Then the expected cumulative block progress is:

    S_b(t) = P_b * clamp(C_b(t) / C_b, 0, 1)

    The overall planned curve is the sum across all effective primary blocks. The weekly quota is the difference of the planned curve between the start and end of the week. School holidays, cancellations, and explicit buffer blocks reduce it automatically.

    Linear distribution within a block is initially a planning approximation. Later, teachers can specify explicit intermediate milestones or an internal ordering for goal groups. Without such data, the runtime must not invent a hidden sequence.

    A block with a positive goal set and zero content capacity is invalid. Parallel blocks are permissible only if their combined capacity allocation is not overbooked.

    9. Individual Actual Curve and Learning Velocity

    9.1 Baseline and Newly Achieved Goals

    Let:

    • G be the unambiguous counted atomic goals of the plan,
    • G_0 be the goals already mastered at the personal learning start,
    • p_g be the plan-local units of a goal,
    • a_g be the timestamp of its first completion after learning start for a goal outside the baseline, classified as new or recovered.

    Then:

    B = sum of p_g for g in G_0

A_seit_start(t)
  = sum of p_g for g in (G without G_0) with a_g <= t

A(t)
  = B + A_seit_start(t)

Q(t)
  = sum of p_g for g in G currently still mastered

    The baseline reduces remaining work, but does not inflate the velocity reported since learning start.

    For comparison, an assignment-specific planned curve S_zuordnung(t) is also defined:

    • under classSchedule, it is the unchanged published class curve;
    • under rebaseAtStart, it starts at B and distributes only the remaining units starting from learningStartedAt across personal remaining capacity.

    Thus, a late entry is measured deliberately either against ongoing class progress or against a freshly rebased personal plan.

    In addition, the current mastery balance is computed. If a previously achieved goal later reverts to open, the historical achievement is preserved while the current gap becomes visible. Historical throughput and current readiness are two distinct statements.

    Remaining open work for forecasting is therefore P_gesamt - Q(t), not P_gesamt - A(t). A subsequent downgrade creates no second "newly achieved" goal, yet surfaces the plan workload that is open again today.

    9.2 Since-Start and Recent Velocity

    The user requirement "since learning start" is fulfilled as a stable headline metric:

    v_ist_seit_start
  = A_seit_start(today)
    / W_zuordnung(learningStartedAt, today)

    Forecasting additionally requires a smoothed recent window:

    v_ist_juengst
  = completion units achieved since learning start within window
    / W_zuordnung(windowStart, windowEnd)

    The UI breaks this down separately into new and recovered. Both reduce open plan workload; only new denotes a goal achieved for the very first time in known history.

    The window is defined in completed effective instructional weeks rather than as a rigid number of calendar days. When history is too short, the UI displays "no reliable forecast yet" instead of an unstable traffic-light judgment.

    9.3 Target-vs-Actual Variance and Required Velocity

    CurrentPlanVariance(t) = Q(t) - S_zuordnung(t)

v_soll_bisher
  = (S_zuordnung(t) - S_zuordnung(learningStartedAt))
    / W_zuordnung(learningStartedAt, t)

v_erforderlich_ohne_puffer
  = (P_gesamt - Q(t))
    / W_zuordnung_reg(t, planEnd)

v_erforderlich_mit_puffer
  = (P_gesamt - Q(t))
    / W_zuordnung_mit_puffer(t, planEnd)

    A ratio alone is hard to interpret. As its primary signal, SkillPilot should therefore present projected completion relative to the content end date and the final plan end date:

    • on track: forecast ahead of content end date; buffer remains protected,
    • buffer needed: goal achievable, but only by drawing on reserved capacity,
    • deadline at risk: forecast falls past the final plan end date,
    • not yet evaluable: history or future capacity is insufficient.

    By default, neutral tolerance should correspond roughly to one regular subject instructional week and be configurable in the plan. Exact variance in units and effective weeks always remains visible.

    A(t) and its breakdown into new and recovered serve exclusively for historical throughput and velocity analysis. Current plan status, open work, and completion forecasts rely on Q(t). Consequently, a goal that reopens later does not remain falsely "green" permanently.

    9.4 Cockpit Speedometer

    A compact velocity gauge makes sense as a Cockpit quick view provided it does not conflate pace with deadline risk. It always belongs to an explicitly named plan or milestone scope; a cross-subject "overall speedometer" would not be meaningfully interpretable.

    The next binding milestone is determined deterministically:

    1. first, open milestones that are already overdue, sorted by due date;
    2. otherwise, the earliest open future milestone;
    3. ties on due date break by hard before target, then by stable milestone ID.

    Fulfilled milestones are skipped. If no open milestone exists, the next open primary block completion serves as an explicitly labeled fallback: earliest endDateInclusive, breaking ties by stable block ID. If none exists either, the final plan end date follows. For this purpose, the runtime constructs a synthetic PacingTarget sharing the exact same calculation interface: for the block fallback, activeWorkloadGoalIds is populated from its countedTargetGoalIds, dueDate from endDateInclusive, and capacityAllocationIds from its matching work slices, while releasableBufferAllocationIds remains empty absent an explicit block binding. For the final plan fallback, these are the still-open plan goal IDs, the final plan end date, their remaining work slices, and any reserve still unbound up to that point. The exact same formulas as for a milestone then apply. Upon late entry into a class, an overdue class milestone remains visible in classSchedule mode and is treated as a catch-up situation. rebaseAtStart or a personal plan may replace it only via a newly published, traceable assignment.

    For the selected milestone m, let:

    • G_m be its activeWorkloadGoalIds materialized for this assignment; without an alternatives rule, this matches the bound preparation workload,
    • G_m_open(t) be the subset thereof still open according to the current mastery state,
    • P_m be the sum of plan-local units in G_m,
    • Q_m(t) be the units of goals in G_m currently fulfilled via mastery,
    • C_m_rest(t, dueDate) be exclusively the upcoming content capacity of linked capacity slices whose workItemGoalId lies in G_m_open(t) and that are available up to the due date,
    • C_m_buffer_rest(t, dueDate) be exclusively the buffer slices falling no later than the end of the local due date and explicitly designated as releasable for m.

    Then:

    R_m(t) = P_m - Q_m(t)

W_m_rest(t)
  = sum over k:
      C_m_rest(t, dueDate, intersected with I_k)
      / referenceWeekMinutes_k

W_m_mit_puffer(t)
  = sum over k:
      (C_m_rest(t, dueDate, intersected with I_k)
       + C_m_buffer_rest(t, dueDate, intersected with I_k))
      / referenceWeekMinutes_k

v_soll_m_ohne_puffer(t)
  = R_m(t) / W_m_rest(t)

v_soll_m_mit_puffer(t)
  = R_m(t) / W_m_mit_puffer(t)

v_ist_m
  = completion units from G_m achieved since learning start within observation window
    / W_G_m(windowStart, windowEnd)

    Minutes belonging to parallel subjects, later blocks, or other milestone workloads do not enter W_m_rest. An identical goal prepared jointly may reference the same real slot allocation across both milestone forecasts; it is nevertheless booked only once in the underlying schedule. An assessment or exam milestone without a positive workloadGoalIds scope has a due-date and fulfillment status, but no fabricated pace pointer.

    Recommended visual layout:

    • main pointer: smoothed milestone-specific actual velocity v_ist_m;
    • small dot or thin secondary pointer: value of the most recently completed local week carrying positive G_m capacity;
    • clear target tick: v_soll_m_ohne_puffer, i.e., the pace currently required through the due date without drawing on protected buffer;
    • numeric readout: actual, target, and unit, for example "2.1 / 2.5 goal equivalents per effective week";
    • text status stating forecast date and remaining buffer.

    The last-week figure is computed strictly from the most recently completed local week with positive content capacity allocated to G_m. An ongoing partial week, holiday week, or pure exam/buffer week generates no apparent slump in pace. For the main pointer, the window walks backward across completed local weeks until— by default—four effective week equivalents drawn exclusively from G_m capacity are accumulated. Window length is part of the versioned display policy. The recommended initial policy simultaneously requires those four equivalents as the minimum observation threshold for a colored velocity judgment. Prior to that, instantaneous values and raw figures may be shown, while the speedometer stays gray and explicitly labeled "provisional." This preserves the quick feedback desired from the latest week without turning a single slow week into a red verdict.

    For a subject-independent, intuitive scale, the speedometer can normalize to:

    Tempoindex_m = 100 * v_ist_m / v_soll_m_ohne_puffer

    The target tick then sits at 100, with raw values remaining visible alongside it. If no regular remaining capacity exists, yet releasable buffer remains, this standard index is not computed; instead, the UI displays the explicitly labeled absolute target rate v_soll_m_mit_puffer. The versioned display policy sets a positive lower bound for a renderable target rate and an upper bound for the scale, initially recommended as maxTempoIndex = 200. Values above that render as > 200, while the clamped-free raw value is preserved for diagnostics and tests. If a positive target rate falls below the configured lower bound, the UI shows the absolute rates and "target near zero" rather than an unstable ratio.

    Distinct states govern zero-denominator and boundary cases:

    • R_m = 0: preparation workload achieved; whether the milestone itself is fulfilled continues to be decided exclusively by its separate fulfillment rule;
    • R_m > 0, W_m_rest = 0, and W_m_mit_puffer > 0: achievable only by releasing buffer; the target value is then explicitly labeled v_soll_m_mit_puffer;
    • R_m > 0 and W_m_mit_puffer = 0: no longer achievable under the published plan—not "no planned progress right now";
    • no allocated content capacity in the observation window: no reliable actual velocity;
    • no usable history: raw status and due date remain visible, without a pseudo-precise pointer.

    Actual and target rates thus always operate over the exact same goal set G_m. Completions in other subjects or later blocks remain visible in their respective views, but must not artificially accelerate this speedometer.

    Color zones are computed from due-date and buffer forecasts rather than arbitrary percentage thresholds:

    Zone Meaning
    Green with reliable data, v_ist_m >= v_soll_m_ohne_puffer; due date achievable and buffer remains protected
    Yellow with reliable data, v_soll_m_mit_puffer <= v_ist_m < v_soll_m_ohne_puffer; due date achievable only with buffer
    Red due date already missed while open, structurally unreachable even with buffer, or with reliable data v_ist_m < v_soll_m_mit_puffer
    Gray no positive pace target, goal already achieved, or insufficient reliable velocity data so far

    State precedence is unambiguous: overdueOpen or a post-due currentlyReopened is always Red; structural unreachability despite buffer follows next. Only for a still-future, structurally reachable due date do data maturity and actual pace decide between Gray, Green, and Yellow. A fully prepared assessment milestone that has not yet been passed past its due date therefore displays Red with the label "Preparation complete – proof overdue," even though the pace pointer itself rests because R_m = 0.

    Red must therefore never arise solely from one weak week. When the pointer sits well above target, the neutral wording is "ahead of plan"; the speedometer should not imply that faster is always better. There is no red high-speed zone like on an automobile tachometer.

    The forecast uses Q_m(t), the strictly milestone-bound future capacity including the strictly assigned releasable buffer, and v_ist_m. Given sufficient observation duration and v_ist_m = 0, an open remaining workload is projected as unreachable. Given zero future capacity, this holds regardless of observation duration. A tolerance band may smooth the green–yellow boundary, but must never mask a deadline with deadlinePolicy: hard or the final plan end date.

    Color is never the sole signal: zone, actual/target figures, forecast, and rationale are exposed as visible text as well as in accessible names and descriptions.

    10. Instructional Actual Curve and Class Plan Posture

    10.1 Instructional Coverage

    Instructional coverage uses the same plan-local units, but relies exclusively on explicit CoverageEvent records:

    D(t)
  = sum of primary planning units explicitly confirmed
    as covered up to t

    From this follow an instructional variance D(t) - S(t) and a dedicated coverage velocity. For class views, individual mastery values are shown alongside this as a distribution rather than folded into D(t). Where coverage confirmation is absent, the statement reads "unconfirmed," not necessarily "not taught"; data completeness and the timestamp of the last entry remain visible.

    For school leadership's legitimate question "How well are you on schedule?", this instructional coverage is the primary operational actuals track: it describes which scheduled goals were actually covered in the course. The learning state of the class is a second statement—pedagogically just as important, yet not interchangeable with instructional coverage.

    10.2 One Target-vs-Actual Row per Plan Section

    For each primary timeline block b, let:

    • G_b be its unambiguous countedTargetGoalIds,
    • P_b be the sum of their plan-local units,
    • S_b(t) be the block progress expected up to the reporting cutoff according to Section 8,
    • D_b(t) be the units from G_b confirmed as covered up to that point via valid CoverageEvent records,
    • Q_i,b(t), given complete evidence, be the units from G_b that learner i currently masters at that point according to existing mastery state; given incomplete evidence, the interval from Section 10.3 applies instead,
    • S_i,b(t) be the assignment-specific target value of that same goal set for that person.

    Then:

    Unterrichtsabweichung_b(t) = D_b(t) - S_b(t)

Lernstandsabweichung_i,b(t) = Q_i,b(t) - S_i,b(t)

    Point-valued learning-state variance is permissible only under complete evidence; otherwise, the report displays lower and upper variance bounds.

    Without a matching CoverageLedgerAttestation, Unterrichtsabweichung_b is likewise not a complete point value, but merely the confirmed lower bound of actual variance. The page then labels it, for instance, "at least 7 of 12 confirmed" and must not treat P_b - D_b as reliably remaining instructional work.

    Only when a matching attestation is present are goals not yet confirmed as covered and their future capacity used for an instructional forecast:

    Here d_b is the published forecast horizon of the block occurrence: the earliest binding milestone due date pinning this block, or otherwise the end of the local endDateInclusive. Capacity after d_b must not improve the block forecast.

    R_U_b(t) = P_b - D_b(t)

W_U_reg_b(t)
  = sum over k:
      C_U_b_rest(t, up to d_b, intersected with I_k)
      / referenceWeekMinutes_k

W_U_mit_puffer_b(t)
  = sum over k:
      (C_U_b_rest(t, up to d_b, intersected with I_k)
       + C_U_buffer_b_rest(t, up to d_b, intersected with I_k))
      / referenceWeekMinutes_k

v_U_soll_ohne_puffer_b(t)
  = R_U_b(t) / W_U_reg_b(t)

v_U_soll_mit_puffer_b(t)
  = R_U_b(t) / W_U_mit_puffer_b(t)

v_U_ist_b
  = newly confirmed unique coverage units from G_b within measurement window
    / W_b(windowStart, windowEnd)

    Ratios are formed strictly when the denominator is positive. The exact same explicit zero-denominator cases apply as for the personal speedometer:

    • R_U_b = 0: primary instructional coverage of the block is complete; no required rate;
    • when coverageForecastQuality = exact, R_U_b > 0, W_U_reg_b = 0, and W_U_mit_puffer_b > 0: structurally, only buffer capacity remains; the buffer-free rate stays undefined;
    • when coverageForecastQuality = exact, R_U_b > 0, and W_U_mit_puffer_b = 0: structurally at risk;
    • no positive observation capacity: v_U_ist_b is unknown.

    Given coverageForecastQuality = exact, sufficient minimum observation, and W_U_reg_b > 0, the zone is derived deterministically:

    • v_U_ist_b >= v_U_soll_ohne_puffer_b: on track;
    • v_U_soll_mit_puffer_b <= v_U_ist_b < v_U_soll_ohne_puffer_b: buffer needed;
    • v_U_ist_b < v_U_soll_mit_puffer_b: deadline at risk.

    In the buffer-only case W_U_reg_b = 0 < W_U_mit_puffer_b, given a reliable v_U_ist_b, achieving at least the defined rate v_U_soll_mit_puffer_b means buffer needed, whereas a lower rate means deadline at risk. Absent a reliable actual rate, the UI states factually "buffer capacity only" without a forecast color.

    C_U_b_rest contains exclusively upcoming work slices of not-yet-confirmed goals up to and including d_b; C_U_buffer_b_rest contains exclusively buffer slices falling up to that point whose forecastReservationKey belongs to this block occurrence or to the exact same functional forecast scope. As with learning velocity, both the measurement window and its minimum observation threshold are versioned. Without sufficient observation, the page displays only planned target and confirmed actuals (or their attested variance), without an apparently reliable deadline/buffer forecast. Only with a matching attestation is R_U_b > 0 under zero future capacity including buffer structurally at risk irrespective of the measurement window.

    A simple valid block may initially specify only allocatedMinutes without breaking them down exhaustively into workItemGoalId slices. That does not imply C_U_b_rest = 0. With a matching attestation but incomplete goal binding, coverageForecastQuality is coarse; the snapshot shows planned target and confirmed actuals, but neither an exact goal-adjusted rate nor a color zone. Without a matching attestation, it is unavailable regardless of capacity modeling. exact is permissible only once both coverage up to the cutoff is attested and all content minutes used for the forecast are unambiguously bound to goals. Buffer slices, by contrast, deliberately carry no learning goal; for exact, they must be assigned exclusively and unambiguously to this block or forecast. Only physically absent future subject capacity paired with attested open work justifies the structurally at-risk zero-denominator state.

    For a linear block with a matching attestation, variance can additionally be converted into intuitive effective weeks:

    W_b_gesamt
  = W_b(blockStart, d_b)

planRate_b
  = P_b / W_b_gesamt

UnterrichtsabweichungWochen_b(t)
  = (D_b(t) - S_b(t)) / planRate_b

    The denominator is guaranteed positive by the required positive block capacity and composed piecewise across revision boundaries according to Section 7.1; negative values indicate a backlog. Without a matching attestation, the arithmetic figure represents only a lower bound derived from confirmed coverage; consequently, the plan posture omits any point-precise statement "x weeks ahead of / behind plan."

    The overall header does not sum such block weeks. Instead, it inverts the monotonic overall planned curve continuously composed across revisions. The effective content-week axis used for this is defined piecewise by revision:

    E(t)
  = sum over all revision intervals k effective up to t:
      primaryContentMinutes_k(up to t) / referenceWeekMinutes_k

e_equivalent
  = inverse(S_gesamt_auf_E)(clamp(D(t), 0, P_gesamt))

UnterrichtsabweichungWochenGesamt(t)
  = e_equivalent - E(t)

    S_gesamt_auf_E is the continuous, piecewise linear interpolation across all materialized cumulative (E, S) anchors at completely elapsed primary slot ends and revision boundaries. Anchors sharing an identical E collapse into a single point; for every segment with Delta E > 0, publication requires Delta S > 0. This makes the curve strictly increasing along its content axis and its clamped inverse unambiguous. A reporting timestamp continues to use solely its latest completely elapsed slot anchor. School holidays, buffers, and other intervals without subject instruction do not advance E and therefore create no artificial plateau. Specifically, when D(t) = S(t), e_equivalent = E(t) holds exactly; a backlog D(t) < S(t) can never produce a positive week variance. Thus, even across changing referenceWeekMinutes, parallel blocks, and revision boundaries, exactly one reproducible course-wide statement "x effective subject weeks ahead of or behind plan" is preserved. This point statement is permissible only when a coverage attestation matching the overall course cutoff is present; otherwise, it stays unavailable while the confirmed minimum bound remains visible.

    All figures in a row share the exact same materialized goal set, plan-local units, and projection digest. A whole-cluster target must never be compared against the actuals of a silently narrowed subset. Each row belongs to a primary plan occurrence, not merely to a cluster ID: if the same cluster is scheduled across two blocks, two distinctly labeled rows appear. Multiply referenced goals count solely via the countedTargetGoalIds of their primary block; child percentages are never summed.

    Block status—"on track," "buffer needed," or "deadline at risk"—is forecast from instructional actuals, remaining block capacity, fixed dates, and the buffer assigned to this block. It is not a judgment on the teacher and is never derived from the class mean mastery.

    A linear block target reliably answers how many units of the cluster are scheduled up to today. Without additional authoring metadata, it does not specify which individual atomic goals ought to be due already. If the page is to report that at the individual goal level, the goal requires a published DatedGoalOccurrenceTemplate with dateSemantics: deadline, an explicit sequence, or an intermediate milestone. A dateSemantics: contextEnd derived solely from the block end date is insufficient. In that case, the drilldown accurately states "within this block, no individual target due date" rather than fabricating an atomic sequence. It may still indicate binarily whether such a goal has been confirmed as covered via coverage. Goal-level class learning state uses its own missingness contract: n_goal_mastered / n_goal_evaluable, paired with n_goal_evaluable / n_goal_in_scope / n_roster as well as n_goal_unknown = n_goal_in_scope - n_goal_evaluable and n_goal_excluded = n_roster - n_goal_in_scope. Computing a share against the entire roster that makes unknown or excluded cases look "not mastered" is impermissible. Any atomic target count or overdue traffic light is likewise omitted absent deadline semantics.

    The progress formulas above govern primary rows. Other roles are never artificially converted into primary learning-goal points:

    • revisit and remediation show course-side execution via the referenced block/coverage event and personal participation via PlanActivityEvent records;
    • assessment shows the scheduled slot and existing mastery of the exam learning goals; sitting or attendance history is never fabricated from this;
    • buffer shows reserved, released, and remaining capacity, but neither coverage points nor mastery.

    10.3 Statistical Learning-State Actuals of the Class

    The statistical class row is built from the authorized PlanAssignment records active at the reporting cutoff. Its counter contract is:

    • n_roster: all active members of the bound roster version as of the cutoff;
    • n_in_scope: among those, assignments compatible with this exact plan row under the inclusion policy and digests;
    • n_excluded = n_roster - n_in_scope: transparently excluded cases, for instance due to a not-yet-reconciled revision;
    • n_complete: cases in n_in_scope carrying complete usable evidence;
    • n_classified: cases whose point value or entire evidence interval falls unambiguously behind, within target corridor, or ahead;
    • n_indeterminate = n_in_scope - n_classified: functionally ambiguous cases that cannot be classified uniquely;
    • n_unplannable: subset of n_indeterminate for which no valid personal planned curve exists, such as remaining work with zero personal capacity under rebaseAtStart.

    n_complete and n_classified are distinct subsets of n_in_scope; neither is required to contain the other. Thus, a learner can have complete mastery evidence for class statistics while still not being schedule-classifiable due to a missing personal planned curve. Median and percentiles exclusively use n_complete. The three status shares use n_classified as their denominator and remain undefined when n_classified = 0. n_indeterminate / n_in_scope (including n_unplannable) and n_excluded / n_roster are reported separately. Every rendering states at least n_classified / n_in_scope / n_roster so that an apparently clean distribution never hides its data gaps. Each row displays at least:

    • median and 25th–75th percentile of the mastered share across learners with complete usable evidence,
    • breakdown behind plan / within target corridor / ahead of plan / indeterminate under a versioned tolerance and evidence policy,
    • the aforementioned counters alongside their fixed denominators,
    • count of learners completely blocked on subject-matter grounds, without turning this into a ranking.

    Target tolerance is defined not as a hidden point margin, but as a positive, versioned width h along the subject-capacity axis effective for the assignment. Under classSchedule, the shared class axis is used:

    lower_i,b(t) = S_b_auf_E_klasse(E_klasse(t) - h)
upper_i,b(t) = S_b_auf_E_klasse(E_klasse(t) + h)

    Personal absences shift neither target nor tolerance corridor in this mode. Under rebaseAtStart or a published individual plan revision, by contrast, the corresponding personal planned curve and its personal subject-capacity axis are used:

    lower_i,b(t) = S_i,b_auf_E_i(E_i(t) - h)
upper_i,b(t) = S_i,b_auf_E_i(E_i(t) + h)

    All axis arguments are clamped to the block's start and end. Under complete evidence:

    behind plan            iff Q_i,b(t) < lower_i,b(t)
within target corridor iff lower_i,b(t) <= Q_i,b(t) <= upper_i,b(t)
ahead of plan          iff Q_i,b(t) > upper_i,b(t)

    The three classes are mutually disjoint; indeterminate remains a separate data-quality state. Every class reports both its numerator and denominator.

    In classSchedule mode, S_i,b(t) = S_b(t) holds: baseline and already mastered goals appear in Q_i,b(t), whereas individual absences modify only remaining capacity and forecasts. Under rebaseAtStart, with block baseline B_i,b and personal content capacity C_i,b:

    S_i,b(t)
  = B_i,b
    + (P_b - B_i,b) * clamp(C_i,b(t) / C_i,b, 0, 1)

    When B_i,b = P_b, S_i,b(t) = P_b holds directly without computing a ratio. Conversely, when B_i,b < P_b and C_i,b = 0, the assignment is unplannable for this block absent additional capacity and receives no fabricated planned curve.

    An explicitly published individual plan revision may establish a further diverging personal planned curve, for instance for authorized disadvantage compensation. Doing so does not retroactively alter the class plan. The report surfaces case counts per assignment mode alongside the roster and evidence policies.

    Missing or stale data in a report is unknown, not automatically mastery 0. This is a report availability attribute, neither a third learning-goal state nor an additional recognition decision. Per learner and block, a versioned data freshness policy defines the set K_i,b(t) of goals with a reliably read current mastery state based on fetch timestamp, access authorization, and state version. Existing mastery never expires due to a report's maximum age threshold. Honest bounds follow directly:

    Q_minus_i,b(t)
  = sum of p_g for known, currently mastered g

Q_plus_i,b(t)
  = Q_minus_i,b(t) + sum of p_g for unknown g

    Only when K_i,b = G_b does the point value enter the median and percentiles. Under partial evidence, a target corridor class is assigned strictly if the entire interval falls unambiguously inside it: Q_plus < lower means "behind," Q_minus > upper means "ahead," and lower <= Q_minus <= Q_plus <= upper means "within corridor." Every other case remains indeterminate. Unknown learning state is thus never imputed as 0.

    fullyBlocked_i,b holds strictly given sufficient evidence, open block workload, and zero currently open goals in G_b eligible for the frontier under effective prerequisites. Individual blockers are explained separately rather than equated with complete blockage. Alongside the robust distribution, authorized users retain access to an individual drilldown showing personal target, actuals interval, assignment mode, forecast, blockers, and data quality.

    10.4 CoursePlanStatusSnapshot — Reproducible Read Model

    The single-page view is a derived read model rather than a new source of truth for plans, coverage, or mastery. A reproducible snapshot binds:

    • course and subject, plan revision, and originating plan lineage,
    • asOfInstant, local report time, time zone, and the cutoff rule ("strictly completely elapsed subject slots" or explicitly "end-of-day projection"),
    • curriculum, projection, calendar, and goal-set digests,
    • roster version plus digests of the inclusion, evidence, tolerance, and quantile policies,
    • the AssignmentRevisionBinding entries effective at the cutoff and an assignment-state watermark; for each included assignment, at least baseline digest, comparison mode, active alternative selection, plus digests of any personal calendar adjustment and individual plan revision,
    • ID, digest, completeThroughSlotId, and coverage-stream watermark of the CoverageLedgerAttestation used—or explicitly its absence,
    • processed event-store watermarks for mastery, coverage, coverage attestations, assessments, and plan activities,
    • full scope disposition plus current block and milestone rows,
    • remaining buffer and unchanged revision history,
    • generator, generation timestamp, report contract, and visibility profile.

    By default, the interactive view uses "as of now" and does not yet count a lesson scheduled later on the same day toward the target. An end-of-day projection must carry a visibly distinct label. Saved or exported reports stay bound to their snapshot digest so that subsequent replanning never rewrites an earlier reported plan posture. Watermarks additionally record which events recorded up to that point were known to the snapshot. A backfill recorded later with an older occurredAt therefore never mutates a saved report retroactively; instead, it appears in a new snapshot with a visible data correction notice. Any later change to roster, baseline, assignment mode, calendar, or policy likewise produces a new snapshot; a leadership aggregate binds the digests used for it without thereby exposing personally identifiable input records.

    11. Worked Calculation Example

    A personal plan in rebaseAtStart mode contains twelve unambiguous atomic goals carrying one planning unit each. Together they form the activeWorkloadGoalIds of a learning milestone "Complete scheduled material" using allCurrentlyMastered, due on the final plan end date. Two goals are already mastered at entry.

    • available time: five regular subject weeks,
    • comprising one week of protected end-of-term buffer and four content weeks,
    • remaining work: ten units,
    • regular content time: four weeks,
    • target velocity without buffer: 10 / 4 = 2.5 units per week.

    After two content weeks, four new units have been achieved:

    • planned target so far: five,
    • actuals: four,
    • variance: minus one unit,
    • required across the remaining two content weeks: 6 / 2 = 3 units per week,
    • required when drawing on the buffer week: 6 / 3 = 2 units per week.

    The reliable current statement first reads "one unit behind target." Arithmetically, the milestone-specific raw pace is 2, while the pace needed to preserve the buffer is 3. Were the raw pace observed so far to continue, the plan end date would be reachable only by drawing on the buffer; that is explicitly a scenario calculation, not yet a yellow forecast zone.

    After just two weeks, the speedometer still sits below the recommended minimum observation threshold of four effective weeks: it displays both raw figures, yet remains gray as a velocity verdict and reads "no reliable forecast yet." Red would likewise be impermissible here. Only once sufficient observation has accumulated may that exact same numeric contract deterministically emit a green, yellow, or red zone. A separate thin marker may additionally indicate the value from the most recently completed week.

    12. Interaction with Personal Curriculum, Focus, and Frontier

    Level 2 — Personal Curriculum

    The published plan binds to a resolved Level 2 scope. Modifying Level 2 marks the plan binding as requiring review. Goal IDs that remain valid are preserved; added, removed, or differently projected goals surface in an explicit reconciliation step.

    The declared planningScope is a visible subset of the Personal Curriculum. A pacing plan must silently shrink neither this planning scope nor the Personal Curriculum; goals outside the concrete plan explicitly remain part of other planning horizons.

    Level 3a — Focus

    An active timeline block can prioritize matching focus options or highlight them along the time axis. Confirming the first-party action Activate plan atomically activates the jointly selected personal subject plans, switches on Learn by plan, and immediately adopts the first due, frontier-eligible goal. When the Cockpit is opened later, an explicit idempotent reconcile request performs the exact same step whenever the mode is active without a selected goal. There is no separate manual initial start step.

    An ongoing active goal is never preempted during normal learning flow. However, the learner may deliberately switch to another subject. That explicit foreground write parks the prior goal without modifying mastery, re-evaluates the chosen subject plan, and sets its next due frontier goal together with focus and subject route. Following a confirmed completion, eligible goals from a valid plan carrying a completion anchor take precedence; otherwise, eligible subject plans are ordered deterministically by due date and stable subject identity. Mere passage of a date, a forecast, or a local teacher plan edit triggers no background write.

    Plan mode alters neither Level 2 nor mastery nor requires. Stale or invalid plan bindings are skipped. Whenever no valid stored plan or due frontier-eligible atomic goal exists, no substitute goal is fabricated. While plan mode remains switched on, general autopilot stays suppressed in those cases as well, and blockers are surfaced visibly. The learner can pause plan mode at any time.

    Today's backend planned_goal model remains Level 3 focus. It is neither extended with date fields nor repurposed as a curriculum pacing plan.

    Level 3b — Active Goal and Frontier

    Within the current plan block, strictly genuinely open frontier goals are prioritized. A future date never unlocks a goal. If a scheduled block is unreachable due to open prerequisites, the system displays the blockers and proposes a validated remediation or replanning step.

    Level 4 — Mastery

    Mastery remains global on stable goal IDs. Plan progress is derived from mastery and the bound plan scope; the plan maintains no second copy of mastery.

    13. Replanning

    Replanning first generates a proposal and, once confirmed, creates a new plan revision. Recommended precedence order:

    1. utilize still-unallocated regular capacity,
    2. draw on buffer according to the approved rule,
    3. shift future blocks without violating fixed dates or DAG dependencies,
    4. add extra subject capacity strictly via explicit entry,
    5. adjust functional goal scope strictly through an explicitly authorized change to the Personal Curriculum.

    Never performed automatically:

    • removing mandatory goals or other target goals,
    • bypassing requires,
    • modifying Personal Curriculum or mastery,
    • altering focus or active goal via replanning, forecasting, calendar edits, or an externally owned class plan; the revocable learner-side plan-mode authorization described in Section 12 remains the sole narrowly bounded exception,
    • overwriting the historical planned schedule,
    • shifting binding milestones without an explicit new decision,
    • inferring ability or grading performance from pace.

    14. Curriculum and Projection Changes

    Every published plan revision is bound to curriculum and projection digests. When the curriculum changes, the historical plan is never live-expanded anew.

    Instead:

    • unchanged stable goal IDs can be carried forward,
    • added and removed goal IDs are listed explicitly,
    • splits and merges require an explicit mapping decision,
    • affected blocks are marked needsReconciliation,
    • historical planned and actual curves remain reproducible against the prior revision.

    16. Privacy, Fairness, and Interpretive Boundaries

    • Each learner is compared against their own authorized plan, never against other learners.
    • Individual absences and disadvantage accommodations can be accounted for via private plan or calendar adjustments.
    • Real names and learner IDs stay separated. Today's trainer frontend stores real names strictly locally; introducing server-side class planning must not incidentally erode that boundary.
    • Holding a "school leadership" role does not automatically grant access to named individual records. By default, leadership-summary-v1 is aggregated and contains neither names nor individual absence, accommodation, or blocker reasons. Accessing a named individual drilldown requires a dedicated, purpose-bound capability and is audited.
    • In small courses, aggregation does not guarantee anonymization. Even the aggregated leadership view stays access-controlled and enforces an institutionally configured suppression and release policy. If a valid policy is absent or its minimum cell size is not met, rendering and export fail closed. The default leadership payload omits both assignment modes and n_unplannable; sensitive sub-counters are at most rolled up into n_indeterminate in compliance with policy. Differencing-prone filters across time, goals, or subgroups cannot be freely combined there and are restricted to pre-approved plan rows.
    • A learner's own assignment, achievement, assessment, and plan-activity history along with individual adjustments must be covered by versioned learner data export, re-import, retention, and deletion workflows. A learner export includes neither an externally owned class plan nor course coverage nor other learners' figures; it references the approved revision strictly by ID and digest or via an explicitly portable, non-personal snapshot.
    • Data quality stays visible: imported timestamps, missing history, and overly short measurement windows must never masquerade as precise measurements.
    • The system speaks of learning progress rate or plan variance, never of talent, diligence, or grade predictions.

    17. Validation Rules

    A published plan revision is valid strictly when:

    1. Level 2 context, planningScope, Curriculum, and Composition View are complete and digest-bound;
    2. calendar revision, materialized capacity, time zone, start-of-week day, and positive referenceWeekMinutes are bound;
    3. all block and milestone references resolve in the bound release;
    4. every planTargetGoalId within the horizon is assigned to exactly one primary block and every other scope goal to an explicit disposition;
    5. prerequisiteOnly counts solely as a scheduled support goal, never as additional target goal scope;
    6. block dates are valid, start date does not fall after end date, and every block carries a deterministic minute/slot allocation;
    7. per slot, the sum of primary, revisit, assessment, remediation, buffer, and unallocated partitions available capacity exactly;
    8. every primary block carrying positive goal scope possesses positive content capacity;
    9. school holidays, exclusions, and buffers are never deducted twice;
    10. every milestone separates kind from deadline bindingness, carries a fulfillment rule admissible for that kind, binds assessments together with version and functional mapping, and never masks deadlinePolicy: hard behind tolerance bands;
    11. milestone workload, linked capacity slices, prerequisites, and assessment slots fit together no later than dueDate, and disjoint workloads never double-claim capacity; each work slice belongs to exactly one workItemGoalId, their sum never exceeds the block, and each buffer slice carries at most one revision-wide exclusive forecastReservationKey; co-references are permissible strictly for the exact same functional forecast scope and count the reserve only once;
    12. every entry in the concrete goal list carries a stable occurrence key plus dateSemantics: deadline | contextEnd and a role-appropriate date source, a contextEnd never triggers overdue status, minimum-count groups stay intact as alternatives, and prior to rendering any single speedometer all currently rule-effective completions plus a concrete assignment-specific selection of remaining alternatives are materialized; completion, schedule, readiness, and attempt remain orthogonal status fields with deterministic primary-badge precedence;
    13. static schedule ordering respects requires while individual mastery blockers are checked separately at the assignment level;
    14. fixed dates and confirmed past coverage are never shifted silently;
    15. selecting the next binding milestone is deterministic even in the presence of overdue and identically dated entries;
    16. division by zero, near-zero target rate, zero remaining capacity, v = 0, speedometer overflow, expired plans, and missing measurement data each yield a well-defined state;
    17. on-time, late, overdue, and subsequently reopened milestone states follow unambiguously from disjoint fulfillment episodes, the truth value at the end of the due date, and the current state in the bound plan time zone;
    18. mastery, assessment, plan-activity, coverage, and coverage-attestation events are idempotent and corrections supersede their predecessors in append-only fashion; every coverage event binds a valid block occurrence of its revision along with its server-derived role; every scheduled assessment attempt binds assignment, occurrence, and slot matching assessment ID and version; every attestation references a slot of its bound plan/calendar revision whose end has already elapsed at the server-confirmed attestedAt, plus a server-existing coverage watermark observable at that moment;
    19. a new revision references the prior one unchanged and continues its curves at the effective date without an artificial jump;
    20. every plan-posture row derives target, coverage, and learning state from the exact same materialized goal set and digest;
    21. asOfInstant, time zone, slot cutoff, event-store and assignment-state watermarks, plus the coverage attestation used unambiguously specify whether "as of now" or an end-of-day projection is meant and which backfills and assignment states were already known;
    22. roster, inclusion, quantile, and evidence policies preserve missing data as an unknown interval, compute point statistics strictly under complete evidence, and uphold the contract for n_roster, n_in_scope, n_excluded, n_complete, n_classified, n_indeterminate, and n_unplannable together with their denominators; analogously, a goal-level drilldown breaks out n_goal_mastered, n_goal_evaluable, n_goal_unknown, and n_goal_excluded separately;
    23. an atomic goal lacking a deadline occurrence, explicit sequence, or intermediate milestone is never labeled individually overdue, and a derived contextEnd provides orientation only;
    24. non-primary rows exclusively apply their role-appropriate event or capacity semantics, and their coverage neither increments the primary D(t) curve nor inherits from an earlier primary occurrence;
    25. instructional schedule status and statistical class learning-state actuals stay separately computed and labeled, and forecasts honor minimum observation thresholds;
    26. an exact coverage forecast requires both a CoverageLedgerAttestation matching the cutoff and coverage watermark and exhaustive goal-bound work slices plus exclusively block-/forecast-bound buffer slices strictly up to the published forecast horizon d_b, whereas missing attestations and coarse block minutes never masquerade either as zero coverage/capacity or as a pseudo-precise forecast;
    27. rebaseAtStart handles complete-baseline cases and positive remaining work with zero personal capacity without dividing by zero;
    28. every velocity and capacity denominator spanning revision boundaries uses the piecewise revision function W_X, and overall week variance is never summed across block figures, but computed via the identically composed content-week axis and its defined strictly increasing piecewise linear planned curve and unambiguous inverse;
    29. snapshots and individual drilldowns enforce their visibility profile and required capabilities fail-closed;
    30. a snapshot binds every effective assignment revision alongside baseline, mode, calendar, and policy digests from which its personal and aggregated metrics were produced;
    31. the report discloses the full disposition of the bound planningScope and never emits a green overall status when unresolved or reconciliation-pending goals exist;
    32. course-plan-overview-v1 never silently truncates plan rows, and a print export discloses every appendix with row and page counts on page one;
    33. lacking a valid release/suppression policy or falling below minimum cell size causes a leadership projection to fail closed and withholds assignment modes, sensitive sub-counters, and freely combinable differencing filters.

    18. Testable Acceptance Criteria

    • Two courses schedule the exact same canonical cluster on different dates without mutating curriculum data for one another.
    • School holidays and cancellations accurately reduce weekly capacity and target quotas.
    • Parallel blocks receive strictly their explicitly assigned slots and cannot double-spend available subject capacity.
    • Prior knowledge counts toward the baseline, never toward since-start velocity.
    • Late entry yields the two explicitly distinct planned curves under classSchedule and rebaseAtStart.
    • In classSchedule mode, an individual absence alters the forecast while leaving both the class target value and its tolerance corridor unchanged.
    • A subsequent mastery update does not shift firstEverAchievedAt; a DATA_CORRECTION_VOID transparently adjusts the projection.
    • An import lacking a reliable original timestamp produces no velocity spike; after learning start, it surfaces as a BaselineAdjustment.
    • Instructional coverage and individual mastery produce distinct metrics.
    • Within a snapshot, the text schedule list and the plan-posture table originate from the exact same revision and contain identical blocks, dates, and goal sets.
    • The header accounts for the entire bound scope as scheduled, authorized outside the horizon, or unresolved; even a single unresolved goal blocks a green overall status.
    • A subject lesson scheduled later today does not yet increment "as of now"; that same capacity appears solely in the explicitly labeled end-of-day projection.
    • A block's instructional status never changes merely because the class's statistical mastery distribution shifts.
    • Missing learning-state evidence renders as [Q_minus, Q_plus] and is imputed neither as mastery 0 nor as passed competency; median and percentiles use strictly n_complete, status shares use n_classified, and the UI reports n_classified / n_in_scope / n_roster alongside n_indeterminate and n_excluded.
    • A goal-level class share uses n_goal_mastered / n_goal_evaluable and visibly reports n_goal_unknown plus n_goal_excluded; it never divides blindly by the full roster.
    • A future block reads "not yet due," and an atomic goal lacking its own target due date is never flagged overdue despite a linear cluster target; its derived block end date carries dateSemantics: contextEnd rather than deadline.
    • Two learners in the same course can have differing personal target values solely via rebaseAtStart or an authorized individual revision; a baseline alone never rewrites the target value in classSchedule mode.
    • The report breaks down case counts per assignment mode; fullyBlocked is reported strictly when open workload exists and not a single goal is frontier-eligible.
    • With attested coverage yet an insufficient measurement window, a block shows solely target, confirmed actuals, and required rates without a buffer forecast; attested open work paired with zero future capacity remains immediately structurally at risk.
    • Without a CoverageLedgerAttestation matching up to the reporting cutoff, a block displays D_b strictly as a confirmed lower bound without claiming certain remaining work, rate, buffer traffic light, or deadline forecast. A subsequent attestation produces a new snapshot without mutating the prior one.
    • An attestation pointing to an unrelated or not-yet-elapsed slot—or to a fabricated or then-unobservable coverage watermark—is rejected server-side and cannot subsequently unlock an exact traffic light.
    • A block carrying coarse allocatedMinutes without complete goal work slices renders coverageForecastQuality: coarse with a matching attestation and unavailable otherwise, rather than falsely claiming zero remaining capacity or an exact color zone.
    • Under rebaseAtStart, a complete baseline stays complete even with zero further capacity; positive remaining work with zero personal capacity is reported as unplannable and n_indeterminate rather than classified against the schedule, while complete mastery evidence may still enter the separate learning-state statistics.
    • When regular remaining capacity is zero while exclusively reserved buffer exists, capacity status reads "buffer only." Paired with a reliable actual rate, this deterministically yields buffer needed or deadline at risk; absent an actual rate, no forecast color appears.
    • Course-wide variance in effective weeks comes from inverting the overall planned curve rather than summing parallel block variances; switching revision-bound reference weeks stays reproducible.
    • Along the overall axis, D(t) = S(t) always yields strictly zero week variance, and D(t) < S(t) never turns positive. Fixtures covering the first slot, subject-free plateaus, and revision boundaries satisfy that exact same invariant.
    • Given identical event and capacity traces, changing referenceWeekMinutes alters strictly the capacity fraction belonging to its new revision; since-start, window, milestone, and coverage rates across the revision boundary never jump retroactively.
    • Revisit, remediation, assessment, and buffer rows each display their own respective events or capacity and generate zero extra primary progress points.
    • A coverage event for a revisit occurrence of the same goal does not increment the primary instructional actual curve; conversely, the earlier primary confirmation does not mark the subsequent review as completed.
    • An older CoursePlanStatusSnapshot remains reproducible after replanning, while the current report visibly displays the revision and rationale for change. Because of its event watermarks, a backfill ingested later with an older functional timestamp does not alter the historical snapshot.
    • Subsequent changes to baseline, assignment mode, individual calendar, roster, or tolerance policy leave a stored snapshot untouched; a new snapshot visibly binds the new assignment revision and its digests.
    • By default, leadership-summary-v1 contains strictly aggregated learning-state metrics; named individual drilldowns fail closed absent a purpose-bound capability, and small or differencing-vulnerable groups are protected under the release policy.
    • Absent that release policy, the aggregate itself fails closed; assignment modes and n_unplannable are withheld by default, and impermissible filter combinations cannot reconstruct small cells.
    • Digitally, the single-page view presents all plan rows without requiring page navigation. A multi-page print export discloses scope and labeled appendix on page one rather than silently dropping rows.
    • API retries, block confirmation plus individual confirmation, and coverage corrections never double-count a covered goal.
    • Multiply referenced goals count as primary strictly once; prerequisiteOnly may appear in remediation, never in the target goal counter.
    • Every goal in planningScope carries either a primary or an explicitly outside-horizon disposition.
    • A dated yet functionally locked goal stays outside the frontier.
    • "Abitur Mathematics problems by date" references concrete approved goals or assessments and completes strictly according to its fulfillment rule.
    • If a goal loses its current mastery, a mastery-based milestone reopens without erasing historical throughput.
    • A passed exam learning goal holds mastery. A subsequent failed attempt leaves it unchanged; a functionally legitimate mastery correction triggers re-evaluation of that exact same milestone rule.
    • When initial and retake exam sittings share the same assessment ID, the same mastery state applies. Existing mastery satisfies the functional goal commitment without proving which sitting was attended. Failed attempts and sitting-specific pass states are not stored additionally.
    • The same goal may carry multiple dated occurrences across distinct roles while counting strictly once as primary; "at least n of m" stays rendered as an alternatives group.
    • Under "at least 3 of 10," an individual speedometer computes strictly after a concrete active selection of three goals is materialized; two matching baseline goals plus one open selected goal yield exactly one open unit, not eight.
    • If A, B, and C are initially selected and the previously unselected alternative D is mastered after learning start, the projection rematerializes D plus two open selections, and the speedometer displays two open units instead of three.
    • A capacity slice may support two milestones strictly via the exact same jointly prepared goal work; disjoint workloads fail publication.
    • Across partially overlapping workloads, strictly the slice of the shared goal ID is shared while remaining minutes stay assigned to their respective goals.
    • A single buffer slice cannot improve two functionally distinct block or milestone forecasts; permissible co-references share the exact same forecastReservationKey and increment course-wide reserve only once.
    • Regular or reserved buffer capacity falling after the pinned fixed date or block horizon d_b never improves the corresponding block forecast.
    • Open work lacking allocated capacity ahead of the due date renders as "unreachable"; parallel yet unlinked subject capacity never lowers the target rate.
    • A goal fulfilled strictly after the end of its local due date renders as completedLate, and a subsequent loss of mastery renders as currentlyReopened.
    • A goal open past a genuine deadline while simultaneously missing a prerequisite carries primary badge overdue alongside readinessState: blocked; a mastered goal whose prerequisite is lost later remains primarily done while surfacing the blocker separately. A contextEnd never becomes overdue under any circumstance.
    • Completion ahead of due date, loss prior to due date, and remaining open status yield currentlyReopened with fulfilledAtDue: false; re-fulfillment after the due date yields completedLate. When loss occurs only after a fulfilled due date, currentlyReopened preserves fulfilledAtDue: true.
    • Reviews can be confirmed as personal activity via PlanActivityEvent without constituting an additional learning-goal achievement. Exam goals exclusively use mastery. Course coverage never substitutes for a goal outcome.
    • An open overdue milestone remains the binding reference ahead of any later due date, with ties resolved reproducibly.
    • Buffer is visible across scheduled, consumed, and remaining capacity.
    • Replanning honors requires, fixed dates, milestones, plan end date, and the prior revision. Absent an event, target, actuals, and variance never jump upon switching revisions.
    • Changing a curriculum digest triggers a visible migration requirement rather than silently mutating the plan.
    • A holiday week or ongoing partial week never shifts the weekly pointer artificially.
    • Insufficient history by itself produces "no reliable forecast yet" in both personal and course speedometers without emitting any pace-based green, yellow, or red zone. However, an already overdue deadline as well as positive remaining work under zero future capacity including buffer take precedence and remain immediately visible (for course statements, that remaining work must be attested). No tolerance band ever renders a deadline carrying deadlinePolicy: hard green.
    • Green, Yellow, and Red rely exclusively on milestone-bound content or buffer assigned ahead of the due date; unlinked buffer never improves the zone.
    • A near-zero target rate never causes a pointer spike; values exceeding the display scale receive an overflow label while preserving raw figures unchanged.
    • An unrelated teacher can neither read nor modify plans or coverage; learners never see classmates' figures, and revoking a role terminates access.
    • Starting already in Stage 1, anonymous as well as cross-user learner, history, assessment, import, and export requests fail closed despite today's general permitAll fallback rule; positive tests alone are insufficient.
    • Stale revision writes fail, event retries stay idempotent, and both export and deletion respect the ownership boundaries separating learner plans from class plans.

    19. Current State and Technical Gaps

    The existing Learning Velocity Concept and today's user interface provide a sensible stepping stone, yet do not constitute reliable pacing schedules:

    • ProgressPopover.tsx buckets entries returned by the history route into eight local Monday-starting week buckets for a bar chart alongside five most recently dated entries;
    • LearnerService.getHistory() reads strictly current mastery rows at or above 0.9;
    • Mastery.updatedAt is overwritten both upon creation and on every subsequent entity update, making it unsuitable as an immutable timestamp of first threshold crossing;
    • the history route does not verify by itself whether a historical entry is a schedulable atomic goal;
    • planned_goal stores Level 3 focus rather than a calendar schedule;
    • there is not yet a persistent CoverageEvent, shared class plan revision, or consequently a reproducible CoursePlanStatusSnapshot;
    • today's __ALL__ view inside TrainerView computes an arithmetic mean of mastery while treating missing goal values as 0. It supplies neither a robust distribution nor an evidence coverage rate, assignment-specific target, or leadership-grade data-quality statement; moreover, its mastery cache indexed solely by goal ID is not a reproducible course snapshot model;
    • no persistent assessment-attempt and outcome ledger currently exists: neither failed exam attempts nor attempt ID, raw score, or assessment version are stored durably; passing an exam workflow persists mastery. That mastery also fulfills exam milestones; no separate pass or proof infrastructure is planned, and historical attempt logs cannot be reconstructed from it;
    • trainer class definitions—including real names and student rosters—currently reside strictly in browser storage, whereas mastery and Level 3 focus of linked learners reside server-side per person. No backend ownership and authorization model for shared class plans exists yet;
    • the general first-party SecurityConfig currently protects only selected routes explicitly while falling back to anyRequest().permitAll(). New personally identifiable event, history, plan, or reporting routes must not inherit that fallback rule and require their own fail-closed ownership and capability checks prior to activation.

    Consequently, the production plan-posture page must not fetch arbitrary learner IDs individually from the browser and aggregate them client-side the way today's trainer view does. Course membership, visibility profile, and metric projections are authorized server-side on every request, returning strictly the aggregates or individual records approved for that concrete capability.

    Before going live across devices, a durable class plan therefore requires genuine teacher/class ownership and authorization. A bounded pilot can rely on a browser-local, exportable schedule template free of student rosters and real names while explicitly assigning versioned copies to individual learners. The existing full class export is unsuitable for that purpose; the pilot requires a dedicated, schema-validated format. Even that remains an interim step rather than the target architecture.

    19.2 Personal Learning-Plan Cockpit Pilot

    The counterpart to local teacher planning is an explicitly personal pacing plan bound to the SkillPilot ID. Existing ID-possession boundaries govern it without introducing an additional role or invitation model:

    • Per personalized subject skill landscape, exactly one personal plan carrying its own revision can be stored. It forms part of the learner record and is handled just like the rest of the learning state across export, re-import, retention, and deletion.
    • The teacher's local course plan stays an independent browser working copy. Only the deliberate action Provision in Cockpit writes a standalone copy of its subject schedule to the known SkillPilot ID. Doing so creates no teacher, class, or membership relationship on the server and transmits neither class ID, real names, coverage, attestations, mastery values, nor the learner-specific planning baseline snapshot.
    • The teacher UI resolves learning blocks from the complete schedulable Level 2 subject scope into canonical atomic goal IDs. The server revalidates those IDs against the current personal subject scope. Newly added goal IDs are adopted strictly while still open; goal IDs already present in the personal subject schedule may be retained during a confirmed replacement to preserve plan continuity. Overlaps are deduplicated chronologically. Stale or out-of-subject goal IDs are never silently reinterpreted into a plausible-looking plan.
    • expectedRevision prevents an older local copy from silently overwriting a personal revision modified in the meantime.     • Absent an effective schedule, Learn by plan defaults to off and can be paused by the learner at any time.     • Following a confirmed completion, an eligible candidate from a plan containing the completed goal takes precedence. Thereafter, other valid subject plans are considered deterministically by action urgency and stable subject identity. Exclusively stale or invalid plans, absence of a stored plan, or absence of an eligible due goal never fabricates a substitute goal. Even then, general autopilot stays suppressed while plan mode is switched on.
    • An explicit subject switch may park an incomplete goal without modifying its mastery, revalidates the target subject's revision, Personal Curriculum fingerprint, and prerequisites, and activates strictly a frontier goal genuinely due there.

    The Cockpit presents all subjects inside a shared compact Today / This Week guide. Plan posture follows the Unified Learning-Plan Status Concept: for each subject, the backend tallies across all of its valid plans on the chosen daily or weekly basis and formulates per-subject period and balance statements. The Cockpit renders those texts verbatim without recalculating them locally. Subject plans run in parallel: there is neither a rigid sequence "first Mathematics, then Physics" nor a cross-subject sum. The learner can switch freely among subjects. Subject rows appear in stable subject order and identify the active subject, offering a direct continue or switch action whenever the subject is backed by exactly one valid plan. Plan title, date range, current section, next goal, milestone, buffer, and plan scope remain accessible inside expandable subject details—describing the schedule rather than the plan status. Non-evaluable plans are explicitly flagged as such. During a refresh, prior figures remain visible for orientation while all plan actions are disabled; if an error occurs, both the stale-as-of timestamp and the lock state are displayed explicitly. Subject plans are combined neither into a cross-subject pace pointer nor into a seemingly comparable overall velocity.

    Teacher and learner projections share the exact same cumulative rounding across chronologically ordered learning blocks. Within each block, atomic goals are topologically sorted stably according to their effective prerequisites, while mutually independent goals preserve their editorial order wherever possible. Prior to publication, all learning blocks of the same subject plan are additionally validated jointly against their computed due dates. Upon a cross-block collision, SkillPilot attempts a deterministic repair strictly by reordering atomic goals inside their already selected blocks, accepting solely a schedule whose prerequisites hold strictly across every relevant plan date. That final check applies the exact same cumulative rounding on every relevant weekday as both the teacher and learner views; its computational budget is bounded and plans exceeding it fail closed. Goal assignments, block dates, and parallel subject plans remain untouched. Consequently, both views agree not merely on a goal count, but on the exact concrete goals due up to the cutoff date. Whenever no conflict-free ordering exists within those bounds, publication fails closed.

    20. Staged Implementation

    The stages below document the original architectural blueprint. All coverage/attestation and separate instructional-actuals components within them are superseded by the decision of 21 September 2026 and must not be treated as pending implementation work packages. Stored learning-goal results remain the sole source of goal achievement.

    Every runtime stage is preceded by a dedicated impact and freeze review. Even a single added event in the shared mastery write path can affect error handling, latency, export, deletion, or state semantics of the frozen OpenAI V1 contract. Whenever complete V1 neutrality cannot be demonstrated, an explicit Product Owner exception, unfreeze, or new contract version is required prior to making changes. None of the stages below lifts a review freeze on its own.
