## 2. Learning Workflow

This chapter traces the operational runtime flow from static skill landscapes under `curricula/` to personalized navigation and binary mastery updates.

Inside the backend the workflow is provider-neutral. For ChatGPT ([8](openai-mcp-oauth-learner-session-architecture.en.md#sec-openai-mcp-oauth-session)), a registered OAuth client authenticates the integration while every explicit **Start Learning** (*Lernen starten*) click creates a fresh, absolutely expiring 24-hour `learningSessionId` injected into the launch prompt and passed unchanged to every domain MCP tool call; the permanent SkillPilot ID stays strictly backend-only. The [Visible Session Flow](chatgpt-visible-session-flow.en.md) remains an isolated rollback path, and the Claude lane uses the same state projection while awaiting full end-to-end client acceptance. Shared ChatClient/backend boundaries are governed by [6](provider-neutral-coach-boundary.en.md#sec-provider-neutral-coach-boundary).

!!! abstract "Learning entirely in coach chat"
    **Binding Product Owner decision of 23 November 2026.** Once the general learning scope is configured in the Personal Curriculum, the entire learning path must be possible in coach chat alone. The learning map and Cockpit are optional ways to access learning.

    Autopilot determines only who chooses the next learning goal from the frontier. With Autopilot off, the coach offers the eligible goals in chat and the learner chooses. With Autopilot on, the backend chooses the next goal from that same frontier and the coach guides the learner to it in chat.

    **Acceptance in both settings requires zero mandatory Cockpit visits.** The learner must be able to enter units and nested units, change goals, practise and use flashcards, attempt an assessment directly, return to study before submission, pause and resume, complete the assignment and choose to pursue voluntary study afterwards. Even a single mandatory Cockpit handoff fails this criterion.

<a id="sec-workflow-data-foundation"></a>
### 2.1. Data foundation: landscapes, structure, and filters

Each skill landscape under `curricula/` is a DAG with `contains` (hierarchy/composition) and `requires` (didactic prerequisites). During ongoing cluster-edge migration, runtime navigation derives **effective prerequisites** from both direct `requires` and `requires` inherited via `contains` ancestors, while Personal Curriculum filters narrow the visible learner view without preventing strict prerequisite checks across the structural graph.

<a id="sec-workflow-steps-1-to-3"></a>
### 2.2. Lifecycle steps 1–3: Base Curriculum, Personal Curriculum, and Focus

1. **Base Curriculum (Level 1):** Preceded by stateless catalog navigation, the learner selects one authority-defined root curriculum providing the complete goal universe.
2. **Committed Personal Curriculum (Level 2):** Configured solely in the first-party WebGUI (jurisdiction/canonical view $\rightarrow$ duration model $\rightarrow$ stage $\rightarrow$ subjects $\rightarrow$ per-subject course profile), producing the candidate space for focus, frontier, progress, and teacher course-plan selectors (which read Level 2 independently of mutable Level-3 focus). Progress and completion use the closure of the selected atomic goals over all canonical prerequisites, including those in other subjects.
3. **Focus and Active Goal (Level 3):** - **Level 3a (Focus roots):** Once Level 2 is complete, focus is never empty (defaulting deterministically to the first visible root if unset, while multi-root corridors remain supported for backend/coach plans). - **Level 3b (Active atomic goal):** At most one atomic goal active in the UI/coach loop. - **Current implementation of plan-guided progression:** Confirmed teacher schedule activation atomically replaces included subject schedules, enables plan mode (suppressing generic Autopilot), and selects the first due frontier goal for an unfinished period quota; foreground subject switches park unfinished work without mutating mastery, and post-completion handoffs rank unfinished subject quotas deterministically without ever bypassing `requires` or inventing fallback goals (see [Unified Learning-Plan Status](../didactic/unified-learning-plan-status.en.md) and legacy [Daily Learning Progress](daily-learning-progress.en.md)). - **Focus widening:** Offers backend-published broader ancestors along the visible path to root only after genuine completion of the current scope and learner confirmation.

<a id="sec-workflow-step4"></a>
### 2.3. Step 4: frontier calculation and evaluation modes

The runtime determines the frontier for regular goal selection. It checks the effective prerequisites of open candidates in the selected mode. `requires` describes preparation. What an assessment actually requires and what the learner demonstrated depend on the task, scoring and result. Selection strategies order goals that are already reachable. The proposed unit-by-unit mode using `after` also concerns that ordering. [Frontier selection strategies](../didactic/frontier-goal-selection-strategies-autopilot.md)

**Contract in force, 4/11 November.** The regular frontier uses effective mastery $M_{\mathrm{eff}}=M\cup I$. Alongside explicit results $M$, the set $I$ contains ordinary goals implicitly recognized by their directly owning unit under R1–R5. Such recognition also applies outside that unit. The runtime implements this contract only in part. [Validity rules and calculation](../skill-graph/assessment-gated-completion.md#sec-why-graph-not-enough)

- **Optimistic mode:** Restricts prerequisite blocking to the active focus (computing transitive reachability prior to target masking so hidden intermediate nodes never sever paths), letting learners start inside their chosen scope.
- **Strict mode:** Keeps candidates inside the filtered scope while enforcing global prerequisite closure to surface foundational gaps outside the filter.

For an ordinary requested goal outside the frontier, the runtime identifies missing preparation. Learners who want to try an assessment before preparation can select it at the assessment goal or ask their coach. Both use the assessments the backend offers in the current learning context. Credit requires the corresponding evidence. The automatic completion calculation can already credit an ordinary goal in scope when no open assessment or memory obligation still requires it. [Assessment access](https://github.com/enpasos/skillpilot/blob/main/app/src/utils/levelCompletionApi.ts) · [Current completion calculation](https://github.com/enpasos/skillpilot/blob/main/backend/src/main/java/com/skillpilot/backend/completion/CompletionEvaluator.java)

**Proposal for subject review, 19 November 2026.** For an assessable subject unit, a learner could choose to work through it or demonstrate it directly. Both routes would require the same evidence for the agreed version. Passing one part supplies its own assessment result. Only full qualification for the unit in question would release its own explicitly reviewed *whole* content atoms for later recommendations. A completed child could do this while the parent route remained open. The runtime would have to explain the evidence for this planning decision. The proposal awaits a product decision and runtime implementation. [Unit qualification and planning](../skill-graph/assessment-gated-completion.md#sec-proposal-planning-release)

### 2.4. Step 5: mastery, aggregation, and feedback

Evidence for the active goal follows that goal’s type. The backend stores its outcome as a binary value (`0.0` or `1.0`) for the stable UUID. A passed assessment supplies the result of that assessment. The coach does not record skipped exercises or individual prerequisites as additional completed work.

- **Global persistence and aggregation:** Mastery stays global across view, Level-2, and Level-3 changes. Cluster/scope progress is the weighted share of unique effectively mastered atomic goals (60% means e.g. 6 of 10 goals mastered, never 60% of one goal).
- **Memorization (SRS) goals (binding completion contract; no claim of already complete runtime conformance):** Derive their binary goal outcome from card state: ordinary flashcard practice in Cockpit or the dedicated MCP App component (`Not yet known` / `Known` with back/next navigation) updates SRS scheduling only, whereas **Verified Recall** is a separate unaided coach batch test. Controlled recall across **all required cards** of a fully resolved deck establishes permanent goal mastery, and subsequent SRS due dates do not withdraw established mastery (see [Node Types](../skill-graph/node-types.md)). Since the decision of 23 November 2026 a card enters the box only when its understanding goal is passed or credited, and leaves it for good once its recall is passed. The backend places the due cards of the current unit as a separate step before the next goal in coach chat. Orientation completion markers record finished onboarding dialogue without claiming subject competence.
- **Implementation status (verified 30 October 2026):** `LearnerService` accepts only `0.0` or `1.0` on new general mastery writes and `GoalCard.tsx` exposes binary controls, while `Mastery.java` retains `double` storage with the `>= 0.9` read compatibility threshold for historical rows. Browser SRS sync cannot fabricate verified recall, and signed imports preserve verified card state (see [Completion Pilot](../../dev/assessment-gated-completion-pilot.md)). Whenever mastery changes, unlocked prerequisites, frontier options, and progress summaries recompute immediately.
