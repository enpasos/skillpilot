## 1. Feynman Technique for Learning

<a id="sec-feynman-principle"></a>
### 1.1. Core principle

Explaining an idea in one’s own words shows how one understands it. The coach can start from what the learner already knows, suspects or wants to find out. When the topic is new, the learner gets an accessible introduction and, when helpful, a worked example.

During the conversation the learner can ask a question, try an explanation or ask for a different example. The coach answers questions and explains the point that needs clarification. The learner then has room to explain the idea again in their own words or apply it to a new case. That independent performance can contribute evidence for the goal. <a id="sec-feynman-steps"></a>
### 1.2. Four steps for trying an explanation

1. **Choose an idea.** The learner can bring a question of their own. The coach can also introduce a concept that leads into the learning goal.
2. **Explain it in your own words.** How would you explain the idea to someone encountering it for the first time? An example of your own can help.
3. **Find the point that needs attention.** The coach responds to something the learner has actually said. If speed and acceleration are mixed up, motion at constant speed in a straight line can help distinguish the two.
4. **Clarify together and try again.** The coach explains the missing connection when it is needed. The learner can then revise their own explanation or interpret a similar case independently. The four steps are an option for the conversation. Their order and extent depend on what the learner already understands.

<a id="sec-feynman-benefits"></a>
### 1.3. Benefits and application tips

- A learner’s own explanation reveals which connections they can already make.
- A learner’s question can show which explanation, representation or application would help next.
- An explanation that carries over to a fresh example gives the coach further evidence of understanding. Completion depends on the requirements of the particular goal.
- The conversation can be spoken or written. Learners can also use a sketch or a diagram to show what they mean.
