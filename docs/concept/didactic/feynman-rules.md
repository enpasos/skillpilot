## 1. Feynman Technique for Learning

<a id="sec-feynman-principle"></a>
### 1.1. Core principle

The Feynman Technique, inspired by physicist Richard Feynman's emphasis on deep understanding over rote memorization, is a method to learn and internalize concepts by simplifying and teaching them. As Feynman noted: *"I don't know what's the matter with people: they don't learn by understanding; they learn by some other way—by rote, or something. Their knowledge is so fragile!"*

In SkillPilot, this principle motivates diagnostic dialogue on understanding goals (see [Node Types](../skill-graph/node-types.md)): genuine mastery shows up when a learner can explain and apply an idea independently without leaning on formulaic cues.

<a id="sec-feynman-steps"></a>
### 1.2. Steps

1. **Choose a concept:** Select the topic or learning goal you want to understand. Write down everything you already know about it.
2. **Teach it simply:** Explain the concept in your own words as if teaching it to someone without prior specialist knowledge. Use plain language, avoid hiding behind jargon, and incorporate concrete examples or analogies.
3. **Identify gaps:** Notice where your explanation falters, becomes convoluted, or relies on unverified assumptions. Those points mark the exact knowledge gaps on the skill graph.
4. **Review and simplify:** Return to the source material or prerequisite goals to close those gaps. Refine your explanation until it is clear, concise, and accurate; repeat as needed.

<a id="sec-feynman-benefits"></a>
### 1.3. Benefits and application tips

- Promotes durable comprehension and transfer rather than fragile recall.
- Reveals superficial familiarity early, before building dependent skills on top of it.
- Applies across disciplines — from physics and mathematics to languages and history.
- Strengthens active articulation in written or spoken coaching sessions: explain aloud or in writing without notes, and iterate until the reasoning stands on its own.
