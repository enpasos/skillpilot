# Determine unbounded areas correctly

Let $f(x)=\frac{x-1}{x^3}$ for $x>0$ and $g(x)=\frac1{x^3}$ for $x\neq0$.
Show your calculations and each required limit setup. A total of
**20 points** is available.

**1. Split the area for $f$ (4 points)**

Determine the sign of $f$ for $x\geq\frac12$. Set up a sign-correct
expression for the entire area between its graph and the $x$-axis,
starting at $x=\frac12$ and extending indefinitely to the right.
Every subarea must contribute nonnegatively.

**2. Calculate and interpret the area (10 points)**

Find an antiderivative of $f$. Calculate the bounded and unbounded
subareas, expressing the latter explicitly as a limit of an integral
with a finite upper endpoint. Find the total area and explain why it
is finite despite extending infinitely far.

**3. Account for an interior point outside the domain (4 points)**

Investigate the area between the graph of $g$ and the $x$-axis for
$-1\leq x\leq1$. Express separate nonnegative subareas on the left and
right of $0$ as one-sided limits. Calculate both limits and decide
whether the total area is finite. A common formula for the equal
subareas is sufficient if it explicitly accounts for both sides.

**4. Assess an apparent cancellation (2 points)**

A student observes that, for every $0<\varepsilon<1$,
$\int_{-1}^{-\varepsilon}g(x)\,dx+\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}g(x)\,dx=0$.
He concludes: “The area is therefore zero.” Assess this conclusion
using your separate limits. No special terminology for the symmetric
cutoff is required.
