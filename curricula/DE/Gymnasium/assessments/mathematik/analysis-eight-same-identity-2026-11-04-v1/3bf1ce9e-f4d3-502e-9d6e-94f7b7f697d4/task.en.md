# Use the natural logarithm in integration

Work on the domain $x>0$ for all antiderivatives below.
Show your setup and reasoning. A total of **20 points** is available.

**1. Justify the basic relationship (4 points)**

Use a differentiation relationship to explain why $\ln(x)$ is an
antiderivative of $1/x$. State all antiderivatives of $1/x$ on this
domain and explain the role of the additional constant.

**2. Apply and check the relationship (10 points)**

Find all antiderivatives of $h(x)=3/x+2x$. Check your expression by
differentiation. Use it to calculate $\int_1^{e^2}(3/x+2x)\,dx$ exactly.

**3. Evaluate variable positive bounds (4 points)**

For $a>0$, let $J(a)=\int_a^{ea}1/x\,dx$.
Calculate $J(a)$ and justify why it is independent of $a$.
You may use the logarithm rule $\ln(uv)=\ln(u)+\ln(v)$ for $u,v>0$.

**4. Explain an invalid substitution (2 points)**

Why cannot the integral from $0$ to $1$ simply be evaluated as
$\ln(1)-\ln(0)$, a difference of two real function values?
You are not asked to investigate an improper limit here.
