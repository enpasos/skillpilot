# Reconstruct a quantity from its rate of change

A tank initially contains $20\,\mathrm{m}^3$ of water. For
$0\leq t\leq5$ hours, its **net rate of change** is $r(t)=6-2t$ in
$\mathrm{m}^3/\mathrm h$; negative values mean net outflow.
Show your calculations. A total of **20 points** is available.

**1. Determine the quantity function (6 points)**

Express the water volume $V(t)$ as an integral using the rate and initial
volume. Calculate the complete expression and state its time interval.

**2. Distinguish values from changes (4 points)**

Calculate $V(2)$, $V(5)$, and the change from $t=2$ to $t=5$.
Interpret the sign of the change with its unit. Explain why it is not
the amount of water present at $t=5$.

**3. Interpret the reconstruction (4 points)**

Use the sign of $r$ to justify when the volume increases or decreases.
Hence determine its greatest value in the model interval. Explain why
a negative rate does not mean a negative volume of water.

**4. Use the same idea in a mathematical context (6 points)**

A function $F$ on $[0,4]$ satisfies $F'(x)=2x-4$ and $F(1)=3$.
Reconstruct $F(x)$ using the given reference point $1$, and check both
the derivative and the value at $1$. Interpret what
$\int_0^4(2x-4)\,dx=0$ tells you about $F(0)$ and $F(4)$, and what
it does not imply about the individual function values.
