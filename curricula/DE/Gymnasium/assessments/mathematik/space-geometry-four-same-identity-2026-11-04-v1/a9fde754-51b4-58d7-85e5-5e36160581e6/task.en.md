# Follow a line through the coordinate planes

Consider the line

$$
g:\quad\vec x=\begin{pmatrix}2\\-3\\6\end{pmatrix}
+r\begin{pmatrix}1\\2\\-2\end{pmatrix},\qquad r\in\mathbb R.
$$

The $xy$-plane has equation $z=0$, the $xz$-plane has equation $y=0$,
and the $yz$-plane has equation $x=0$.

Show your setup and calculations. A total of **20 points** is available.

**1. Find and check all three trace points (12 points)**

Find the intersection of $g$ with each of the three coordinate planes.
For each one, state the plane condition used, the line parameter, and the
point. Check each point algebraically against both the original line
equation and the corresponding plane condition.

**2. Interpret the points geometrically (2 points)**

Explain why these are intersection points, also called trace points, of
the line with the coordinate planes. Explain why, in general, a trace
point cannot be found by taking an arbitrary point on $g$ and simply
replacing one coordinate by zero.

**3. Distinguish two special cases (6 points)**

Also consider

$$
h:\quad\vec x=\begin{pmatrix}1\\2\\0\end{pmatrix}
+s\begin{pmatrix}2\\-1\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad s\in\mathbb R,
$$

$$
k:\quad\vec x=\begin{pmatrix}1\\2\\4\end{pmatrix}
+u\begin{pmatrix}2\\-1\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad u\in\mathbb R.
$$

Investigate **only their relationship to the $xy$-plane**. Use the coordinate
condition to justify whether each line has no common point, exactly one,
or infinitely many common points with that plane. In each case, decide
whether a single unique trace point can be given.
