# Prove two statements about even integers

In both problems, $n$ is an arbitrary integer, so $n\in\mathbb Z$. Give general proofs and justify the essential steps. A few numerical examples do not replace a proof. Submit your reasoning as an ordinary answer in the Coach.

## A. Prove an implication by contraposition — 10 points

Prove the following statement:

> If $n^2$ is even, then $n$ is even.

1. State the corresponding contrapositive and prove it generally. Explain the required parity argument; an unsupported assertion of the parity to be proved is not sufficient. (6 points)
2. Explain why your proof of the contrapositive also proves the original implication. Merely naming a proof rule is not sufficient. (4 points)

## B. Prove a statement by an exhaustive case distinction — 10 points

Prove the following statement:

> For every integer $n$, the number $n(n+1)$ is even.

1. Choose suitable cases and explain why they cover all integers. (4 points)
2. Prove the statement in each of your cases and combine the results into a general conclusion. (6 points)

Mathematically equivalent representations and arguments are accepted in both problems. The proof method explicitly requested in each problem must be carried out recognisably; another method on its own does not replace it.

Scoring: 20 points in total; pass from 12 points. The individual points are added into **one** total score. This score is capped at 11 points if the general contrapositive proof in A, a substantive justification of its logical equivalence, or the exhaustive proof by cases in B is entirely absent or conceptually mistaken. An isolated calculation or writing error, a concise valid argument, or a partially successful step does not automatically trigger this cap. Traceable follow-through and partial achievement receive credit.
