# Draw integral functions and infer integrands

Graph A shows the integrand q as the line through (0,2), (2,0), (4,-2).

![Graph A: integrand q](/assets/assessment-materials/mathematik/2a371566-b59c-5e32-98b4-c2e41e2b7280/integrand-line.svg)

1. Define the integral function A with starting point 0. Explain its variable upper limit and positive and negative area contributions. (4 points)
2. Draw A on [0,4] from the q graph. Find A(0), A(2), A(4) using areas and justify increase, decrease and concavity. (6 points)
3. Graph B shows an integral function F with starting point -2. It consists of two smooth parabolic arcs through (-2,0),(-1,1),(0,4) on the left and (0,4),(1,7),(2,8),(3,7),(4,4) on the right; they meet at 0 with a common tangent.

![Graph B: integral function F](/assets/assessment-materials/mathematik/22842d80-de9d-5dad-9819-6ae6e9ca61be/graph-a-antiderivative.svg)

Draw its integrand f from the slopes of F, mark zeros, signs and the corner at 0, and justify the drawing. (6 points)

Submit both drawings; formulas alone do not replace the graphical work.

## Solution

1. $A(x)=\int_0^xq(t)\,dt$ has fixed lower limit 0 and variable upper limit x; signed areas accumulate.

2. A(0)=0, A(2)=2 and A(4)=0. A increases up to 2, decreases afterwards and is concave downward throughout because its derivative q decreases. Draw the parabola with maximum (2,2); the checking formula is $A(x)=2x-x^2/2$.

3. Draw straight segments through (-2,0),(0,4),(2,0),(4,-4). They represent the tangent slopes of F. The integrand is continuous with a corner at 0, positive on (-2,2), zero at -2 and 2, then negative. Checking formulas are $2x+4$ on the left and $4-2x$ on the right.
