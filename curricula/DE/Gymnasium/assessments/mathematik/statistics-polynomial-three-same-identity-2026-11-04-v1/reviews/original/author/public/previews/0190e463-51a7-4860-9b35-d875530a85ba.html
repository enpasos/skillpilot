Check symmetry from polynomial expressions

All functions have domain R\mathbb RR. Consider only symmetry about the y-axis and point symmetry about the origin. Use calculations and expression structure to justify your decisions. Total: 20 points.

1. Three polynomial expressions (9 points)

Referenzlösung: Symmetrie

Die Identitäten sind für alle reellen xxx zu prüfen: y-Achsensymmetrie bedeutet F(−x)=F(x)F(-x)=F(x)F(−x)=F(x), Ursprungssymmetrie F(−x)=−F(x)F(-x)=-F(x)F(−x)=−F(x). Ein einzelnes passendes Wertepaar reicht nicht aus.

f(−x)=2x6−3x4+5=f(x).f(-x)=2x^6-3x^4+5=f(x).f(−x)=2x6−3x4+5=f(x).

fff ist y-achsensymmetrisch. Es kommen nur gerade Exponenten einschließlich 000 vor. Ursprungssymmetrie liegt nicht vor: etwa f(0)=5≠0f(0)=5\ne0f(0)=5=0.

ggg ist punktsymmetrisch zum Ursprung. Alle vorhandenen Potenzen sind ungerade; der konstante Summand ist 000. Y-Achsensymmetrie liegt nicht vor, etwa g(1)=1g(1)=1g(1)=1 und g(−1)=−1g(-1)=-1g(−1)=−1.

h(−x)=x4−x3−2.h(-x)=x^4-x^3-2.h(−x)=x4−x3−2.

Ein rein formaler Vergleich oder passende konkrete Gegenwerte für die jeweils verworfene Identität sind zulässig.

2.

u(x)=x2+2x+1+x2−2x+1=2x2+2.u(x)=x^2+2x+1+x^2-2x+1=2x^2+2.u(x)=x2+2x+1+x2−2x+1=2x2+2.

Nicht verschwindende Koeffizienten sind entscheidend; bloß hingeschriebene Nullsummanden zählen nicht. v(x)=7v(x)=7v(x)=7 ist nur y-achsensymmetrisch, denn 7≠−77\ne-77=−7. z(x)=0z(x)=0z(x)=0 erfüllt beide Identitäten und besitzt beide Symmetrien. Reference solution: symmetry

The identities must hold for all real xxx: y-axis symmetry means F(−x)=F(x)F(-x)=F(x)F(−x)=F(x), and origin symmetry means F(−x)=−F(x)F(-x)=-F(x)F(−x)=−F(x). One matching pair of values is insufficient.

1.

f(−x)=2x6−3x4+5=f(x).f(-x)=2x^6-3x^4+5=f(x).f(−x)=2x6−3x4+5=f(x).

Thus fff has y-axis symmetry: only even exponents, including 0, occur. It lacks origin symmetry; for example, f(0)=5≠0f(0)=5\ne0f(0)=5=0.

g(−x)=x5−4x3+2x=−g(x).g(-x)=x^5-4x^3+2x=-g(x).g(−x)=x5−4x3+2x=−g(x).

Thus ggg has origin symmetry: only odd powers occur and the constant term is zero. It lacks y-axis symmetry: g(1)=1g(1)=1g(1)=1 whereas g(−1)=−1g(-1)=-1g(−1)=−1.

h(−x)=x4−x3−2.h(-x)=x^4-x^3-2.h(−x)=x4−x3−2.

This is neither identically h(x)=x4+x3−2h(x)=x^4+x^3-2h(x)=x4+x3−2 nor −h(x)=−x4−x3+2-h(x)=-x^4-x^3+2−h(x)=−x4−x3+2. Thus hhh has neither symmetry under investigation. Its collected expression has both nonzero even and odd parts. Formal identity comparisons or suitable countervalues for each rejected identity are acceptable.

2.

u(x)=x2+2x+1+x2−2x+1=2x2+2.u(x)=x^2+2x+1+x^2-2x+1=2x^2+2.u(x)=x2+2x+1+x2−2x+1=2x2+2.

The linear terms cancel. Hence u(−x)=u(x)u(-x)=u(x)u(−x)=u(x): it has y-axis symmetry, but not origin symmetry, since u(0)=2u(0)=2u(0)=2. Direct substitution also works: u(−x)=(−x+1)2+(−x−1)2=u(x)u(-x)=(-x+1)^2+(-x-1)^2=u(x)u(−x)=(−x+1)2+(−x−1)2=u(x). Shifted individual squares can therefore combine into an even expression.

3. In a collected polynomial, F(−x)=F(x)F(-x)=F(x)F(−x)=F(x) holds exactly when every coefficient of an odd power is zero. For F(−x)=−F(x)F(-x)=-F(x)F(−x)=−F(x), every coefficient of an even power, including the constant coefficient, must be zero. Nonzero coefficients matter; written zero terms do not change the result. v(x)=7v(x)=7v(x)=7 has y-axis symmetry only, because 7≠−77\ne-77=−7. z(x)=0z(x)=0z(x)=0 satisfies both identities and has both symmetries. A concise equivalent rule is sufficient.

4. Both claims are false:

  • F(x)=x2+xF(x)=x^2+xF(x)=x2+x has degree 2, but F(−x)=x2−xF(-x)=x^2-xF(−x)=x2−x is not identically F(x)F(x)F(x); for example, F(1)=2≠F(−1)=0F(1)=2\ne F(-1)=0F(1)=2=F(−1)=0. An even highest exponent does not exclude lower odd powers.
  • F(x)=x3−xF(x)=x^3-xF(x)=x3−x satisfies F(1)=F(−1)=0F(1)=F(-1)=0F(1)=F(−1)=0, but is not y-axis symmetric: F(2)=6≠F(−2)=−6F(2)=6\ne F(-2)=-6F(2)=6=F(−2)=−6. Two matching function values do not prove an identity for all xxx.
