# Evaluate a survey and plan its follow-up

**Total: 56 points; passing score: 45 points.** A calculator is permitted. Explain your reasoning. Accept equivalent correct methods and traceable follow-through.

All reports, numbers and pilot records in this task are purpose-made fictional material. No real survey is conducted. You process the data and document an example of data recording yourself.

## Material: the published results

A fictional school newsletter publishes this article:

> **Our useful CycleApp doubles the cycling rate!**
>
> Among app users, 66% cycle regularly, compared with only 34% among non-users. Our survey with 250 responses therefore proves that the app makes young people cycle to school more often. The results apply to all 1,000 students at our school.

The publication displays this table:

| Percentage of all students who cycle regularly | With app | Without app |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Percentage | 66% | 34% |

The methods box says: For one week, participation was voluntary through a link shared by the cycling club and the app newsletter. Each person could respond once. The cycling question was: “Our useful app makes young people cycle more often. Did you cycle to school on at least three of the five school days last week?” In this analysis, “regularly” means precisely those three or more days.

A supplementary data sheet contains all 250 responses. A “short journey” is at most 3 km; a “long journey” is more than 3 km.

| Journey | App use | Number of responses | Of these: cycle regularly |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| short | yes | 120 | 96 |
| short | no | 20 | 18 |
| long | yes | 30 | 3 |
| long | no | 80 | 16 |

Across the entire school, 400 students have a short journey and 600 a long journey. There are no data on cycling before app use began, and nobody was randomly assigned to an app group.

## A. Evaluate the report critically (32 points)

1. Select one of the 250 respondents at random. Let $A$ denote app use and $R$ regular cycling. Find $P(R\mid A)$, $P(R\mid\overline A)$ and $P(A\mid R)$ as **proportions in these response data**. State each reference group and explain why the first and third quantities answer different questions. (6 points)
2. Find the four proportions of regular cyclists within the combinations of journey and app use. Compare users and non-users separately for short and long journeys. How does this relate to the ungrouped comparison of 66% with 34%? (6 points)
3. Find the proportion with short journeys in each app group. Use this to explain the reversal. Also make a **purely numerical comparison** in which both groups are mixed with 40% short and 60% long journeys: use their observed subgroup rates to calculate two weighted cycling rates. This comparison does not assume that the subgroups are representative. (6 points)
4. Assess the selection method, sample size and composition. Refer to the access channels, the school's population totals and at least one specific subgroup size. Explain why “250 responses” alone does not justify generalising to all 1,000 students. Confidence intervals and significance tests are not required. (5 points)
5. Examine the cycling question and the heading of the published percentage table. Explain each problem and provide a neutral question and a correct table heading. (5 points)
6. Assess the claimed **causal** app effect. Then write a suitable replacement report in two or three sentences that makes the differing comparisons and the limits of the survey clear. Also assess the reverse claim, “The app demonstrably causes harm.” (4 points)

## B. Plan a follow-up and record example data (24 points)

The school wants to describe cycling during a specified week with five regular school days more accurately. A list of anonymous codes and journey groups for all 1,000 students is available for planning. **100 people are to be selected; this does not guarantee 100 valid responses.**

7. Design an executable selection and procedure plan: population, random selection within journey groups, numbers allocated **proportionately to the group sizes**, common reference period, access to the survey and treatment of non-response. Explain how you avoid merely obtaining cycling-club/app volunteers. (6 points)
8. Define a small recording sheet and codebook. Specify journey group, number of cycling-to-school days and app use during the reference week, and the derived “regular” category. Justify the purpose of the three variables. Word the questions neutrally and explain how missing or invalid responses remain identifiable. (6 points)
