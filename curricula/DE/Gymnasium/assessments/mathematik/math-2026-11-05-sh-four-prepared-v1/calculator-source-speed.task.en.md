Two test runs of a model vehicle are recorded at two checkpoints. The position readings come from a route map; the stopwatch is already running before the first checkpoint. For each run, use only the section between its two recorded checkpoints.

| Run | Position at checkpoint 1 | Position at checkpoint 2 | Stopwatch at checkpoint 1 | Stopwatch at checkpoint 2 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A | 0.030 km | 0.175 km | 12.4 s | 44.1 s |
| B | 28 m | 170 m | 9.5 s | 40.7 s |

Treat the position readings as exact for this task. Each individual stopwatch reading may differ from its actual value by up to 0.1 s because of reading and triggering. The two deviations within a run may have different signs. An ordinary physical or software calculator is required; no particular model is prescribed.

1. Extract the matching position and time readings, including their units, from the source. Explain what each reading refers to: what does a position entry represent, and what does a stopwatch entry represent? Determine the distance actually travelled in metres and the time taken between the checkpoints for both runs. (3 marks)
2. Determine the average speed of each run in m/s and km/h. Independently construct your approach from distance and time, explain the meaning of the quotient, and first compare the values obtained from the recorded data. (3 marks)
3. Actually perform the calculations with your calculator. Record the expressions entered and the displayed results for both speeds; merely describing possible calculator use is insufficient. Check the order of magnitude using an estimate you construct yourself. Also actually carry out a simple cross-check, such as calculating back to the original distance, and record that calculation and its result. Round sensibly only for the final presentation. (3 marks)
4. Decide whether the data support the statement “A was definitely faster”. Account for the stated possible timing deviation at both checkpoints. Check a specific admissible alternative timing scenario or corresponding bounds and justify your conclusion about the comparison. A general error-propagation formula is not required. (3 marks)

Submit the extracted source data, your own approaches, actual calculator inputs and outputs, an estimate, a completed cross-check, and a justified comparison that considers measurement precision. The ordinary pass threshold is at least 10 of 12 marks.
