Two homogeneous materials are available for an insert in a workshop. A completely filled sample of each material is considered. The following model values each refer to the same entire sample; the volumes are already given and need not be calculated geometrically.

| Sample | Mass | Volume |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| A | 450 g | 0.180 L |
| B | 520 g | 260 cm³ |

1. Determine the density of both materials in a suitable common unit of your choice. Construct your approach from mass and volume, perform the necessary unit conversion, and document your calculations. (3 marks)
2. Explain for each material what your density value means as **mass per unit volume**, for example for 1 cm³ or 1 L. A formula with a unit alone is insufficient as an interpretation. Also explain what happens to the mass, volume and density when exactly half of sample A is taken. (3 marks)
3. Justify which material has the greater density even though sample B has the greater total mass. The insert requires exactly 80 cm³ of one material: calculate the required mass for each material and justify which material has the smaller mass at that same volume. (3 marks)

Mathematically equivalent approaches, such as ratio reasoning or unitary methods, are acceptable; no particular formula notation or calculator model is prescribed. An explained, labelled representation may replace a verbal explanation if it clearly shows the same meaning. The ordinary pass threshold is at least 7 of 9 marks. There is no further passing tier.
