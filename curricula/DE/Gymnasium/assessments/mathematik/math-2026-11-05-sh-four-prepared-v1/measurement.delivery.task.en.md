1. Identify the quantity to be measured. Choose a suitable one of the two displayed scales and justify the choice. Calibrate its divisions using the 2-cm reference: state how many millimetres one small division represents. (3 marks)
2. Actually carry out the measurement on the image now: transfer both endpoint marks to the selected scale, independently read both positions, and record them with units. You may enlarge the entire view together and use a paper edge, a transparent ruler solely for alignment, or a conventional image annotation. Determine the strip length from your two readings and document the calculation. Do not use the ruler to claim a physical screen dimension. A description of what one could do does not replace the two actual readings. (4 marks)
3. Record a sensibly rounded measurement with a unit and realistic precision. Justify it using the scale divisions and the clarity of the endpoint marks; describe a concrete possible source of deviation and a suitable way to reduce it. Briefly explain why enlarging everything together makes reading easier but does not produce arbitrarily precise measurements. (3 marks)

Submit the quantity, scale choice, calibration, both endpoint readings, their difference, and the precision explanation. No personal images or data are required. If the fine scale remains unreadable after enlarging the whole view, the attempt must not be graded as a completed measurement; the same PNG must first be supplied legibly. There is no additional passing tier: the ordinary pass threshold is at least 8 of 10 marks.
