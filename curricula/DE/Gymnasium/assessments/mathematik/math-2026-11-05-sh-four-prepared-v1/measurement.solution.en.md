The required quantity is length. Scale II is suitable because it has finer divisions. The 2-cm reference spans 20 small intervals on that scale, so one interval represents 0.1 cm = 1 mm. Scale I only has 1-cm divisions and permits only a coarser estimate at these endpoint marks.

Transfer both endpoint marks to Scale II. The readings are approximately 1.2 cm on the left and 9.5 cm on the right. The strip does not start at zero, so its length is 9.5 cm − 1.2 cm = 8.3 cm = 83 mm. An equivalent, clearly documented reading in millimetres is acceptable.

8.3 cm, to approximately 1 mm, is an appropriate report. A conservative uncertainty of roughly ±1 to ±2 mm is also defensible when both endpoint readings and the display are considered explicitly. The divisions do not justify arbitrarily many decimal places. For example, a slightly tilted transfer line or blurred endpoint marks may change a reading; enlarging the whole image and carefully aligning the transfer reduce this effect. Enlargement preserves the ratio between the model strip and the scale and creates neither physical screen calibration nor new original information. This task demonstrates digital measurement and reading on a calibrated model; it does not observe placement of a measuring tool against a physical object.
