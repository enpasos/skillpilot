# Scoring rubric: Dividing a tetrahedron

There are **24 total points** and exactly one passing threshold: **16 points**. First award points for the mathematical work shown using the criteria below. Then apply the caps to that total if necessary. Only the resulting total determines the outcome. As with other assessments, the only persisted result is mastered/not mastered; points and detailed feedback support AI grading. There is no second passing status or additional qualification record.

1. **a: planes (2 points).** Derives $F:z=0$ from the points (1); justifies and geometrically interprets $E$ and $F$ as distinct parallel planes (1). Reasoning from constant height is as valid as a normal-vector argument.
2. **a: line $g$ (2 points).** Finds $6-t=3$, $t=3$ and $P(6\mid0\mid3)$ (1); justifies and interprets the unique intersection (1).
3. **a: line $h$ (2 points).** Examines the impossible equation $0=3$ or the constant height outside the plane (1); explains the parallel position with no common point (1).
4. **a: line $k$ (2 points).** Shows that $z=3$ holds for every parameter (1); interprets containment and distinguishes it from a parallel line outside the plane (1).
5. **b: $h$ and $k$ (2 points).** Establishes parallel directions (1); justifies that the lines are distinct, hence distinct parallel lines (1).
6. **b: $g$ and $k$ (2 points).** Uses calculation to justify that no common point exists, for example $P\notin k$ (1); also establishes non-parallel directions and explains the false statement using the actual skew position (1).
7. **c: cross-section (4 points).** Finds $U$, $V$ and $W$ with supporting work (1 each); justifies the entire cross-section as the filled triangle, for example using the three intersected edges and parallelism to the base (1). The point $V=P$ already found in a may be reused with a clear reference.
8. **d: whole volume (2 points).** Establishes a suitable base area $48$ and perpendicular height $6$ (1); calculates $V_{\mathrm{whole}}=96\,\mathrm{LU}^3$ with supporting work (1).
9. **d: upper part (2 points).** Justifies the upper solid as a pyramid with base area $12$ and height $3$, or establishes similarity with length factor $\frac12$ (1); uses this to find volume $12\,\mathrm{LU}^3$ (1).
10. **d: remainder and ratio (2 points).** Finds remaining volume $84\,\mathrm{LU}^3$ by a valid partition/subtraction (1); gives the ordered ratio $12:84=1:7$ and interprets it as a comparison of the two solids (1).
11. **e: claim (2 points).** Rejects the claim and explains that cross-sectional/base areas decrease as well as height (1); connects factors $\frac14\cdot\frac12=\frac18$, or justified cubic scaling, to the shares $\frac18$ and $\frac78$ (1). This reasoning may already appear in d.

**Cap protecting the whole competencies.** The total score is capped at **15** if either of the following whole performances is absent from the submission as a whole:

- A calculation-supported, mathematically justified and geometrically interpreted investigation of **different** positional relationships of lines and planes. In this task, distinguishing containment from a parallel line outside the plane, finding the unique intersection and justifying the line comparisons demonstrate this performance. One named case, an isolated intersection calculation or a list of unsupported classifications does not yet demonstrate the whole competency.
- An independent investigation of the **actual volume partition**, correctly identifying the two solids, calculating their volume ratio by a sound method and geometrically explaining/comparing that ratio. The whole volume alone, an unrelated scaling formula or a guessed ratio does not yet demonstrate the whole competency.

This cap is a rule for the ordinary total score. If both performances are absent, the same maximum of 15 applies; caps are not added. The final decision uses only the single threshold of 16. The cap does not require perfect answers to every subpart: what matters is the demonstrated connection between investigation, justification and interpretation for both whole competencies.

**Fair grading.** A local arithmetic, sign or copying error loses the relevant result/accuracy points. Sound setup, correct reasoning and mathematically coherent subsequent work retain credit; one initial error is not repeatedly penalised as new errors. For example, consistently carrying forward a miscopied value does not by itself trigger the cap when both solids have been correctly identified and the underlying method is sound. A fundamentally wrong model such as “half the height is enough to halve the volume” is not merely an arithmetic error. Unsupported answers receive only the credit evidenced under the criteria. Credit may be earned in another subpart through explicit references, but each performance is counted once. Half-points may be awarded when a compound criterion is demonstrably partly met.

Equivalent correct approaches earn equal credit: point/direction checks instead of substitution, complete coordinate-equation comparisons of lines, midpoint reasoning for the section points, similarity instead of recalculating areas, or a correct spatial partition. No extra method is prescribed. A sketch alone does not replace requested computational justification; a correctly justified solution needs no sketch. Grade only reliably legible mathematical evidence, and do not invent a particular error from illegible work. Grade the submitted work conclusively without follow-up questions.
