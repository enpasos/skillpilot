We have $f'(x)=2x$ and $g'(x)=1$. At $P_0$, the tangent slopes are $m_f=0$ and $m_g=1$; at $P_1$, they are $m_f=2$ and $m_g=1$.

Here the smaller angle between the tangents can be found from
$$\tan\alpha=\left|\frac{m_f-m_g}{1+m_fm_g}\right|.$$
At $P_0$, this gives $\tan\alpha_0=1$, hence $\alpha_0=45^\circ$. At $P_1$, it gives $\tan\alpha_1=\tfrac13$, so the acute angle is determined by $\arctan(\tfrac13)$ and has degree measure $\alpha_1\approx18.4349488^\circ\approx18.4^\circ$.

Equivalent methods include a suitable difference of tangent direction angles or the angle between direction vectors $(1,m_f)$ and $(1,m_g)$, selecting the smaller angle.

The intersection angle is the angle of the two tangents **at that particular intersection**, not one global angle for the entire curves. The parabola tangent has slope $0$ at $P_0$ and $2$ at $P_1$, whereas the line tangent has slope $1$ at both points. The graphs therefore meet locally at $45^\circ$ and approximately $18.4^\circ$, respectively. The line joining the two intersection points is not a substitute for the parabola tangent.
