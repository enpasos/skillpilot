# Congruent and scale images of a triangle

Author draft v1 · existing goal ID: 2041f4ec-620d-4a20-9922-6ebf16f8f8fa.
One whole task, 20 points. No content, placement, or source approval.

Use suitable geometry software. The product and command names are your choice.
Set up Cartesian coordinates with the same scale on both axes; one coordinate
unit represents one length unit (LU).

1. Create the triangle with \(A=(1,1)\), \(B=(5,1)\), and \(C=(1,4)\).
   Construct its image under a counterclockwise rotation of \(90^\circ\)
   about \(A\). Label the image points \(A_D,B_D,C_D\).
2. Construct two further images of the original triangle by dilation about
   \(O=(0,0)\): an enlargement with factor \(2\) and a reduction with factor
   \(\frac12\). Clearly identify which image points correspond to \(A,B,C\).
3. Use the software to determine all three side lengths and interior angles
   of the original and its three images. Record the measurements clearly;
   angles to about one decimal place are sufficient. Compare the values and
   justify the following using properties of the transformations:
   - Why is the rotated image congruent to the original?
   - Why are the two dilated images similar, but not congruent, to the original?
   - Why is the right angle at \(A\) preserved in every image?
   Explain which part of this reasoning measurements merely illustrate.

Submit your actual software construction as a legible image or accessible
construction file. Briefly explain the construction actions used and how
they produce the transformations. Computing image points yourself and then
actually creating them in the software is also accepted if you explain this
relationship. A list of final coordinates alone does not replace the software
construction.

Show your own construction and reasoning steps. No particular software,
sequence of clicks, or trigonometric calculation of the angles is required.
