Worked solution: quadratic sieve

1. Idea and mathematical basis

The method seeks small values of Q(x)=x2−NQ(x)=x^2-NQ(x)=x2−N whose prime factors all belong to a small factor base. Such values are smooth over that base. Sieving marks periodically recurring values divisible by each base prime and removes their prime powers. Exponent vectors record the factorizations. Choosing relations with all exponent sums even makes their product a square Y2Y^2Y2. Since Q(x)≡x2(modN)Q(x)\equiv x^2\pmod NQ(x)≡x2(modN), it is also congruent to the square of the product of the corresponding xxx values, namely X2X^2X2.

From X2≡Y2(modN)X^2\equiv Y^2\pmod NX2≡Y2(modN) it follows that

N∣(X2−Y2)=(X−Y)(X+Y).N\mid(X^2-Y^2)=(X-Y)(X+Y).N∣(X2−Y2)=(X−Y)(X+Y).

The factors of NNN may be distributed between these two brackets. Then gcd⁡(X−Y,N)\gcd(X-Y,N)gcd(X−Y,N) or gcd⁡(X+Y,N)\gcd(X+Y,N)gcd(X+Y,N) can yield a divisor ddd with 1<d<N1<d<N1<d<N. Not every square congruence yields such a divisor; the gcd values must be checked.

4. Limits of an individual step

For X=Y=32X=Y=32X=Y=32, the square congruence does hold, but

gcd⁡(0,1649)=1649,gcd⁡(64,1649)=1.\gcd(0,1649)=1649,\qquad\gcd(64,1649)=1.gcd(0,1649)=1649,gcd(64,1649)=1.

Both divisors are trivial. This neither establishes that NNN is prime nor contradicts the method. One needs a different suitable combination or additional smooth relations; if these are insufficient, the sieving interval or factor base can be enlarged. A primality test investigates whether a number is prime (with a strength of conclusion depending on the test); a factorization method seeks a decomposition into nontrivial factors. Claiming that they always return the same kind of result would be false.
