Correct a diagram claim clearly

A school library counts all visits during two school weeks of equal length. A visit is a recorded stay in the library; the same person may visit more than once. The following bar excerpt is published. Its scale denotes visits, and the numbers on the right give the endpoint values.

Visits per school week
           90   100  110  120
           |----|----|----|
Week A     |##### 100
Week B     |############### 120

A headline says: “Week B had three times as many visits as A!” A total of 20 points is available. Use the display to justify your answers and write so another pupil can understand your explanation.

1. Check the statement (6 points)

Read both visit counts. Calculate their absolute difference and the percentage increase relative to week A. Use these to assess the headline.

2. Explain and correct the misconception (6 points)

Explain how the displayed bar lengths can suggest “three times as many”. Refer to where the scale starts. Rewrite the headline and add a short, clear, mathematically correct explanation. Also explain why the visit counts do not imply that 120 different people visited the library in week B.

3. Propose a clearer representation (8 points)

Propose a concrete alternative representation of the same data. Implement it as a labelled diagram or a value table with an appropriate explanation. Show which scale, labels and information prevent the original misconception. Keep the original visit counts unchanged. Justify why your representation makes the comparison clearer.
