# Check chart claims: heights and time intervals

DRAFT / needs_review · CC-BY-4.0 · SkillPilot

## Task

The data and chart drafts are invented for this task. A calculator is allowed. Supplied definitions: comparison factor = larger value ÷ smaller value; average rate of change = volume change ÷ elapsed time. “Three times as much” means a comparison factor of 3.

**Case A – compare stored volumes:**

![Column chart with volume values A and B; the visible volume axis starts at 30 litres.](/assets/assessment-materials/mathematik/c76fae5e-70e6-5f20-9abe-e9e790d95324/truncated/c76fae5e-70e6-5f20-9abe-e9e790d95324.png)

1. Check: “B contains three times as much water as A because its visible column is three times as tall.” Support your judgement with the actual values and a suitable calculation.
2. Explain exactly how this axis produces that impression. Does this make every cropped axis unacceptable? Justify with reference to the intended comparison.

**Case B – compare rates of change:**

![Chart draft: volume in litres, time marks 0, 1 and 5 hours at three equally spaced positions.](/assets/assessment-materials/mathematik/c76fae5e-70e6-5f20-9abe-e9e790d95324/unequal-time/c76fae5e-70e6-5f20-9abe-e9e790d95324.png)

3. Check: “The volume grows equally fast during 0–1 h and 1–5 h because the drawn line segments rise equally steeply.” Calculate both rates, including units.
4. Identify the specific representational mechanism and explain why it does not support the rate claim.

## Evaluation material: release only after submission

A: The claim about the water volumes is false. A contains 40 L and B 60 L; 60/40 = 1.5, so B contains 1.5 times as much, or 20 L more. The visible heights represent 40−30 = 10 and 60−30 = 30 scale-litres. Their ratio is 3 because the axis starts at 30 L rather than zero; this is not the ratio of the full volumes. A clearly labelled cropped axis may help show small differences, but its visible height ratio must not be treated as a full-volume ratio. The chart alone does not establish deceptive intent.

B: The equal-rate claim is false. The rates are (20−10)/1 = 10 L/h and (30−20)/4 = 2.5 L/h. The first drawn interval represents 1 h and the second 4 h, although they have equal widths. Equal drawn rises therefore cover different elapsed times and imply different rates. Equal increases of 10 L are real; equal rates are not.

## Analytic rubric

- **claims-a-evidence** (4): A: supported numerical judgement: 1 point each for rejects triple claim; reads 40 L and 60 L; comparison 60/40 = 1.5 or equivalent evidence; correct replacement statement with unit/reference quantity. Subject-correct equivalents receive equal credit.
- **claims-a-distortion** (4): A: specific axis mechanism: 1 point each for identifies axis starting at 30 L; identifies visible differences 10 and 30; distinguishes their ratio 3 from the volume ratio; cropping may suit a purpose but cannot license an uncritical height-ratio comparison. Subject-correct equivalents receive equal credit.
- **claims-b-evidence** (4): B: both rates as numerical evidence: 1 point each for rejects equal-rate claim; first rate 10 L/h; second rate 2.5 L/h; calculation uses 1 h and 4 h with the appropriate volume change. Subject-correct equivalents receive equal credit.
- **claims-b-distortion** (4): B: time-spacing mechanism: 1 point each for names actual intervals 0–1 and 1–5; explains equal drawing widths despite 1 h and 4 h; distinguishes equal 10 L increases from equal rates; clearly links the mechanism to the false visual slope inference. Subject-correct equivalents receive equal credit.

Maximum: 16; passing: 13.
