# Explain chart misreadings and propose clearer displays

DRAFT / needs_review · CC-BY-4.0 · SkillPilot

## Task

All data are invented. A calculator is allowed. Supplied definitions: comparison factor = larger value ÷ smaller value; proportion = part count ÷ group size; percentage = proportion × 100. “Three times as much” means factor 3. For each case, submit either a sketch or a display specification detailed enough for someone to draw without asking you for missing information.

**Case A – the impression of column heights:**

![Column chart with volume values A and B; the visible volume axis starts at 30 litres.](/assets/assessment-materials/mathematik/c76fae5e-70e6-5f20-9abe-e9e790d95324/truncated/c76fae5e-70e6-5f20-9abe-e9e790d95324.png)

1. A younger student says: “B has three times as much water; its column is three times as tall.” Explain clearly what she is confusing and correct the statement with numerical evidence.
2. Propose a clearer display for comparing the full water volumes. Specify the values, axes or columns, units and relevant scale starting point. Explain why your display makes the confusion less likely.

**Case B – count and proportion:**

![Count chart for participation in two groups; their group sizes appear below A and B.](/assets/assessment-materials/mathematik/57bf531d-0920-5424-bef4-f704f5462477/participation/57bf531d-0920-5424-bef4-f704f5462477.png)

3. Group A has 40 children in total and group B has 60. A student says: “27 is more than 24, so a larger proportion participates in B.” Explain the confusion, calculate both proportions and state a correct conclusion.
4. Propose a clearer display for comparing proportions. Preserve the information about participant counts and group sizes. Specify labels and the comparison base, and explain the benefit.

## Evaluation material: release only after submission

A: She confuses the visible heights above 30 L with the full volumes. The visible heights 10 and 30 have ratio 3, but the data are 40 L and 60 L, giving 60/40 = 1.5. B contains 1.5 times as much as A, or 20 L more. One clearer display uses equal-width columns A=40 L and B=60 L on a common linear volume axis starting at 0 L, for example 0–70 L in steps of 10 L. The category axis names A and B and direct labels show 40 L and 60 L. Full heights then represent full quantities. A labelled table with container, volume and an explicit comparison factor is also suitable. Cropped axes are not generally forbidden; a zero baseline makes this full-quantity ratio comparison clearer.

Possible solution sketch, evaluation material only:

![Column chart of the same stored water volumes with its volume axis starting at zero litres.](/assets/assessment-materials/mathematik/c76fae5e-70e6-5f20-9abe-e9e790d95324/zero-solution/c76fae5e-70e6-5f20-9abe-e9e790d95324.png)

B: He compares absolute counts while making a claim about proportions. A has 24/40 = 0.60 = 60%; B has 27/60 = 0.45 = 45%. B has 3 more participants, but A has the higher proportion, by 15 percentage points. The percentage-point statement is optional. One clearer display uses equal-width percentage bars A=60% and B=45% on a common, uniformly divided 0–100% axis, labelled “A: 24 of 40” and “B: 27 of 60”. Equal proportions then have equal lengths despite different group sizes, and the counts and bases remain visible. Equally long 100%-strips or a fully labelled table containing counts, group sizes and proportions are valid alternatives. An unchanged count chart alone does not resolve the confusion.

## Analytic rubric

Only the following subject-matter essentials are scored. Each criterion earns 0, 1 or 2 points: 2 means a minimally adequate, independently visible core performance; 1 means a recognizable but incomplete partial performance; 0 means no defensible performance on that criterion. Criteria are scored separately, without offsetting one against another. The passing threshold equals the total of all essential points because the existing runtime contract checks only the total score.

Full essential credit requires neither the reference wording nor a prescribed calculation method, chart type, polished sketch, formal interval notation or repeatedly written units. Subject-correct clear explanations, calculations, understandable ratio/change comparisons and suitable tables receive equal credit. Implicit information counts when unambiguous. An isolated transcription or arithmetic slip does not reduce essential credit when correct reading, reference base, method and contextual conclusion are unambiguous in the rest of the answer and the slip does not determine an essential decision. An unresolved contradiction of a criterion’s core claim prevents 2 points on that criterion; no hidden additional passing veto is introduced.

Supplementary calculations and details receive feedback but no compensation points. Partial credit remains useful feedback but does not by itself pass this assessment identity. These tasks introduce no new learner-result or repetition semantics.

- **correction-a-correct** (2): A: explain visible height versus full quantity. 2: Clearly explains the confusion of heights above 30 L with full quantities and corrects it using suitable numerical evidence from 40/60: 1.5 times, not three times. Equivalent evidence such as 60 not equalling 3 times 40 is accepted. 1: The error, numbers or correction are partly correct, but their explanatory connection remains incomplete. 0: No defensible explanation/correction, or the false quantity inference is retained.

- **correction-a-alternative** (2): A: specify a suitable alternative. 2: Specifies an implementable, correctly labelled alternative for full quantities preserving A=40 L and B=60 L, for example a common linear zero base or a clear table with volumes and an explicit ratio reference. Axis upper limit, step size, wording and drawing polish are unrestricted provided the comparison and values remain clear. 1: A basically suitable change is recognizable, but its necessary base, data or labels remain unclear. 0: No suitable alternative display; mere repetition of the original chart or a number list without an identifiable comparison base.

- **correction-a-reason** (2): A: justify the chosen display’s benefit. 2: Explains for the proposed alternative why full quantities or their reference ratio are now compared rather than differences from 30 L. The reason may appear in the earlier explanation or labels if it clearly connects this proposal to the clearer comparison. 1: A benefit such as clearer layout is suggested, but the link between the chosen display and full-quantity base is still missing. 0: No reason for the proposal, or a subject-incorrect reason for its clarity.

- **correction-b-correct** (2): B: explain and correct count versus proportion. 2: Explains the different group sizes as reference bases and clearly corrects the claim: B has more participants in absolute terms, A the larger proportion, supported by 24/40 = 60% versus 27/60 = 45% or equivalent proportion comparisons. Percentage points are not required. 1: Recognizes the distinction or partly calculates the proportions but does not yet connect them into a defensible correction. 0: No defensible reference base, or the count-based conclusion remains as the proportion judgement.

- **correction-b-alternative** (2): B: specify a suitable proportion comparison. 2: Proposes an implementable display on a common proportion base, retaining 60% and 45% or equivalent proportions together with participant counts and group sizes: for example percentage bars, equal-length 100%-strips or a labelled count/group-size/proportion table. No chart type is prescribed. 1: A proportion display is recognizably intended, but the common base or a necessary piece of information remains unclear. 0: No suitable proportion comparison; an unchanged count chart earns no essential credit here solely for copied data.

- **correction-b-reason** (2): B: explain the benefit with different group sizes. 2: Explains for the proposed alternative that a common proportion base makes different group sizes comparable while preserving the count/proportion distinction. An already unambiguous explanation need not be repeated; drawing instructions alone are not an explanation. 1: A benefit is named, but the connection to reference bases and unequal group sizes remains incomplete. 0: No justification for the proposed proportion comparison, or only the claim that a larger count means a larger proportion.

Maximum: 12; passing: 12.
