1. Bias 5 V; at 40 µA: 6 V; at 60 µA: 4 V (2 points). A small ±10 µA base-current change controls ±1 mA collector-current change here; energy comes from the 10 V supply, not newly created in the base branch (2 points). Increasing base current increases the collector-resistor drop and decreases output voltage: phase inversion of this common-emitter circuit (1 point), not every amplifier.
2. Linear extrapolation gives I_B = −10 to 110 µA and U_C = 11 to −1 V (1 point). Cutoff at 10 V and saturation at 0.2 V instead limit the two sides (2 points). The waveform clips/distorts and constant gain no longer applies (1 point).
3. At 80 µA, U_C is 2 V: only 1.8 V to the lower limit but 8 V upwards (1 point). ±30 µA would demand 5 to −1 V in the active model and clips at the lower limit (1 point). Shift bias to 50 µA: 20 to 80 µA yields 8 to 2 V without clipping (1 point). A justified smaller amplitude is acceptable only if the original amplitude requirement is explicitly relinquished.
