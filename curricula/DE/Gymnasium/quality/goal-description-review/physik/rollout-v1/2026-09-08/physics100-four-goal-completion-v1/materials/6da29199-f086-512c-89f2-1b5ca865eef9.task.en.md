**Material M1 (simplified physical account):** The early universe produced mainly hydrogen and helium with small amounts of other light nuclei; carbon, oxygen and gold were not significant components of that original mixture. Under suitable evolutionary conditions, stellar fusion can produce carbon and oxygen, among other elements. Nuclei well beyond the iron region can form through processes including neutron capture in various astrophysical environments, such as evolved stars or ejected neutron-rich matter from neutron-star mergers. No individual star necessarily produces every element.

**Material M2 (matter cycle):** enriched outer stellar layers or ejected material → mixing with interstellar gas → a new gas cloud → a new star and possible planetary system. Arrows indicate matter transport. An element can occur in a later-formed body without having formed there. M1/M2 are supplied teaching materials, not an exhaustive list of nucleosynthesis routes.

A claim says: “We find carbon and gold in a young planetary system. Therefore its central star has already made both itself by fusing hydrogen; their co-occurrence suffices to prove this.”

1. Reconstruct the claim's premise and conclusion. Separately assess the proposed formation process and the inference from location of detection to location of formation, using M1 and M2. (5 points)
2. Give a material-supported alternative explanation for both elements and explicitly assign nuclear production and matter transfer to distinct steps. (4 points)
3. Assess whether M1/M2 determine which individual earlier object produced the detected gold. State a limitation and one additional kind of observation that could further constrain its origin without promising uniqueness on its own. (3 points)
