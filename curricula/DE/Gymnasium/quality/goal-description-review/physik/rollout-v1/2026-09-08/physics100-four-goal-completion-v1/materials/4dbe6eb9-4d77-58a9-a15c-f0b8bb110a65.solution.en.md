1. One assignment is A→red and B→blue; the combiner sends both into one fibre. The receiving end uses the beam splitter, a matching filter in each branch and separate receivers. Interchanging channel labels is equivalent. Complete independently constructed structure: 3 points; explanation of distinguishability and filtering: 2 points.
2. Common path: red only; blue only; red and blue simultaneously; no signal light. After the red filter: 1,0,1,0; after the blue filter: 0,1,1,0. Common states: 2 points; recovery and explanation of simultaneous transmission in the third slot: 2 points. Components present simultaneously remain distinguishable by spectral band.
3. Both red signals appear together in the red receiver branch, while the ideal blue filter blocks both. This superposition alone does not uniquely recover the original channel assignment: with equal intensities, A=1,B=0 and A=0,B=1 produce the same red level. This does not assert that other additional coding methods are impossible. Missing distinguishability: 2 points. A filter that incompletely blocks the other band leaks its signal and causes crosstalk: 1 point.
