**Material: three deliberately simplified evolutionary models.** All masses are in solar masses. The rows are supplied model cases, not a universal classification by initial mass alone. Lost envelope mass, total mass remaining before collapse and later compact-remnant mass differ.

| Case | Initial mass | Modelled mass lost before the end stage | Additional supplied evolutionary information |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| A |1.0|0.4|The remaining 0.6 is an electron-degeneracy-supported C/O core with no further collapse.|
| B |18|8|Within the remaining total mass 10, an inner core forms the collapsing region. The explosion model ejects outer matter and predicts a stable neutron-rich compact remnant of 1.6.|
| C |18|2|Within the remaining total mass 16, a larger inner region collapses; fallback leads to a compact remnant of 5.0 in the model, above its assumed maximum stable neutron-star mass of 2.3.|

The 2.3 threshold is an assumption of this model only. Core masses or universal end states must not be read from the supplied initial masses alone. For B/C, later mass ejection is already included in the additional information; it must not be subtracted again from the compact remnant.

1. Calculate total mass remaining after the stated early loss in each case and distinguish it from later core/remnant mass. (3 points)
2. Predict a possible model-consistent end state for A, B and C: white dwarf, neutron star or black hole. Justify each from the evolutionary information and explain why B and C can end differently despite equal initial mass. (5 points)
3. A variant of C predicts only a remnant-mass range 2.1–2.5 with the same assumed stability threshold. State the permissible conditional prediction. Name additional model information that could constrain the choice and explain why 2.3 is not a general initial-mass threshold. (4 points)
