**Context:**

A project group investigates sports and traffic situations using physical models and evaluates protective measures.

**Material 1 (sports projectile):**

- Launch speed $v_0 = 14.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$
- Launch angle in variant A: $30^\circ$
- Launch angle in variant B: $40^\circ$
- Launch and landing points are at the same height; air resistance is neglected.
- Gravitational acceleration: $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

**Material 2 (traffic):**

- When a stationary obstacle is noticed, the vehicle is still 40 m away from it.
- Vehicle speed $v = 70\,\mathrm{km/h}$
- Reaction time $t_R = 0.90\,\mathrm{s}$; the speed remains constant during this interval.
- Subsequently, constant deceleration of magnitude $a_B = 7.0\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ until rest
- Idealised straight-line braking model; no evasive manoeuvre

**Material 3 (impact loading):**

- Mass $m = 75\,\mathrm{kg}$
- Magnitude of the velocity change $|\Delta v| = 13.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ when slowing to rest
- Variant 1: stopping time 0.05 s
- Variant 2: stopping time extended to 0.15 s through deformation
- Both variants describe the same mass and the same momentum change in an idealised impact model.

**Tasks:**

1. Calculate the projectile ranges for variants A and B and justify which setting is more suitable for a greater range. (6 marks)
2. Calculate the reaction distance, braking distance and total stopping distance from Material 2. Evaluate whether the initial distance of 40 m to the stationary obstacle is sufficient to stop before it. (7 marks)
3. Calculate the magnitude of the average force in both impact variants from Material 3 and evaluate the reduction in average force as a modelled protective effect. (6 marks)
4. Give two physically justified suggestions for improvement: one for the sports situation and one for the traffic situation. (6 marks)
