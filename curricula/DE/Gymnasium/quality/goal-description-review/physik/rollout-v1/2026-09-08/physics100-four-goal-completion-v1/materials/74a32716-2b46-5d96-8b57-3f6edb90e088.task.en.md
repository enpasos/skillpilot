Material: A simplified thermal-reactor model has these functional connections: In the fuel, slow neutrons trigger fission, producing heat and fast neutrons. The moderator slows neutrons by collisions without predominantly absorbing them. Movable control rods absorb neutrons. Coolant takes heat from the fuel to a heat exchanger; a pump maintains coolant flow. Separately, heat from the exchanger drives a steam circuit with a turbine/generator. Passive safety systems and the variety of real reactor designs are omitted. Radioactive decay may still produce heat after the chain reaction stops. This is a paper task, not construction or operating guidance.

1. Use the stated connections to explain how fuel, moderator, control rods and coolant together allow controlled energy release and heat removal. Distinguish neutron control from heat transport. (6 points)
2. Compare inserting the control rods further with failure of the coolant pump at unchanged rod position. Predict the immediately affected function in each case and justify why the changes are not equivalent. (4 points)
3. Evaluate: “Once the chain reaction stops, heat removal is immediately unnecessary.” Use the supplied information and state one limitation of the simplified diagram. (2 points)
