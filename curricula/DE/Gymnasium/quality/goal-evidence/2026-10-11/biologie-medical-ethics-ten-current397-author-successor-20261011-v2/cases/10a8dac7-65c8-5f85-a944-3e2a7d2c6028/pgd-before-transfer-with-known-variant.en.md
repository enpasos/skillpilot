# pgd-before-transfer-with-known-variant

Authored teaching case using entirely fictional data. No learner observation, actual execution, clinical or legal recommendation. Cases illustrate possible evidence; they do not impose an additional learner task quota.

Classroom model: Fertilization outside the body is followed by development to a suitable embryo stage. A few suitable cells are tested before possible transfer for known familial variantV. Testing does not change V. The finding concerns that variant and guarantees neither complete health nor pregnancy. Supplied perspectives concern avoiding suffering and autonomy, versus protective interests of embryos and social selection or expectation pressure. Legal conditions remain unresolved.

Explain PGD in correct temporal sequence and its claim limits. Explain both ethical tensions using the example and identify an unresolved point. No particular value judgment is required.

Fresh variation: A laboratory claims to exclude every genetic disease despite testing only V. Explain methodological and ethical objections.

## Model response and scoring

Fertilization and early development precede sampling and genetic examination before possible transfer. PGD provides information on V rather than gene correction. Avoiding suffering may support testing, whereas embryo protection and selection pressure challenge particular choices; autonomy requires voluntary informed choice. Conditions, reliability and handling findings remain possible questions. The model explains the tension without one universally correct family choice.

Transfer: The claim exceeds the tested scope; unexamined variation remains unresolved. Choices based on exaggerated certainty are inadequately informed and may distort expectations or pressure.

Scoring criteria: Locate testing before transfer, not within an established pregnancy; separate testing from alteration; explain limits and competing values. Assess reasoning, not a prescribed yes or no.
