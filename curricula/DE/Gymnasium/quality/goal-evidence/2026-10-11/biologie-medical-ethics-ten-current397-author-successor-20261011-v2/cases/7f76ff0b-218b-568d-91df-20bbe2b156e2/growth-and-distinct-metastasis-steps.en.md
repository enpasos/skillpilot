# growth-and-distinct-metastasis-steps

Authored teaching case using entirely fictional data. No learner observation, actual execution, clinical or legal recommendation. Cases illustrate possible evidence; they do not impose an additional learner task quota.

Schematic model: A control protein in tissueT normally restrains the cell cycle when DNA is damaged. A change disables this brake in one cell lineage; other altered signals favor survival. Cell number increases. In a second finding, cells leave the primary tumor, invade nearby tissue and vessels, and travel in blood to a distant site. Some leave the vessel, survive and divide to form another collection. No timing or success probability is supplied.

Explain the molecular, cellular and tissue sequence producing the tumor, then separately the steps of metastasis. What is the origin of cells at the distant site?

Fresh variation: A model intervention prevents vessel entry but does not restore cycle restraint. What follows for local growth and the depicted spread route?

## Model response and scoring

Loss of cycle restraint and enhanced survival alter division and cell loss, allowing a tumor collection. Distant metastasis additionally requires detachment and invasion, vessel entry, transport, exit, survival and colonization. Faster division alone does not establish it. The distant tumor cells descend from lineageT; their location does not turn them into normal cells of the destination organ.

Transfer: Local growth may persist because altered regulation remains. The specified route is interrupted at vessel entry. This establishes neither elimination of every alternative route nor clinical cure.

Scoring criteria: Explain both level sequences and distinct dissemination steps; preserve cell origin and limit the intervention to its model effect. Increased division alone is not evidence of metastasis.
