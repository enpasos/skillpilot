# prenatal-screening-and-informed-options

Authored teaching case using entirely fictional data. No learner observation, actual execution, clinical or legal recommendation. Cases illustrate possible evidence; they do not impose an additional learner task quota.

Classroom model: ProcedureS examines cell-free DNA from maternal blood for an indication of a selected chromosome difference. S is screening rather than diagnostic confirmation. ProcedureD examines a suitable collected fetal cell sample for that chromosome question; sampling requires information about its own burdens and risks. Individual risk figures and legal conditions are not supplied. PersonC wants reliable information but fears added burden and social pressure.

Compare evidential scope and burden. Evaluate options by information, autonomy and burden, stating a conditional course, an open question and a respectfully considered alternative.

Fresh variation: After information, C does not want further testing now. Does that alone make her choice indefensible?

## Model response and scoring

S supplies a bounded indication and must not be called a confirmed diagnosis. D addresses the chromosome question with its own conditions and burdens. Information may help C but must be weighed against burden and autonomous choice. Understandable counseling about purpose, limits and missing risk information is a defensible initial course; targeted testing or informed refusal may be reasoned from personal priorities. Concrete burdens and intended use of results remain open. Social pressure does not replace consent.

Transfer: No. Informed refusal may protect autonomy and reflect personal weighting of burden. Evaluation should explain consequences and uncertainty without treating acceptance as the only correct result.

Scoring criteria: Distinguish screening from diagnosis, reason about at least two material-related criteria, identify missing information and an alternative; give neither a health guarantee nor invented individual risk figures.
