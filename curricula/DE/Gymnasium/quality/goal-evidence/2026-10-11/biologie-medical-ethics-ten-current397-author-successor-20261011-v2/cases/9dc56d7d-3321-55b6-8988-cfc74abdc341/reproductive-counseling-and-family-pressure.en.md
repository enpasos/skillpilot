# reproductive-counseling-and-family-pressure

Authored teaching case using entirely fictional data. No learner observation, actual execution, clinical or legal recommendation. Cases illustrate possible evidence; they do not impose an additional learner task quota.

Fictional case: PersonB wants to understand a familial finding before possible family planning. The material supplies one known variant and inheritance model, without individual prognoses. B wants information but fears pressure toward a family-preferred choice. Counseling offers explanations of findings and limits, voluntary further examination, reflection time and confidential conversations.

Explain how genetic counseling supports a choice while preserving nondirectiveness. Formulate a response with an option, a values question and a suitable next step.

Fresh variation: B wants a relative to attend. Does that automatically permit unlimited later sharing of every result?

## Model response and scoring

Explain the finding and model so B understands meaning and open questions, explicitly identifying absent individual prognoses. “Which information would help, and which family expectation is difficult? We can discuss the finding first without immediately deciding on testing or a course of action. If you wish, we can then clarify purpose, limits and result disclosure.” Information, voluntariness, reflection and confidentiality support personal rather than family-prescribed choice.

Transfer: No. Accompaniment and later result disclosure are distinct choices. Clarify which material will be discussed jointly and which sharing B wishes, for specified purposes; accompaniment does not replace understandable information for B.

Scoring criteria: Connect information and personal choice, state limits and pressure, provide a nondirective response and justify the new disclosure distinction. Prescribe no personal medical decision.
