# pgd-chromosome-rearrangement-and-voluntariness

Authored teaching case using entirely fictional data. No learner observation, actual execution, clinical or legal recommendation. Cases illustrate possible evidence; they do not impose an additional learner task quota.

Fictional case: Testing suitable cells from an embryo before transfer addresses a known familial chromosome rearrangement. Sampling, evaluation and counseling precede transfer choice. The technique can fail and addresses only the specified chromosome question. Two statements are supplied: A “Information can support our own choice,” and B “Not using the option is irresponsible.” Legal permission is not decided by this model.

Explain method and timing. Analyze A and B by information, voluntariness, protective interests and social pressure. State a question to resolve before deciding.

Fresh variation: The test fails technically. What is known, and why is portraying it as a normal result problematic?

## Model response and scoring

Information comes from the sample before transfer; the examination does not repair the rearrangement. A can express nondirective information. B assumes a moral duty and may pressure voluntary choice. Embryo protection and the couple’s wishes may receive different weight; technical possibility does not resolve that conflict. Reliability, conditions or handling of findings are unresolved questions.

Transfer: An uninterpretable result is not a negative finding for the rearrangement. Methodological uncertainty remains. False reassurance removes the factual basis for informed choice.

Scoring criteria: Explain principle, timing, limits and at least two competing ethical aspects. The changed technical premise must alter the information basis; claim neither permission nor actual execution.
