# fictional-testing-rule-and-public-hearing

Authored teaching case using entirely fictional data. No learner observation, actual execution, clinical or legal recommendation. Cases illustrate possible evidence; they do not impose an additional learner task quota.

Entirely fictional legal systemF, not a statement of current German law: RuleF1 permits genetic testing only after understandable information and voluntary consent. Results may be used only for the agreed medical purpose. A public advisory body hears affected people, researchers and privacy advocates; parliament decides amendments. A company wants additional use for advertising without new consent.

Explain the action regulated by F1 and its binding conditions. Distinguish the hearing from parliamentary decision. Separate a biological DNA claim from a current rule and a proposal to change it.

Fresh variation: The advisory body unanimously supports wider data use, but parliament has not decided. Has F1 changed?

## Model response and scoring

F1 regulates testing and use of results. Missing voluntary consent or an unagreed advertising purpose fails its stated conditions. A hearing collects reasoned perspectives and does not enact law. Parliament decides amendments. A DNA variant is a biological finding, F1 a binding rule, and permitting advertising use is a political proposal.

Transfer: No. The vote contributes to deliberation and may influence politics, but the material assigns legal change to parliament. The original conditions remain binding until a legislative decision.

Scoring criteria: Apply the supplied rule accurately; identify information, consent and purpose; distinguish deliberative and decision authority, facts and normative demands. Do not require invented current German legal sections.
