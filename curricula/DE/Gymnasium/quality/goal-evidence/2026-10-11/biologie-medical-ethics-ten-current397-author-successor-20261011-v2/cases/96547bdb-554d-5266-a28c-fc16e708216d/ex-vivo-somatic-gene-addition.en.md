# ex-vivo-somatic-gene-addition

Authored teaching case using entirely fictional data. No learner observation, actual execution, clinical or legal recommendation. Cases illustrate possible evidence; they do not impose an additional learner task quota.

Didactic procedure: A person’s blood-forming stem cells are collected. Outside the body, a delivery vehicle introduces functional genetic information for missing enzymeE into suitable cells. Tested cells are returned, intending enzyme production in their descendants. Possible risks are unwanted regulation changes from the intervention and insufficient functional cells; no outcome data are supplied. Egg and sperm cells are not treated.

Describe target cells, genetic function, delivery and return. Evaluate intended benefit and supplied risks through control questions. Distinguish somatic change, an inheritance claim and demonstrated effectiveness.

Fresh variation: The added information is detected, but enzymeE is not produced. Which conclusion changes?

## Model response and scoring

The person’s somatic stem cells are targets; vehicle delivery and cell treatment occur ex vivo before return. Added information should enable enzymeE. Benefit would be functional improvement if enough suitable descendants produce it. Unwanted regulation and absent lasting function require intervention, cell-function and follow-up checks. Somatic treatment does not establish germline change. The intended mechanism is not yet effectiveness evidence or a cure promise.

Transfer: Gene delivery is demonstrated, but intended function is not. The benefit claim lacks support; expression and suitable cell function remain unresolved. DNA detection alone does not establish therapeutic success.

Scoring criteria: Explain the complete process, condition benefit on mechanism, examine both supplied risks, distinguish somatic and germline change and do not infer success from gene presence.
