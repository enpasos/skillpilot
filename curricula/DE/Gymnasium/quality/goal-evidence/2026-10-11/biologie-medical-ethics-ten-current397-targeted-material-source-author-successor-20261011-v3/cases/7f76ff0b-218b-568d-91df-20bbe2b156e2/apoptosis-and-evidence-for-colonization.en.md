# apoptosis-and-evidence-for-colonization

Authored teaching case using entirely fictional data. No learner observation, actual execution, clinical or legal recommendation. Cases illustrate possible evidence; they do not impose an additional learner task quota.

Two fictional findings: LineageA has altered survival signaling and undergoes apoptosis less often despite damage; continued division increases cell number in the original tissue. SeriesB additionally shows lineage cells in a vessel and later a growing collection in distant tissue. Detecting a single cell in blood would not itself establish a growing distant tumor.

Explain disrupted molecular regulation, cell number and possible tumor formation. Reconstruct dissemination and distinguish evidence of transport from successful colonization.

Fresh variation: After transport to tissueU, the cells die without forming a growing collection. Does the model establish a metastasis there?

## Model response and scoring

Altered survival regulation reduces cell loss; with continued division, more cells may remain and form a tumor collection. B requires leaving the origin and local barriers, entering vessels, traveling, exiting, surviving and dividing at the new site. The later growing collection supports colonization. A blood sample with individual cells demonstrates only part of this sequence.

Transfer: No. Transport and arrival are supplied, but successful colonization and growth are absent. This does not restore regulation in the primary tumor; its relation to tumor formation remains a separate claim.

Scoring criteria: Distinguish apoptosis from division, explain the causal cell-number balance, separate transport from colonization and invent no treatment success.
