# contained-insulin-protein-production

Authored teaching case using entirely fictional data. No learner observation, actual execution, clinical or legal recommendation. Cases illustrate possible evidence; they do not impose an additional learner task quota.

Teaching model: Bacteria carry a suitable construct configured for bacterial expression of an insulin product. They grow in a contained production vessel. The protein is then recovered and purified; bacteria are not administered as the medicine. Two hazards require examination: inadequate product purity and escape of viable production bacteria. No clinical effectiveness data are provided.

Analyze production and distinguish organism from product. Explain a benefit, both supplied risks and appropriate controls. Give a conditional judgment with an unresolved information question.

Fresh variation: The same construct is used in bacteria that do not express the protein. Why does possessing the gene not establish usable production?

## Model response and scoring

Suitable genetic information is expressed; bacteria produce protein for separate recovery and purification. A possible benefit is controlled protein production. Impure preparations may contain unwanted components, calling for purification and quality control. Escaping viable organisms create environmental contact, requiring examination of containment and inactivation. The method is defensible under demonstrated controls; the diagram establishes neither medicinal approval nor effectiveness. Evidence for purity and containment remains to be provided.

Transfer: Without effective expression, the desired protein is not produced in this model. Recovery and purification cannot establish a product that was never formed. Gene possession, function and usable purified product require distinct evidence.

Scoring criteria: Connect construct, expression, production and purification; do not equate bacteria with protein; explain both material-bound risks and controls, with a judgment conditional on missing evidence. No general GMO approval.
