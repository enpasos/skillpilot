# prenatal-targeted-result-and-limits

Authored teaching case using entirely fictional data. No learner observation, actual execution, clinical or legal recommendation. Cases illustrate possible evidence; they do not impose an additional learner task quota.

Fictional case: Targeted analysis of a suitable cell sample does not find the tested familial variantV. Only V was examined. No information on other genetic differences, later development or overall health is supplied. PersonD wants private discussion; an organization demands the result as a condition for an offer. Voluntariness, confidentiality and permission for requested sharing remain to be examined.

Evaluate the methodological claim and ethical effects of demanded disclosure. Give a reasoned conditional course and a possible alternative weighting.

Fresh variation: The organization promises confidential storage but plans a later unagreed use. Does confidentiality alone resolve the ethical question?

## Model response and scoring

The finding concerns V, not “completely healthy.” Disclosure affects private information and autonomy; conditional access may pressure choice. First clarify purpose, necessity, desired consent and permission. Privacy protection may carry greater weight; alternatively D may consider sharing for a clearly limited, voluntarily desired purpose. That alternative must retain claim limits without assuming legal permission.

Transfer: No. Confidentiality and purpose-limited voluntary informed use are distinct requirements. Secure storage may still contain unagreed use; new purpose and consent require separate clarification.

Scoring criteria: Limit the finding, weigh competing disclosure values, explain conditional options, and evaluate the changed purpose independently. Provide no blanket real-world legal advice.
