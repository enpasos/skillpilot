# stem-cell-model-not-clinical-success

Authored teaching case using entirely fictional data. No learner observation, actual execution, clinical or legal recommendation. Cases illustrate possible evidence; they do not impose an additional learner task quota.

Fictional research data: Induced pluripotent stem cells derived from donated body cells are differentiated into a nerve-cell model. A laboratory test shows altered electrical activity after compoundZ. The donor consented only to the agreed model research. Human cell administration lacks evidence on safety, function in the organism and effectiveness. These invented data establish no actual study.

Evaluate research utility from origin, differentiation and model findings. Distinguish research from medical claims. Weigh consent, benefit and burden in a conditional judgment.

Fresh variation: Researchers now propose an unagreed commercial transfer of the cells. What changes in the evaluation?

## Model response and scoring

A pluripotent source provides differentiated cells for the specified model question. Activity findings can inform a model mechanism rather than establish human treatment. Medical use needs distinct safety, function and effectiveness evidence. Potential knowledge and respected informed purpose support bounded research, while added burden or wider use need separate evaluation. The agreed model research may be defensible under transparent conditions, without endorsing every clinical use.

Transfer: The model finding is unchanged. Consent for the new purpose is not automatically established; its scope and voluntariness, benefits and affected interests require clarification for that use.

Scoring criteria: Connect source and differentiation to research, distinguish lab findings from clinical success, justify consent and a competing protection or burden consideration, and recognize the purpose change.
