# enzyme-organism-and-environmental-release

Authored teaching case using entirely fictional data. No learner observation, actual execution, clinical or legal recommendation. Cases illustrate possible evidence; they do not impose an additional learner task quota.

Fictional comparison: After gene transfer, strainA produces a specified enzyme in a contained vessel. ProposalB would introduce the same living bacteria into open water. The model enzyme degrades compoundX; effects on other compounds were not tested. Survival and growth of bacteria outside containment are unknown. Recovered enzyme solution can be investigated separately.

Compare A and B by mechanism, benefit, risks and controllability. Explain why evaluating purified enzyme solution does not evaluate release of living organisms.

Fresh variation: A new investigation establishes X degradation by purified enzyme but still provides no data on bacteria in water. Which judgment may change?

## Model response and scoring

A uses expression for controlled enzyme production and recovery. Degradation of X is a potential benefit. B changes exposure and reversibility: unknown organism survival and other enzyme effects are distinct risks. Containment, inactivation and targeted effect studies address different controls. The evidence does not establish B as acceptable; environmental and effect data are missing. Solution contains the product, whereas release introduces a potentially reproducing producer.

Transfer: The specific product benefit has stronger evidence. Survival, spread and other effects of organism release remain unresolved. Demonstrated enzyme utility does not make every GMO use acceptable.

Scoring criteria: Analyze the organism and product, explain benefit and two bounded risk questions, weigh controllability, and apply new evidence only within its scope.
