# predictive-counseling-and-right-not-to-know

Authored teaching case using entirely fictional data. No learner observation, actual execution, clinical or legal recommendation. Cases illustrate possible evidence; they do not impose an additional learner task quota.

Fictional counseling script: PersonA considers predictive testing for a known familial variant. The methods sheet states that testing addresses this variant only; finding it does not automatically establish timing or severity of a possible later course. A says, “I first want to know what the result would change; I may not want to know it yet.” A relative asks for telephone disclosure without A’s consent.

Explain counseling principles and give a nondirective response with options, a values question and a next clarification step. Address claim limits, reflection time and the relative’s request.

Fresh variation: A declines after understanding the information. Is that automatically noncooperation, and may counseling covertly force another choice?

## Model response and scoring

Counseling explains purpose, limits, consequences and options understandably. One response is: “We can first clarify which information you want and what it might change. After information, you may choose testing, further counseling or no testing for now. Which consequence matters most to you?” Variant findings must not imply a certain course. A has reflection time and autonomy. The relative’s request does not automatically permit disclosure; wishes, consent, confidentiality and communication conditions need clarification.

Transfer: No. Informed refusal can be autonomous choice. Counseling may discuss questions and consequences openly without replacing personal choice with pressure or a prescribed “correct” answer.

Scoring criteria: Explain purpose and limits clearly; include nondirectiveness, reflection time, knowing/not knowing and confidentiality; offer genuine options and clarification without covert recommendation.
