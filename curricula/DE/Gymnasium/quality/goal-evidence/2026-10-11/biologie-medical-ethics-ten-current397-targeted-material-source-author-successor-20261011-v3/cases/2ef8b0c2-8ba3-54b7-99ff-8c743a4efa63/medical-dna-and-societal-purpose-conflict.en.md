# medical-dna-and-societal-purpose-conflict

Authored teaching case using entirely fictional data. No learner observation, actual execution, clinical or legal recommendation. Cases illustrate possible evidence; they do not impose an additional learner task quota.

Fictional use: A targeted DNA test examines variantV for a medical question; the finding concerns V rather than comprehensive prognosis. A second societal application compares selected markers for identity assessment. Depending on method, matching markers supplies evidence without independently resolving every identity, relationship or health question. PersonE consents to the medical purpose. A provider plans later identity comparison and data collection without further agreement.

Explain medical and societal benefits and limits. Analyze consent, purpose limitation, confidentiality and possible family interests. Give a conditional judgment and a defensible opposing position.

Fresh variation: The provider encrypts storage but retains the unannounced new purpose. Is the ethical conflict completely resolved?

## Model response and scoring

V analysis may support the specific medical question; marker analysis may inform identity. Each claim depends on method and tested data. Consent to one purpose does not automatically permit another; autonomy and confidentiality support separate clarification. Shared genetic features can indirectly affect relatives without granting blanket disclosure. Additional use should be conditional on transparent purpose and voluntary information. An opposing position may emphasize societal identity benefits but must still justify protections and conditions.

Transfer: No. Encryption may support confidentiality but does not replace informed voluntariness or purpose limitation. The new benefit still requires weighing against these protections.

Scoring criteria: Explain both contexts and limits, analyze actual consent/purpose/disclosure conflict, weigh at least two criteria and articulate an opposing position. Do not equate security technology with consent.
