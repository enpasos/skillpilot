# prenatal-chromosome-and-target-sequence

Authored teaching case using entirely fictional data. No learner observation, actual execution, clinical or legal recommendation. Cases illustrate possible evidence; they do not impose an additional learner task quota.

Teaching model, not individual diagnosis: Fetal cells obtained from amniotic fluid undergo examination of chromosome number and large structural changes after suitable preparation. A second test reads a specified DNA sequence from the same suitable sample. The chromosome finding in the model shows an additional copy of chromosome21; the tested sequence has no change at positionM. Other sequence regions were not examined.

For both methods, explain sample, investigation step, biological target and warranted claim. Why does the unchanged sequence establish neither a normal chromosome count nor comprehensive health?

Fresh variation: The new question concerns a small base change in geneN despite a normal chromosome count. How should the method sequence change?

## Model response and scoring

The cells provide chromosomes for visual comparison of number and large structure; here the additional copy is visible. Sequence analysis determines the bases in a selected region and excludes the tested change there. These distinct targets complement one another. A normal positionM addresses neither chromosome count nor every genetic or nongenetic health question.

Transfer: Chromosome counting cannot resolve this small sequence change. A suitable targeted DNA test must examine geneN; an earlier test of M is insufficient. The new finding remains limited to the variation actually investigated.

Scoring criteria: Match method to question: explain cell sample→chromosome comparison and DNA sample→targeted sequence. Credit distinct limits and justified geneN testing; an overall health guarantee is a scientific error.
