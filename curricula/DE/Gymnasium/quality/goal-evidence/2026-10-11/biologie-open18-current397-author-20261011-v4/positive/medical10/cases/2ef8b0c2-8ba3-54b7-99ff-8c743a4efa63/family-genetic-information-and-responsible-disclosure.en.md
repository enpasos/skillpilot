# family-genetic-information-and-responsible-disclosure

Authored teaching case using entirely fictional data. No learner observation, actual execution, clinical or legal recommendation. Cases illustrate possible evidence; they do not impose an additional learner task quota.

Fictional conflict: A targeted medical DNA test finds variantW in personF. The material supplies possible medical relevance without a certain individual course. Relatives may find the information relevant. A social counseling organization proposes anonymized group analysis for a voluntary information course, but actual anonymity and voluntariness are unverified. F does not want unsolicited family disclosure.

Explain both uses, analyze protections and family interests, and evaluate a possible course. Give a reasoned alternative or opposing position and an unresolved question.

Fresh variation: F now expressly agrees to limited discussion with a named relative but still rejects group analysis. Which use changes in the evaluation?

## Model response and scoring

Testing can inform the bounded medical question without certain prediction. Group analysis may aid societal information, conditional on suitable data, actual anonymity and voluntary use. F’s autonomy and confidentiality coexist with possible family information interests. Confidential counseling on limits and voluntary communication is a defensible initial course. A counterposition may give family exchange greater weight without justifying unexamined disclosure. Consent scope and demonstrated protection of group data remain unresolved.

Transfer: The specifically desired limited discussion has a new consent basis. It does not establish consent to group analysis or additional recipients. Variant limits and the agreed boundaries must remain in the discussion.

Scoring criteria: Explain medical and societal significance, analyze knowing/not knowing, purpose and confidentiality with family relevance, weigh values and do not generalize newly limited consent.
