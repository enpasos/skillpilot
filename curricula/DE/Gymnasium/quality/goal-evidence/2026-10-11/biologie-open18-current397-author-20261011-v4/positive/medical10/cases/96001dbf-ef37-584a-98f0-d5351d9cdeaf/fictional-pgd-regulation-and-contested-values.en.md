# fictional-pgd-regulation-and-contested-values

Authored teaching case using entirely fictional data. No learner observation, actual execution, clinical or legal recommendation. Cases illustrate possible evidence; they do not impose an additional learner task quota.

Fictional ruleR: Genetic testing before embryo transfer requires voluntary choice after counseling and prior assessment by the competent body. Counseling alone does not replace that assessment. A citizens’ conference may publish recommendations for future rules. A biological methods sheet describes testing a cell sample for a specific feature; it does not establish which uses are legally permitted.

Explain the relation of method, conditions, decision authority and public participation. Why does technical feasibility alone not establish permission? Identify reasoned perspectives for the citizens’ conference.

Fresh variation: R is later explicitly amended to require a second counseling session. What is now binding, and what remains a recommendation?

## Model response and scoring

The sheet explains biology; R specifies permission conditions. Counseling and voluntary choice are conditions, with separate assessment by the competent body. The conference may discuss autonomy, protective interests and equal treatment, but has only advisory powers in the material. Feasibility does not answer normative or legal permission.

Transfer: The added counseling is now a binding condition under the stated effective amendment. Other conference proposals remain recommendations unless adopted. The biological method remains a distinct description of facts.

Scoring criteria: Explain all four functions, handle the supplied amendment accurately and give a reasoned participation perspective. Confusing an opinion with authorization or law misses the goal.
