# pgd-before-transfer-with-known-variant

Entirely fictional authored teaching case; no actual clinical execution, legal advice, learner observation or additional learner task quota.

Classroom model: Fertilization outside the body is followed by development to a suitable embryo stage. A few suitable cells are tested before possible transfer for known familial variantV. Testing does not change V. The finding concerns that variant and guarantees neither complete health nor pregnancy. Supplied perspectives concern avoiding suffering and autonomy, versus protective interests of embryos and social selection or expectation pressure. Legal conditions remain unresolved. Fictional classroom ruleR, not a statement of current law: Under the model, testing before embryo transfer is permitted only after understandable counseling, freely given explicit consent and prior positive assessment by bodyG. Here counseling and free consent are present, but G’s assessment is unresolved. A favorable test result does not replace that assessment.

Explain PGD in correct temporal sequence and its claim limits. Explain both ethical tensions using the example and identify an unresolved point. No particular value judgment is required. Apply supplied ruleR to the case and distinguish technical possibility, rule-based permission and ethical judgment. Which step remains unresolved before testing?

Fresh variation: A laboratory claims to exclude every genetic disease despite testing only V. Explain methodological and ethical objections. In addition, G has now issued a positive assessment, but the person withdraws consent before testing. Does that satisfy all conditions under R?

## Model response and scoring

Fertilization and early development precede sampling and genetic examination before possible transfer. PGD provides information on V rather than gene correction. Avoiding suffering may support testing, whereas embryo protection and selection pressure challenge particular choices; autonomy requires voluntary informed choice. Conditions, reliability and handling findings remain possible questions. The model explains the tension without one universally correct family choice. Under ruleR, counseling and consent are insufficient: positive assessment by G is still missing. Testing may be technically possible but the described state does not satisfy all modeled permission conditions. Even a later positive assessment would not determine ethical agreement; reasoned weighing of values remains a separate question.

Transfer: The claim exceeds the tested scope; unexamined variation remains unresolved. Choices based on exaggerated certainty are inadequately informed and may distort expectations or pressure. Under the changed rule premise the assessment condition is met, but free consent is no longer present. Thus testing still is not permitted under R. Counseling, assessment and technical findings cannot replace current consent. This limits the rule-bound course of action without substituting for an independent ethical rationale.

Scoring criteria: Locate testing before transfer, not within an established pregnancy; separate testing from alteration; explain limits and competing values. Assess reasoning, not a prescribed yes or no. Apply all three conditions of the supplied fictional rule and withdrawn consent; do not require claims about current German law.
