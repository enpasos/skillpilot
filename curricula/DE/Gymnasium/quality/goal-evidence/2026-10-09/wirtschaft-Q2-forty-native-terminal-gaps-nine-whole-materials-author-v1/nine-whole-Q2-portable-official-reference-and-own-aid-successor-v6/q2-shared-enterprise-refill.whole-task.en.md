## ReFill – one fictional business dossier
ReFill sells repairable bottles. Consumer market: 1,500 interested people, at most two yearly; actual market volume2,100, own sales600. Business market: 40 potential customers buying50 each, only25 initially reachable. Potential is not guaranteed own sales.
Procurement A buys1,000 shells upfront at EUR8, tying up8,000; verified labour/environment inspection but500km transport. B supplies weekly at10,40km, labour conditions self-reported; failure would stop production for five days. Unsupported certification is not evidence.
Production per batch: cutting25, assembly35, inspection10minutes plus90 waiting before assembly. Cutting alone to15 leaves waiting unchanged. Variants require setups; defect rate4%.
Cash: initial6,000 plus24,000 new operating cash, machine22,000, supplier bill12,000 due in four weeks. Three-month bank loan10,000; machine payoff expected after24months. Equity10,000 requires20% voting rights, not debt. Old machine book value9,000, documented buyer offers7,000.
Price20, variable cost12, annual fixed8,000. New cooling variant cost14; its price/sales untested. SWOT: internal repair know-how/low cash, external durable demand/cheap rivals. Options sell existing product more to existing customers or develop cooling variant for them; a limited pilot is possible.
Environmental units: single-use1 each use; refill manufacture12 plus cleaning0.2 and transport0.1 each use; disposal unquantified. Customers seek durability, workers predictable conditions, suppliers fair payment. Advertising does not prove environmental/social performance.
1. Assess procurement/sales across economic, environmental, social and ethical dimensions; calculate10/20-use totals and limits.
2. Classify financing by origins, cash, profitability and control; calculate the gap and sale profit/cash, then justify a choice.
3. Apply SWOT and break-even; assess the variant conditionally.
4. Classify both strategies and design responsibility, pilot, indicators and stop/adapt rules.
5. Compare purchasing forms, cash/inventory, reliability and evidenced sustainability; request a concrete check.
6. Analyse process, bottleneck/waiting and cutting gains; propose another improvement and examine quality/variant risks.
7. Calculate consumer/business potential, actual market and own shares; propose a tested entry plan without guaranteed sales.
