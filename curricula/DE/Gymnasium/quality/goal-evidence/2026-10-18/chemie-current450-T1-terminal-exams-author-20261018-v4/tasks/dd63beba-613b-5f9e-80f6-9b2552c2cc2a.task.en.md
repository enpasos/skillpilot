# Examine catalysis and conductivity evidence in dialogue

Work independently. Data explicitly marked as models are teaching assumptions. Total 12 points. Passing requires 12/12: every essential aspect must be demonstrated. Each task earns 4 performance points only for a complete coherent answer (0–3 for gaps) and 2 points for a justified scope limit (0–1 for gaps). Missing required practical execution or actual exchange cannot be offset by written answers.

### Supplementary complete task material

Discussion material: explicitly synthetic model reaction A ⇌ C in a closed system with equal temperature, volume and initial A=1.00 mol/L, C=0. After 1 min, C=0.15 mol/L without and 0.40 mol/L with a suitable catalyst. At established equilibrium both contain C=0.60 and A=0.40 mol/L. No real measurement. The partner starts with “The catalyst produces more product”. State your own initial position, ask genuine follow-ups and respond to actual contributions. New discussion card: “A catalyst only speeds the forward reaction”. Blank log: speaker | actual contribution | scientific response/evidence | own standpoint review.
Alternative conductivity card, also synthetic: A20=1800, B35=2100, A35=2098 µS/cm; repeat scatter ±3 µS/cm, temperature in °C. The initial NaCl claim compares A20 with B35; precise ion species and concentration calibration are initially unknown. New card after independent argument: A20=1800 and B20=2100 µS/cm, same known ion type/matrix, checked standards, scatter ±3 each. Cards initiate discussion; they do not prescribe replies as a dialogue. Complete both contexts with actual questions and responses.

### Task 1 (6 points)

Scientifically explain to the partner the difference between rate and equilibrium. Constructively ask about their evidence, respond to their reply and reflect/correct an initial own statement if needed. Retain an actual dialogue or moderator log.
Fresh variation: Partner now claims “a catalyst only speeds the forward reaction”: respond scientifically and constructively considering equilibrium.

Justify the scientific claims and state one specific limit to the inference.

### Task 2 (6 points)

Conduct a scientifically constructive dialogue: explain, argue from the control, listen/ask, respond to an actual counter-question and review an own standpoint. Document contributions by speaker.
Fresh variation: Partner supplies new equal-temperature data: examine evidence and change your standpoint if supported.

Justify the scientific claims and state one specific limit to the inference.
