### Task 1

D+2 H2O → Me2Si(OH)2+2 HCl. Locally: ≡Si–OH+HO–Si≡ → ≡Si–O–Si≡+H2O. Hydrolysis followed by controlled condensation gives predominantly uncrosslinked chains in A, with M limiting ends: silicone oil/lubricating film. Much T permits a dense spatial network in B: silicone resin/hard coating. Long D chains with a few crosslinks give a sparse network in C: silicone rubber/elastic seal. More crosslinker can restrict mobility and extensibility; unchanged seal suitability does not follow. Grade data are needed for limits. Other silicone cure routes exist; this model is not a universal manufacturing route.

Scope limit: The supplied models establish no universal moisture requirement or temperature guarantee for all silicones. Function depends on the actual chain/network structure and stated service conditions.

### Task 2

MeSi(OEt)3+3 H2O → MeSi(OH)3+3 EtOH; two silanols condense with H2O release. Difunctional chain formation with monofunctional end limitation gives oil O; many trifunctional units give resin network H; sparse crosslinking of long chains gives rubber K. The supplied properties therefore fit damping fluid, varnish and joint sealant. Dry K lacks the intended hydrolytic activation of its crosslinker, so the specified cure may be inhibited. Already manufactured O/H are different cure situations. This implies neither moisture dependence of other routes, such as addition-cured systems, nor universal temperature suitability.
