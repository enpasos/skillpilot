# Personally investigate metal deposition

## Task and setting

In supervised school chemistry, investigate whether known iron changes a dilute copper(II) sulfate solution and produces metal deposition. Compare two controls, record your own observations and justify a bounded redox interpretation. There are no supplied observation results. Watching a demonstration, using another person's images or copying a completed model is not personal execution.

This small, unheated method is for year 12. Begin only after instruction and express authorisation by the present qualified teacher, with the current local risk assessment, required protection and waste arrangements in place. Otherwise the practical requirement remains open. Do not perform it at home.

## Supplied materials

- Three intact laboratory tubes/vessels of at least 15 mL, stable in a rack and containment tray, labelled A/B/C.
- Ready-to-use **0.010 mol/L copper(II) sulfate**, **5.0 mL each for A/B**, supplied by the teacher in a labelled small working portion. Do not prepare a solid reagent or independently dilute it.
- **5.0 mL deionised water for C**, supplied separately.
- Two known clean, bare, uncoated iron strips, approximately **20 × 5 × 0.5 mm**, with deburred edges, already prepared and checked by the teacher. No unknown, galvanised, painted or rusty objects; no learner cutting, sanding or acid cleaning.
- Two clean forceps, one 10-mL measuring cylinder for copper sulfate and a separate one for water, timer, white comparison background, separate labelled specimen trays and a small teacher-supplied rinse-water portion.
- Side-protection goggles, closed laboratory clothing/footwear and properly fitting chemical-protective gloves selected by the teacher against the current actual product SDS. The SDS used here specifies EN-374-tested nitrile **over 0.11 mm**; the teacher checks actual product suitability and fit. Gloves do not replace forceps.

## Protection and return

The actual solution SDS classifies it as an aquatic environmental hazard, H411. Small quantities or a pale colour do not make it drain waste. Avoid eye/skin contact; no tasting or intentional smelling, food/drink or mouth pipetting. Secure hair/loose clothing. Use no added acid, heat, flame, power supply or other reagent.

Keep all solutions, controls, rinse water, solids and contaminated implements in the tray. Return them to the teacher for separately labelled compatible metal-containing liquid and solid waste collection under the local disposal arrangement. Even colourless-looking liquid must not enter the drain. Do not take specimens home. The teacher organises final disposal.

For a spill, broken glass, skin/eye contact or unexpected marked heating/gas release: stop and inform the teacher immediately; no learner neutralisation or repair. Immediately rinse affected skin/eyes with water and follow the school first-aid procedure; the teacher uses the actual SDS and arranges necessary medical help. The instructed teacher collects spills with suitable absorbent rather than flushing them into a drain. After return, remove gloves correctly and wash hands.

## Your procedure

1. With the teacher check product/metal identity, authorisation, protection and waste-return vessels. In the blank log record actual stock/concentration, iron source/surface, equipment, room temperature and your own role. Record the initial surfaces and solutions before adding anything.
2. Label A/B/C. Personally measure 5.0 mL supplied copper sulfate into A and B. Use the separate cylinder for 5.0 mL water in C. Record actual readings and graduations. Do not return portions to stock or cross-contaminate equipment.
3. Using separate forceps, place one iron strip in A and one in C, completely immersed. **B has no metal.** Do not touch specimens. Record actual insertion times and start the timer. Keep conditions identical, with no heating, shaking or unequal treatment.
4. Observe all three immediately after insertion and at approximately **2, 5, 10 and 15 minutes**, recording actual times. Separately record surfaces/coatings, liquid, loose solids and deviations. Use the same lighting and white background. Also record no change or uncertainty. Never invent fading, coating or an expected result.
5. At 15 minutes remove the two strips with separate forceps, letting drops return to the corresponding vessel. Place strips in separate labelled trays and compare with your starting record. If expressly authorised, rinse only with the supplied small water portion above the waste tray and retain rinse water. Do not dry-brush or scrape coatings.
6. Record completion, deviations and your personal actions, and return all materials under the teacher's instructions. In group work each action credited as personal must actually have been performed by that person; observation alone does not suffice.

## Interpret your actual evidence

Compare A with B/C, separating observation and interpretation. Which control supports an effect of iron in the copper-ion solution? What confounders remain? Derive electron loss/gain and a charge-/atom-balanced net reaction from the known starting substances; distinguish metal from metal ions. Explain the limited connection to recovering metal from solution, including consumption of the added iron.

Explain why coating colour alone is not an unambiguous elemental identification and your data establish neither complete recovery, purity nor industrial yield. Little colour change proves neither completion nor absence of reaction. Honestly record missing or contradictory evidence, check materials/controls and discuss a justified next step with the teacher. Another trial needs fresh authorisation; do not intensify conditions yourself.
