### Task 1

Cap: inject melt into closed mould, cool/eject. Cup: pressure/vacuum thermoform warm sheet, then cool. Sheet: calender plasticised material through rollers. Tube: continuously extrude melt through an annular die, then cool. Melt routes 180–210°C; thermoforming 100–130°C; avoid 250°C. Check thickness, cooling and flow/stretch behaviour rather than blindly reusing temperatures.

Scope limit: The supplied process window applies only to this specimen. Successful industrial shaping additionally needs suitable flow/stretch response, cooling and, if relevant, crosslinking kinetics; decomposition creates no melting window.

### Task 2

The finished network cannot be remelted/injected/extruded or thermoformed like a thermoplastic; more heat leads to decomposition. Continuously extrude precursor through a profile die, then crosslink under controlled conditions if reaction time and shape stability permit. Thermoforming reshapes existing warm sheet into a discrete form; calendering makes sheets and injection moulding discrete components. Verify processing window and crosslinking kinetics.

Scope limit: The supplied process window applies only to this specimen. Successful industrial shaping additionally needs suitable flow/stretch response, cooling and, if relevant, crosslinking kinetics; decomposition creates no melting window.
