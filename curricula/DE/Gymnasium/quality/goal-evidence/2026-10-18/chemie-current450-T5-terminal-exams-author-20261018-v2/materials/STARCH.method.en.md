## Concrete permitted option: enzymatic starch pretreatment

First answer the new-substrate question without the solution: justify whether ordinary baker's yeast uses starch immediately like glucose and independently propose control comparisons. Then use this complete method card as the defined operating framework for personal execution. This is an original adapted teaching design, not a manufacturer-validated fermentation or yield method. Complete saccharification or a specified gas volume is not promised.

### Materials and preparation

Use only fresh commercial baker's yeast, food-grade potato starch and glucose. Each batch contains 10.0 g substrate and 100.0 mL total aqueous liquid before yeast addition; use 1.00 g yeast where indicated. Enzyme: identified liquid NCBE amyloglucosidase (AMG 300L from Aspergillus niger), never saliva, unidentified enzyme powder or environmental cultures. The supervisor checks product identity, shelf life and safety sheet; record lot and declared activity if supplied. This recipe doses the delivered liquid product by volume without inferring conversion from its trade name.

Prepare aqueous 10-mmol/L citrate buffer at pH 4.50 ±0.10: the supervisor dissolves 1.9212 g anhydrous citric acid in about 700 mL water, adjusts to pH 4.50 at 20–25°C using 0.100-M sodium hydroxide with stirring and measured pH, and fills to 1.000 L; recheck pH. Only the supervisor handles acid/base stocks. For the enzyme control, the supervisor heats a separate AMG portion in an open loosely splash-covered tube for ten minutes at 80–85°C after reaching 80°C, cools it and replaces a measured evaporative loss with water to its original mass. This heat-treated enzyme is an inactivation control, not a demonstrated completely enzyme-free preparation.

Provide at least eight 250-mL flasks, stable 60°C and 30°C water baths, thermometers, balance, graduated vessels/pipettes, filters and tared receivers, and one freely moving 100-mL gas-syringe setup per batch, with unobstructed gas path and 1-mL graduations. Wear goggles/coat; no flames or pressure-tight bottles. The supervisor prepares hot starch and handles hot vessels. Never spray enzyme or let spills dry; avoid skin/eye contact and immediately wet-clean spills under school rules. Stop for respiratory symptoms, splashing, blocked gas paths or broken glass and notify the supervisor.

### Batches and actual procedure

All volumes precede yeast addition. Justify the controls before work; outcomes are open:

| Code | Substrate | Liquid | Yeast after pretreatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| H | 10.0 g starch | 99.0 mL buffer + 1.00 mL active AMG | 1.00 g active yeast |
| I | 10.0 g starch | 99.0 mL buffer + 1.00 mL heat-treated AMG | 1.00 g active yeast |
| N | 10.0 g starch | 100.0 mL buffer, no enzyme | 1.00 g active yeast |
| E | no substrate | 99.0 mL buffer + 1.00 mL active AMG | 1.00 g active yeast |
| EI | no substrate | 99.0 mL buffer + 1.00 mL heat-treated AMG | 1.00 g active yeast |
| G | 10.0 g glucose | 100.0 mL buffer, no enzyme | 1.00 g active yeast |
| H0 | as H | as H | no yeast |
| HI | as H | as H | 1.00 g supervisor-inactivated/cooled yeast |

1. For H/I/N/H0/HI the supervisor disperses starch in its buffer portion and gelatinises for ten minutes at 80–85°C with stirring, open and loosely covered; no enzyme has yet been added. Give starch-free buffer/glucose portions the same heat history. Never heat dry or seal. Record initial/final masses; replace only plausible pure evaporation with water to initial mass. Spilled material is a deviation, not automatically a water loss to repair.
2. Bring every batch to 60 ±2°C. Add its identified 1.00-mL enzyme portion to H/I/E/EI/H0/HI; N and G already contain their full 100.0 mL buffer from step 1; add no further liquid. Record start, measured pH and temperature. Hold for 60 minutes at 60 ±2°C, open/loosely covered, stirring equally and gently every ten minutes without enzyme aerosol. The supervisor checks bath water. This is a tested operating interval, not an asserted quantitative endpoint.
3. Cool every batch to 30 ±1°C before adding any yeast; record final pH and remaining mass. Do not selectively readjust pH. Unequal pH, substantial losses or temperature deviations make a disturbed comparison that must be recorded; never manufacture matching results. Never add live yeast to hot batches.
4. The supervisor prepares HI yeast: heat its weighed baker's-yeast portion for ten minutes at 80–85°C using a little liquid withdrawn from its own 100.0-mL batch, cool, then quantitatively return it. Suspend each active 1.00-g portion in its own withdrawn and returned batch liquid too. Do not silently add extra liquid. H0 stays yeast-free. Label batches and change tools to avoid cross-contamination.
5. After yeast addition investigate for 20 minutes at 30 ±1°C. Record gas at 0/5/10/15/20 minutes with actual times and resolution. Check free syringe movement first; never clamp tubing. At 80 mL, resistance or obstruction stop immediately, let the supervisor vent safely and record time/volume as an interrupted run; do not treat it as a complete 20-minute reading. No fermentation batch operates at 60 or 80°C.
6. At the end/stop actually filter preparations, recording filtrate volume, losses and appearance. Yeast cells and undigested particles may be retained; dissolved sugars and possible fermentation products pass. No distillation, tasting or food use. Collect biological/enzyme residues under school hygiene/disposal rules and clean equipment.

### Analysis and empty record

Compare H with I/N and E with EI: commercial AMG liquid can contain reducing sugars, making E/EI necessary carrier/enzyme blanks. Also compare H/H0/HI and positive condition G. Do not report simple subtraction as a full material balance; different matrices can affect yeast. Gas differences identify neither glucose, CO₂ nor ethanol and establish no complete starch hydrolysis. Benedict's reagent alone would not demonstrate starch-derived glucose with this enzyme product. Justify additional selective analyses needed for such product claims. Report null results and disturbed controls unchanged.
