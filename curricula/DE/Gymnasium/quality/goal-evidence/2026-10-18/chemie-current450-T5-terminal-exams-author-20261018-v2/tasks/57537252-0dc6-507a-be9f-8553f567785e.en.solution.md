## 3. Assess sites for cress germination using field and effect data

In the teaching data,sunny/shaded sites differ jointly in vegetation and moisture index; the index is neither percent water nor a cress water dose. Lawn distribution is not a cress germination result. An original controlled comparison can support a moisture effect on the tested cress germination under recorded conditions; it does not rank the two lawns without justified comparability. A valid report may state that the tested wetter or drier laboratory condition is more favourable/equal/unresolved according to original data,while actual site suitability remains unresolved because of unknown transferability,changing moisture,temperature,light or substrate. Any direction must follow from original counts and vessel replicates. Specify suitable repeated site observations and a justified match to the actual germination environment before preferring a site. Do not claim lifetime suitability,species richness or the whole community.

The same relative moisture index does not establish the same germination environment or temperature effect. The old controlled result applies to its temperature and conditions. Record the new temperature range and uncertainty separately,leave the decision unresolved if necessary,and justify a suitable additional time series or approved controlled test. Do not transfer an effect direction without evidence.

The teaching data give mean cover20% versus about6.7% and differ in both light and temperature. Cover is standing state,not increase. Original controlled light data with matched covariates may support a light effect on frond multiplication of this population under laboratory conditions. An instantaneous field reading does not establish equivalent exposure without matched measurement/time and where relevant spectrum. Site judgement must separately address temperature,nutrients,light history,initial density and other factors; missing evidence may leave the decision unresolved. More light or cover does not universally mean better habitat suitability. Conflicting or null results belong in the evidence table and must not be forced to fit.

Equal instantaneous readings do not establish equal daily exposure; unknown nutrients remain unresolved. The laboratory effect applies to its population,illumination duration and nutrient medium. Separate supported effect,bounded site hypothesis and missing evidence; leave suitability unresolved where needed. Justify an appropriate further measurement or controlled variation without already attributing an effect to the site.
