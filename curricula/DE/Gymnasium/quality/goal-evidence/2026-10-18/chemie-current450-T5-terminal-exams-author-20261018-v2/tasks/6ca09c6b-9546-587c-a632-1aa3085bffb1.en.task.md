## Personal dye electrophoresis at pH 7

This is an original prospective teaching design for supervised investigation. Its particular phosphate matrix and run limits are not a manufacturer-validated dye method. No migration distances or observed learner actions have been obtained.

### Fully identified materials

- Enclosed Mini-Sub Cell GT EDU with 7×7-cm gel tray, compatible comb with at least four wells and intact original safety lid; compatible ground-isolated PowerPac Basic DC supply and original leads. No exposed homemade contacts or instrument modification. The supervisor checks identity, condition and the corresponding manufacturer instructions before use.
- At least 400 mL clear aqueous 10-mmol/L sodium phosphate buffer, checked using a calibrated pH meter at 20–25°C for pH 7.00 ±0.10. Use the same buffer for gel and running liquid. The supervisor may prepare it from 61.85 mL 0.1000-M NaH₂PO₄ and 38.15 mL 0.1000-M Na₂HPO₄, diluted to 1.000 L; this calculation is approximate and does not replace measured pH verification.
- A supervisor-precast, cooled 0.8% agarose gel in this buffer: 0.160 g agarose in 20.0 mL, completely dissolved and solidified in its original tray. Heating and casting are performed only by the qualified supervisor following equipment/gel guidance; learners use the cooled gel.
- F1: pure Brilliant Blue FCF/E133/FD&C Blue No. 1; F2: pure tartrazine/E102/FD&C Yellow No. 5. Each at 0.050% mass/volume in the same buffer with 10% volume/volume glycerol. Example supervisor preparation: 25.0 mg identified dye and 5.00 mL glycerol, filled to 50.00 mL with buffer. Do not use unknown mixed baking colours. Both dye ions are treated as net negative at pH 7.
- Supervisor-supplied coded mixture U and dye-free blank B, both in the same buffer with 10% glycerol; 2–20-µL micropipette and fresh tips; timer, ruler, pH meter and thermometer. No DNA, amino acids, UV illumination or additional detection stains in this actual run.

### Procedure and personal evidence

1. Wear eye protection and a lab coat; do not eat, drink or mouth-pipette. Keep the instrument exterior dry. The supervisor checks enclosed contacts. Switch off and unplug output leads before placing the gel, pipetting or measuring; never open the lid with the output connected.
2. Insert the cooled gel with wells at the black negative side. Follow the manufacturer instructions for 2–6 mm buffer coverage; Table 4 specifies 265–320 mL base buffer volume for the named Mini-Sub Cell GT, depending on gel size/thickness. Record actual fill, coverage, pH and starting temperature. If fill and coverage do not match the chosen original tray/gel according to its instructions, or temperature is outside 20–25°C, do not start and consult the supervisor. The supplied 400 mL covers running liquid, gel preparation and reserve. Sketch wells, polarity and origin line; measure actual electrode spacing.
3. Load 10.0 µL each of F1, F2, U and B into separate documented wells. Use a fresh tip for each solution; do not puncture the gel or overfill. Take a starting photograph or make a personal scaled sketch showing well identities.
4. Close the original lid. The supervisor connects black to negative and red to positive. Set 30 V constant voltage and a 20-mA current limit; record actual displayed voltage/current. Do not increase voltage when current-limited. Maximum energized time is 30 minutes. E = actual voltage/electrode spacing approximates the field; it does not guarantee field uniformity inside the gel.
5. Observe band positions/colours through the closed lid every five energized minutes. At ten and twenty minutes stop, unplug output leads and only then open for a temperature check; record temperature, close unchanged and resume with the same polarity. Exclude interruptions from energized time. Stop immediately at 30°C or above, leakage, damaged protection, unusual gas formation, equipment fault or a band reaching 5 mm from the gel edge; consult the supervisor. Do not force migration by increasing voltage.
6. Stop no later than 30 energized minutes and unplug leads. Only then open and record the final image with a ruler. Determine each visible band's direction, centre-to-origin distance and width from personal observations; enter no target distances. Check B for carryover. Interpret U only against actually observed references; unresolved bands or failed controls leave a unique assignment open.
7. Explain movement of negative ions toward the positive anode and effects of matrix, size, shape, charge and buffer. Similar migration alone establishes neither unique identity nor molecular mass or purity. Predict reversed migration and possible loss from the gel if polarity were swapped for the same starting state; do not perform a second run solely for this transfer question. The separate amino-acid/pI planning task remains theoretical and earns no actual-performance credit from this dye run.
8. Collect dye liquid as labelled aqueous laboratory waste and the gel separately according to local rules; clean the bench. Drain disposal and successful separation are not assumed.
