## Concrete permitted option: enzymatic starch pretreatment

First answer the new-substrate question without the solution: justify whether ordinary baker's yeast uses starch immediately like glucose and independently propose control comparisons. Then use this complete method card as the defined operating framework for personal execution. This is an original adapted teaching design, not a manufacturer-validated fermentation or yield method. Complete saccharification or a specified gas volume is not promised.

### Materials and preparation

Use only fresh commercial baker's yeast, food-grade potato starch and glucose. Each batch contains 10.0 g substrate and 100.0 mL total aqueous liquid before yeast addition; use 1.00 g yeast where indicated. Enzyme: identified liquid NCBE amyloglucosidase (AMG 300L from Aspergillus niger), never saliva, unidentified enzyme powder or environmental cultures. The supervisor checks product identity, shelf life and safety sheet; record lot and declared activity if supplied. This recipe doses the delivered liquid product by volume without inferring conversion from its trade name.

Prepare aqueous 10-mmol/L citrate buffer at pH 4.50 ±0.10: the supervisor dissolves 1.9212 g anhydrous citric acid in about 700 mL water, adjusts to pH 4.50 at 20–25°C using 0.100-M sodium hydroxide with stirring and measured pH, and fills to 1.000 L; recheck pH. Only the supervisor handles acid/base stocks. For the enzyme control, the supervisor heats a separate AMG portion in an open loosely splash-covered tube for ten minutes at 80–85°C after reaching 80°C, cools it and replaces a measured evaporative loss with water to its original mass. This heat-treated enzyme is an inactivation control, not a demonstrated completely enzyme-free preparation.

Provide at least nine 250-mL flasks, stable 60°C and 30°C water baths, thermometers, balance, graduated vessels/pipettes, filters and tared receivers, and one freely moving 100-mL gas-syringe setup per batch, with unobstructed gas path and 1-mL graduations. Wear goggles/coat; no flames or pressure-tight bottles. The supervisor prepares hot starch and handles hot vessels. Never spray enzyme or let spills dry; avoid skin/eye contact and immediately wet-clean spills under school rules. Stop for respiratory symptoms, splashing, blocked gas paths or broken glass and notify the supervisor.

Cool all other batches to 30 ±1°C before adding any yeast; record final pH and remaining mass. Do not selectively readjust pH. No fermentation batch operates at 60 or 80°C.
6. At the end/stop actually filter preparations, recording filtrate volume, losses and appearance. Yeast cells and undigested particles may be retained; dissolved sugars and possible fermentation products pass. No distillation, tasting or food use. Collect biological/enzyme residues under school hygiene/disposal rules and clean equipment.
