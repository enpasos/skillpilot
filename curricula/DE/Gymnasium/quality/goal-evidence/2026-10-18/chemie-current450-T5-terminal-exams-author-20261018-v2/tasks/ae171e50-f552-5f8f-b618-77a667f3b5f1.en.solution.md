## 1. Model wall with insulation and load-bearing layers

Plaster supplies shape, cork can delay heat transport and adhesive transfers forces. Lower outer temperature in this interval is consistent with delay, not a full conductivity measurement. Increased total thickness is a factor; an equal-thickness comparison clarifies material effect. Moisture, fire behaviour, durability and loads remain untested for real use.

Moisture can weaken adhesive and plaster. Defined moisture/adhesion testing and suitable protection are new requirements independent of the thermal trial.

## 2. Filter layers as a functional system

Coarse support, finer separation and retention paper have distinct roles; interfaces must prevent material loss. Greater retention can limit flow;100 mL in90 rather than20 s is a trade-off. Dissolved substances/microbes were not tested. Layer-height differences and repeatability limit inference; record original volumes and retained water.

Conductivity before/after with a water blank tests passage of dissolved ions; mechanical clarity does not demonstrate salt retention.

## 3. Prepare a model cream as an emulsion

Stirring disperses droplets but does not make a permanently homogeneous solution without stabilisation. Amphiphiles interact with both phases and can stabilise droplets. Model cream is not a new pure substance.30-min stability proves neither shelf-life, skin compatibility nor medical effects; the water phase can spoil.

Oil4.0 g, emulsifier0.5 g and water5.5 g total10.0 g; altered interfaces/phase fractions can change stability, requiring matched time/stirring controls.

## 4. Cast a water-free model balm

Wax forms a solid network on cooling; oil changes consistency. This is initially physical preparation, without demonstrated synthesis of new substances. Higher wax fraction may give lower indentation here; control temperature, probe, time and thickness. Usability/compatibility need other evidence; water-free does not mean sterile.

Store both recipes at20 and30°C for equal times and test under equal load. Melting/softening depends on temperature and mixture.

## 5. Leaven dough with a controlled yeast process

Yeast metabolises sugar; CO2 can accumulate in the dough network. Mean A122 mL means52.5% rise; B81.5 mL about1.9%. Rise is not a direct measure of all produced CO2 because gas escapes and kneading changes structure. Inactivated yeast is a control, but heating may change other properties. A separate controlled gas test complements it.

Equal yeast gas amounts with different dough rise suggest changed retention; controlled gas collection and equal kneading separate effects.

## 6. Refine milk with a defined starter culture

Starter bacteria can convert milk sugar into lactic acid; lower pH changes protein interactions and favours coagulation. A pH4.5 strip reading is approximate, not exact acid concentration. Thickening alone identifies neither microbes nor product safety. A no-starter batch can still change on prolonged storage; control interpretation is time-bounded.

Lactose-free may mean enzymatically split milk sugar. Whether the specific starter uses available sugars needs testing with matched protein/temperature conditions.
