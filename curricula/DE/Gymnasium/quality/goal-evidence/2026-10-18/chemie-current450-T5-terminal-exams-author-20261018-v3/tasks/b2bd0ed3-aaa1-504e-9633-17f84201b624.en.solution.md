## 2. Starch granules as a material specimen

Mean A40 µm, B about16.7 µm; small selected samples do not establish distributions or species. Debris, overlap and missing calibration distort measurements. A granule is not an entire cell; this is material analysis. Labels identify outline, observed features and scale, without invented cell structures.

Shape observations may remain valid; quantitative micrometre values need calibration valid at400×. Do not reuse the100× scale unchanged.

## 3. Test gentle-touch responsiveness in Mimosa

A defensible hypothesis predicts a larger prompt increase in closed pairs after the defined contact than after sham, without requiring every plant to respond. All outcomes remain open until original data exist. Comparable baseline, random assignment/order, constant conditions and sham motion limit explanations involving hand/shadow motion, time and care state without removing all variability. Larger touch Δp values with interpretable sham data support stimulus-associated responsiveness of these plants; similar/ambiguous values do not. A seconds-scale change is compatible with rapid movement rather than newly produced growth, but does not demonstrate electrical signals, particular ion channels, pain, consciousness or learning. Video does not measure a cellular cause. Leaflet pairs are not independent replicates; n=3 plants per condition is small. Absent responses, strong sham responses and missing data must be reported rather than removed by repeating until a desired result appears.

Withhold: One defensible comparison randomly assigns within three size pairs slow sliding versus a matched-duration hand approach to a stationary pot; both without leaf contact, under matched light/temperature and with original N/C/U videos before/after. Other equivalently controlled justified designs are acceptable. Different responses may show that handling is also a plausible mechanical stimulus under the tested conditions; no difference does not prove that stronger or different movement never works. The new investigation neither separates cellular signaling pathways nor rules out an additional direct-touch effect. Repeating16 times or dropping a pot is not an acceptable design.
