### Task 1

R is radical chain polymerization: initiator decomposition generates radicals and chain growth consumes C=C;70°C serves the selected initiator and oxygen may inhibit. K is polyester polycondensation: COOH/OH form ester and H2O; chosen heat/catalysis promote conversion, while water removal favours condensation and high conversion. A is polyaddition: NCO/OH form urethane without a small main product; dryness limits water side reaction, heat removal controls exothermicity, and group balance permits chain formation. The initiator supplies reacting radicals; a catalyst offers a more favourable pathway and accelerates equilibrium approach without changing the equilibrium constant at fixed temperature. Moisture in A consumes NCO and can create CO2/bubbles; absent water removal in K changes condensation conditions. Water has different chemical roles. These examples establish no universal temperature or moisture rule for entire classes.

Scope limit: Temperature alone does not classify a polymer synthesis. The three examples establish no universal temperature, moisture condition or catalyst identity for an entire reaction class.
