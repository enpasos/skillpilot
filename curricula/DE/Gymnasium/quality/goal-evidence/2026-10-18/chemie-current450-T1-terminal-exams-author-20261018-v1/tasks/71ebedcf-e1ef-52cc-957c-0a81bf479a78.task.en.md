### Supplementary complete task material

Complete starting cards: case1 Br–(CH₂)₆–Br with 2 OH⁻; intended diol HO–(CH₂)₆–OH for a polyester with a dicarboxylic acid. Transfer CH₃CH₂Br with OH⁻. Case2 Br–(CH₂)₃–Br with 2 OH⁻, explicitly supplied intermediate X=HO–(CH₂)₃–Br. Faulty sketch A: arrow from OH⁻ hydrogen to primary C and C–Br pair to Br. Faulty sketch B: overall equation alone with no local pair arrows, labelled “complete mechanism”. These are simplified written substitution models, not laboratory instructions.

### Task 1 (6 points)

Show both substitutions with structural formulae and two electron-pair arrows for each. Explain the nucleophile, attacked C and leaving group. Write the intermediate, overall equation and charge balance. Justify suitability as a diol monomer. Transfer the mechanism to bromoethane and explain why its alcohol product does not provide the same bifunctional bridge. An overall equation alone is not a mechanism.

Justify the scientific claims and state one specific limit to the inference.

### Task 2 (6 points)

Develop both local electron-pair arrow sequences and the balanced overall equation. Explain X and the remaining step to the complete diol. Judge X’s monomer suitability, correct both sketches and transfer the explanation to the changed chain length from case1. Also evaluate the claim that one OH− equivalent per dihaloalkane proves every molecule is exactly X.

Justify the scientific claims and state one specific limit to the inference.
