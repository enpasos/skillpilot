### Task 1

A carbon atom may move from atmospheric CO2 into dissolved CO2: physical transport preserves the molecular species. Photosynthesis incorporates the carbon into organic material; respiration oxidizes it back to CO2. Both are chemical redox conversions and their overall equations conserve carbon atoms. Light energy is converted into chemical energy; respiration releases energy, part of which ultimately disperses as heat. Substance forms change while the element is conserved. The two overall processes are not identical mechanisms run backward.

Scope limit: Only the explicitly bounded exchange of molecular CO2 is physical here; CO2 reactions with water are additional chemical processes.

### Task 2

Calcination, hydration and carbonation change chemical species; transport and mere dispersion do not. Calcium returns to CaCO3 and the ideal equations conserve all atoms. Calcination nevertheless needs heat and initially releases CO2. Later carbonation may bind CO2, but without amount, time and process data this does not prove complete recapture or climate-neutral energy supply. Even an ideal material balance requires external energy; real material may be lost or leave the chosen system.

Scope limit: A balanced overall equation does not replace an actual energy, emissions or recapture assessment.
