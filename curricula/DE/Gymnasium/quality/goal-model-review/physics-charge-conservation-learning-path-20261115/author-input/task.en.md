**Two independent cases of charge redistribution**

Use a simple metal model: positive atomic cores remain in place; mobile electrons can move between conductively connected bodies. The stands are insulating. There is no conducting connection to Earth. Explain your answers using the charge model. Clearly labelled sketches and charge balances are acceptable.

**Case A – Separating charge without touching either body**

Two initially neutral metal spheres A and B touch. A negatively charged rod is brought close to A without touching either sphere. While the rod stays in place, A and B are separated. Only then is the rod removed. Treat the two spheres together as the system.

A1. Explain the initially neutral state and state the direction in which electrons move between the spheres while the rod is close to A. Explain the direction. (2 points)

A2. State the charge signs on both spheres after separation and removal of the rod. Explain why the separated charges remain. (2 points)

A3. Write the total-charge balance of the two spheres before and after the process; no charge magnitudes are supplied. Use the model to explain why neither the rod nor separating the spheres creates or destroys net electric charge. (2 points)

**Case B – Equalization with negative total charge**

This case starts afresh and is independent of case A. Two identical metal spheres M and N stand far apart on insulating stands. Initially M carries +2 nC and N carries −6 nC. They are connected conductively and disconnected again after charge equalization. External electric disturbances are neglected. In the model used, the identical spheres carry equal charges after equalization. No charge is exchanged with the surroundings. The conducting connection stores no charge of its own in this model; the isolated system for the charge balance consists of M and N.

B1. Calculate the initial total charge and each sphere's charge after equalization. Show the calculation with signs and units. (2 points)

B2. State the direction in which electrons are transferred through the connection. Describe how the excess or deficit of electrons on each sphere changes. (2 points)

B3. Someone claims: “During charge equalization positive and negative charges disappear. Therefore both spheres become neutral.” Check and correct this claim using case B. Compare with case A: what is conserved in both cases despite the different charge distributions? (2 points)
