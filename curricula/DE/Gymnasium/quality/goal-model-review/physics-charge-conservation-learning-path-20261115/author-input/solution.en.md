A1. Neutral means that each sphere has equal positive and negative charge contributions. The negative rod repels mobile electrons. Because A and B touch, electrons move from A to B; the positive atomic cores remain fixed.

A2. After separation A is positive because of its electron deficit, whereas B is negative because of its electron excess. The spheres are separated before the rod is removed and stand on insulating supports, so electrons cannot subsequently flow back between them. The spheres remain oppositely charged.

A3. Initially Q_A = Q_B = 0 and Q_total = 0. Afterwards one may write Q_A = +q and Q_B = −q, with q > 0: Q_total = (+q) + (−q) = 0. The rod does not touch the spheres and transfers no charge to them. Only existing electrons are redistributed between the spheres; separating them breaks the conducting connection without creating or destroying charge. The magnitude of q cannot be determined from the supplied material.

B1. Total charge is (+2 nC) + (−6 nC) = −4 nC. The identical spheres carry equal final charges and the overall system is isolated, so Q_M = Q_N = (−4 nC)/2 = −2 nC. The final balance is (−2 nC) + (−2 nC) = −4 nC.

B2. Electrons are transferred from N to M. N loses part of its electron excess. M gains electrons: its initial deficit is first removed, and finally M also has an electron excess. The charge changes are ΔQ_M = −4 nC and ΔQ_N = +4 nC. These numbers are an optional further balance, not an additional required calculation. A description using conventional current from M to N is correct provided the opposite electron motion is made clear.

B3. The claim is false. No positive or negative charge is destroyed. Existing electrons are redistributed and the total charge of −4 nC is conserved. In the supplied model, equalization leads to equally charged spheres, not necessarily neutral spheres. Both end at −2 nC. Case A produced spatial charge separation at zero total charge; case B equalizes an initially unequal distribution at negative total charge. In both cases the respective total charge of the isolated sphere system remains unchanged.

The six blocks are scored separately according to the scientific content actually expressed. An error receives no repeated additional deduction. If charge conservation is absent or physically contradicted, the corresponding essential block earns 0 points; the other blocks cannot compensate. An isolated arithmetic slip with a correctly formulated balance and a fully consistent redistribution explanation can earn 1 point in exactly the affected block.
