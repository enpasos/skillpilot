content:

This class builds on the “E_M1
- Advanced Solid State Physics” lecture at LMU (or similar class at
- and
develops an introductory-level insight into the main concepts and the rich
phenomenology of graphene and other two-dimensional materials, leading up to
the recent advancements in moiré superlattices.
In particular, this class aims to introduce all the main concepts and
techniques that are needed for the study of the key experimental literature on
the emergent field of moiré materials, with a strong bias towards
low-temperature electronic experiments.

outcomes:

- After completing the Module the student is
able to:

- Develop understanding of modern
experimental techniques in nano-fabrication, nano-characterization, cryogenic
techniques, electronic transport (Drude, Hall, Sommerfeld, Boltzmann).

- Apply tight binding band-structure  calculations of graphene, bilayer graphene,
hBN and TMDs.

- Understand Dirac equation for relativistic
massless electrons and the pseudo-spin texture in graphene.

- Understand basic concepts of
topological systems, quantum Hall effect, Berry’s phase, Chern numbers, Haldane
model, topological insulators.

- Understand concepts in band-structure engineering
with super potentials, moiré patterns, Hofstadter butterfly physics,
flat-bands.

- Understand symmetry-breaking and
phase-transitions to explain cascade of many-body ground states in twisted
bilayer graphene.

- Understand  strong electron-electron interactions, Fermi
liquids, the Hubbard model, superconductivity and strange metallicity in
twisted bilayer graphene.

preconditions:

Quantum mechanics, statistical physics,
solid state physics, at the level of elementary modules from a Bachelor’s
- degree in
- Advanced solid state physics at the Master’s level.
