title_en: Introduction to Graphene and 2D Materials
ects: 6
ects_plan: 5
semester_plan: -

content:

This class builds on the “E_M1
Advanced Solid State Physics” lecture at LMU (or similar class at TUM) and
develops an introductory-level insight into the main concepts and the rich
phenomenology of graphene and other two-dimensional materials, leading up to
the recent advancements in moiré superlattices.
In particular, this class aims to introduce all the main concepts and
techniques that are needed for the study of the key experimental literature on
the emergent field of moiré materials, with a strong bias towards
low-temperature electronic experiments.

outcomes:

- After completing the Module the student is able to:
- 1. Develop understanding of modern experimental techniques in nano-fabrication, nano-characterization, cryogenic techniques, electronic transport (Drude, Hall, Sommerfeld, Boltzmann).
- 2. Apply tight binding band-structure calculations of graphene, bilayer graphene, hBN and TMDs.
- 3. Understand Dirac equation for relativistic massless electrons and the pseudo-spin texture in graphene.
- 4. Understand basic concepts of topological systems, quantum Hall effect, Berry’s phase, Chern numbers, Haldane model, topological insulators.
- 5. Understand concepts in band-structure engineering with super potentials, moiré patterns, Hofstadter butterfly physics, flat-bands.
- 6. Understand symmetry-breaking and phase-transitions to explain cascade of many-body ground states in twisted bilayer graphene.
- 7. Understand strong electron-electron interactions, Fermi liquids, the Hubbard model, superconductivity and strange metallicity in twisted bilayer graphene.

preconditions:

Quantum mechanics, statistical physics,
solid state physics, at the level of elementary modules from a Bachelor’s
degree in physics. Advanced solid state physics at the Master’s level.
