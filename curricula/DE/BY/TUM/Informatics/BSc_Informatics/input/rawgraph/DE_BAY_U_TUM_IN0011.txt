# IN0011 Introduction to Theory of Computation - rawgraph breakdown

Node: Introduction to Theory of Computation (IN0011)
Description: The learner can analyze formal languages, computability, and complexity.

  Node: Regular languages and automata
  Description: The learner can work with regular languages and finite automata.

    Node: Regular expressions and automata
    Description: The learner can convert between regular expressions and DFA/NFA models.

    Node: Pumping lemma and minimization
    Description: The learner can apply the pumping lemma and minimize automata using Myhill-Nerode.

  Node: Context-free languages
  Description: The learner can analyze context-free languages and pushdown automata.

    Node: CFGs and PDAs
    Description: The learner can translate between CFGs and PDAs and analyze their power.

    Node: Normal forms and CYK
    Description: The learner can use Chomsky/Greibach normal forms and the CYK algorithm.

  Node: Context-sensitive languages
  Description: The learner can describe context-sensitive languages and linear bounded automata.

    Node: CSLs and LBAs
    Description: The learner can explain CSLs and the role of LBAs.

    Node: Closure and hierarchy properties
    Description: The learner can reason about closure properties and the Chomsky hierarchy.

  Node: Computability theory
  Description: The learner can analyze decidability and computation models.

    Node: Turing machines and equivalences
    Description: The learner can describe Turing machines and equivalent models (While/Goto, recursive functions).

    Node: Decidability and reductions
    Description: The learner can classify problems by decidability and apply reductions.

  Node: Undecidability results
  Description: The learner can explain classic undecidability theorems.

    Node: Halting problem and Rice theorems
    Description: The learner can explain the halting problem and Rice/Rice-Shapiro theorems.

    Node: PCP and CFG undecidability
    Description: The learner can explain the Post correspondence problem and undecidable CFG problems.

  Node: Complexity theory
  Description: The learner can analyze complexity classes and reductions.

    Node: Time and space complexity classes
    Description: The learner can define classes such as P and NP and their properties.

    Node: NP-completeness and reductions
    Description: The learner can apply polynomial reductions and explain the Cook theorem.
