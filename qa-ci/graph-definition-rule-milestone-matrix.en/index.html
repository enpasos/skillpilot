    Compendium: §§1–5 Core · §§6–10 Context · §§11.1–11.4 Units · §11.5 Progression · §12 Planning · §13 Implicit Mastery · Appendices B–F · Appendix A (Rules & Ampel)

    This appendix is the reference register for all 145 skill-graph rules (GD-*) in this document. For every rule, it answers three questions: what is required, which validation area owns the obligation, and what the existing checks actually prove.

    • How to read a row. Each GD-* row links an obligation to its designated validation area (target), existing checks (actual), and their evidentiary limits: ID (GD-, the document-part letter, and a sequential number: C for the formal core, X for context, U for learning units, P for planning, A for implicit-mastery recognition, and D for the document appendices); Traffic light (technical enforcement level at the stated audited repository state: HARD for rules enforced fail-closed across their entire declared domain, PARTIAL for a materially incomplete intended domain or enforcement, including merely indirect maturity-floor protection, OPEN for binding obligations without an established hard gate on the core claim, and OPT/DOC for optional profiles, undecided policy, or document checks); Obligation (concise statement of the rule); Target (the validation area assigned to the obligation: a milestone M0–M7 or one of the area codes in Section A.2); Actual and gap (what existing checks prove and what remains open); Section (the named section where the rule is defined). Rows use concise telegraphic style and recurring shorthand: Unit for learning unit, Exam or Assessment for an exam or assessment goal, Memory for a memory goal or memorization, Normal and Strict for normal mode and strict mode, Closure for transitive closure, Scope for validation or applicability scope, Runtime for runtime execution, unit-wide lock for holistic unit gating, and human trial for learner evaluation.
    • Milestones and rule families. Milestones M0–M7 structure automated curriculum QA; runtime contracts, concepts, optional profiles, and artifact or document obligations are tracked separately. The two existing rule families are Curriculum Quality Review (CQR) and graph-validation rollout rule (GVR), denoting curriculum quality checks and skill-graph validator rollout rules respectively. The legend in Section A.2 defines every code referenced here along with its actual scope.
    • An assignment is not a pass. Target assignment names the validation area responsible for each obligation; the existing gates listed alongside prove only their actual scope. A binding rule without a complete gate is an open verification obligation, not an already passed check. M0 is a possible incomplete starting state, not a quality seal. Profile-bound route gates apply within their declared QA scopes. By contrast, the global base check for complete atomic normal form and transitive minimality (check code G17) must cover the entire declared canonical subject inventory, including relevant cross-subject prerequisites and inheriting ancestors; a QA profile subset or partial scan is insufficient.
    • Base before every milestone. Complete atomic normal form and a full transitive-minimality check are the required target base before any promotion to M1–M7. A G17 violation or an incomplete check blocks every milestone pass M1–M7 in the affected subject, even though individual source or review checks may still pass on their own. The current atomic target state \(R \subseteq A \times A\) and its transitive minimality are binding. Under the Product Owner decision of 12 November 2026, a particular migration order and equality with an earlier prerequisite closure are no longer acceptance conditions. Complete current validation is independent of historical reduction proofs. CQR-102 and GVR-012 remain directly atomic.
    • Memory goals. Flashcard repetitions belong to the learning process. Completing a memory goal requires its own explicit verification record; subsequent review due dates alone do not revoke that completion. Ordinary completed goals receive neither review due dates nor any mastery revocation derived from them. No new assessment entity or schema is introduced by this contract.
    • Scope of the register. The 145 stable GD-* IDs cover all six parts of the compendium. Section references use named sections rather than volatile line numbers. Runtime contracts, new recognition and learning-unit semantics, optional planning, and publication or document obligations are never assigned a fabricated M7 gate; where the Product Owner fixes a target area, such as M5/M7 for the coverage evidence of learning units (R2), the row states that it is not yet enforced. Subject-matter accuracy, human trials, and learning effectiveness remain independent forms of evidence. Under the Product Owner decision of 13 November 2026, the grading domain of GD-* rows is the 21-root canonical Gymnasium catalog. Milestone-bound obligations apply from their stated threshold (ten M6/M7 subjects, including five M7 subjects; twelve route scopes). Non-canonical landscapes alone do not prevent a hard grade; open runtime or subject-matter sub-obligations within this domain remain. The graph validator loads 548 live files: 21 canonical Gymnasium roots and 527 landscapes outside Gymnasium. The 45 unchanged Bavarian input graphs remain source-only; explicit compatibility access stays separate from the canonical live domain. The source audit documents consumers, boundaries, and negative tests.

    Traffic-Light Status: What Is Truly Hard-Enforced Today

    Many of the 145 GD-* rule groups bundle a technically gated structural core together with broader runtime, migration, or subject-matter obligations. The four-state traffic light therefore makes visible—both per individual validator gate and per rule group—where a technically hard (fail-closed) gate already operates today and where scope limits or open obligations remain. The CQR/GVR hardening from PR #131 is integrated into main; the later mainline state 2129b207f4 passes the complete CI #2315. The scope statements below also reflect the Gymnasium decision of 13 November and the current follow-up work; broader GD obligations retain their own classifications. Historical CI does not automatically verify later changes. The delivery record distinguishes PR verification, integration, and the two remaining expansion targets.
